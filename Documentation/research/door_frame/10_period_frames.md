# Door frames: period sizes vs ours (10)

2026-10-30 · 游戏视觉 line (account B, session local_a9a1dce2), workflow stage "Research + measure".
Red's ask (verbatim): "目前门框稍微有一些太厚了，我需要你调研那个年代门框应该是多大".
Scope: research and measurement only. Nothing built, nothing in main changed except this folder. No Unity job was run. Sources are in `SOURCES.md` (tags F1…F23); wanted media in `media_candidates.md`. [E] marks my inference; [K] marks a number read from our own code or mesh. 1 in = 25.4 mm.

**Verdict.** A c. 1990 US commercial door frame shows **2 in (51 mm)** of steel on the wall, or **2-1/4 in (57 mm)** of wood casing, on a 3-0 × 7-0 door (5.6–6.3 % of the door's width per side). It stands **7/16–1/2 in (11–13 mm)** proud if steel and **11/16 in (17.5 mm)** if wood. Our map doors show **77 mm, 25 mm proud** on every side including the head (7.7 % of the opening). Red is right, and the size is forced: the kit is a sleeve around the map's 70 × 20 mm trims, and the sleeve cannot get below about 73 × 22.5 mm while those trims exist. The title / station pair (50 mm, 14 mm proud) is already at or under period, so it needs no change. The EXIT frame is the map's own trim, 70 × 20 mm.

Next to a 3-0 × 7-0 door: **steel reads as a thin line**, about 1/18 of the door's width, and a 4 in head is the one place it gets heavy. **Wood reads a little wider and softer**, at about 1/16.

### 1.2 Hollow metal, the detail

- **Profile.** SDI 111A-24 gives every standard profile the same dimension string: 2 in face, 5/8 in stop, 1-15/16 in door rabbet, 1-9/16 or 1-15/16 in second rabbet, 1/2 in return typical, "7/16" Return Typical for 5-3/4" Jamb Depth" (F1, read off the drawing page). 16 gauge is typical for interior, 14 for exterior.
- **Jamb depth arithmetic [E].** A 3-5/8 in stud with 5/8 in board each side is a 4-7/8 in wall. A 5-3/4 in jamb depth less two 7/16 in returns is exactly a 4-7/8 in throat. So a period frame stands 11.1 mm proud of each face, and F1's own note fits that wall.
- **One correction to the brief.** The door rabbet is 1-15/16 in, not 1-1/2 in. The 1-9/16 in rabbet is the spare one, for a 1-3/8 in door (F1, F7, F12). Slip-on soffit minimum is 1 in (F1).
- **Knock-down vs welded.** Both existed. The Feb 1991 San Diego spec wants "welded unit type", "not … lighter than 16-gauge", and "trim faces mitered, welded, and finished smooth", with "combination buckled frame and trim" (F2). Ceco's 1996 spec offers three-piece knock-down and welded, and DS/DC slip-on drywall frames in 18 ga standard, 16 optional, "2 inches face dimension", "7/16 inch minimum backbend" (F5). US patents show knock-down drywall frames by 1973 and knock-down steel frames with the casing built into the jamb in 1981–83 (F21, F22).
- **Finish.** Prime-painted and painted on site with the door (F2 primes; our `02_period_hardware.md` has the same-colour putty frame). Dark bronze is a period aluminium finish in F3. A dark-bronze paint on a steel frame is our own choice, not a sourced default [E].

### 1.3 Wood

- Parts: "two sidejambs and a headjamb, together with separate moldings called doorstops"; stock jambs "of nominal 1-inch material" (F13). Nominal 1 in = 3/4 in actual [E]. Retail flat jamb today is 11/16 in (F23).
- Casing: width 2-1/4 to 3-1/2 in, thickness 1/2 to 3/4 in, "11/16 inch … standard for many narrow-line patterns", about 3/16 in from the face of the jamb (F14). Agriculture Handbook 73 (F13) has a similar sentence and a margin on the jamb edge, but its OCR garbles the fractions (the width reads "1%" to 3%""), so its limits may differ and F14 is the reading I rely on.
- Stops: applied, 7/16 in × 3/4 to 2-1/4 in (F14). A double-acting door has none.
- A period spec for hardwood frames exists: "Door frames, chair rails, and hardwood trim shall be [WIC] Premium Grade [select white oak] [red oak] [maple]", transparent or paint finish, Feb 1991 (F4). It gives no sizes.
- **Our 77 mm is a 3 in casing, inside the wood range by width.** Its 25 mm thickness is past the 3/4 in top of that range. So Wood_DA is wide, while Steel_DA is simply wrong by 52 %.

### 1.4 Double-acting doors

No stop is formed in the frame: the frame is a "Cased Open" profile, with "no stops formed in the frame" (F1, F19, F12). It keeps the 2 in face and the return. So a DA door in 1990 does not get a wider frame. What changes is the soffit (flat, no rabbet), the pivot reinforcement, and the gap line, which brush strips cover. A floor-spring pivot sits 1-1/2 in (38 mm) from the jamb (F18), which agrees with our 36 mm `PIVOT_Z`.

### 1.5 What I could not prove

**No source dated ≤ 1993 that I read states "2 in" in so many words.** The Feb 1991 spec leaves profile to shop drawings (F2). The dated evidence for 2 in starts at the 1996 guide specs (F5, F6) and runs to SDI 2024 (F1). I carry 2 in back to 1990 as [E]: the 1996 specs are three years outside the lock, two makers write the same number there, and nothing I read suggests the face changed after 1990. A 1985–93 Sweet's page would close this (`media_candidates.md` row 1). The aluminium numbers are firm, as F3 is dated Feb 1991.

### 2.2 What makes it read too thick, in order

1. **The sleeve around the map's trims. This is the root cause.** The kit must enclose boxes reaching Z −0.07, Y 2.17 and X ±0.10, so `CASING_U` is 0.077 and `CASING_X1` 0.105 by construction (`10_spec` §1.4; the spec already notes a "0.051 face and 0.011 return, used only if the map stops building door trims", §2.3, and the optional skip, §6.5 item 9). All of the +26 mm face and +14 mm proud comes from this.
2. **The head.** It is the same 77 mm, seen against the lit wall, and it joins the jambs into one heavy square. A period head on a single door is 2 in.
3. **The deep reveal.** The 160 mm wall (period 124 mm) plus the 25 mm proud puts the leaf 83 mm behind the frame face (period cased frame about 51 mm [E]). From 3 m the dark lining adds 6–8 px, 16–22 mm of apparent band. Dropping the proud to 11 mm takes the recess to 69 mm, about a sixth less. The wall thickness is the map's and stays.
4. **The dark finish.** At 0.15 against 0.45 the casing, the reveal and the shadow merge into one band. Colour does not change the size, but it sets how much of the size shows. Keep this as a modifier, not the fix.

The ranch back band on Wood_DA is not a cause on its own: it is the outer 17 mm of the casing, and it exists only to reach past the trim's edge.
