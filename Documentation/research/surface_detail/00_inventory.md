## 1. How every surface is drawn today: `FrontRooms/Surface`

256 of the 267 materials under `Resources/Surfaces` use one shader, `Assets/Resources/Rendering/FrontRoomsSurface.shader` (main 7be4e235; clone C c1a74c99 = main + the staged waterline knob, inert by default). The other 11: 6 `FrontRooms/Glass`, 5 others.

| Path (keyword) | Materials | What reaches the light |
|---|---|---|
| plain (no keyword: world-planar UVs in metres) | walls, floors, ceilings of every family | `_BaseMap` x tint; `_MaskMap` R x `_Smoothness` = smoothness, G = cavity (to `occlusion`, i.e. indirect light and SSAO only); `_BumpMap` tangent normal x `_BumpScale` |
| `_FR_MESH_UV` (192) | doors, frames, props, trims, kit pieces | the same, on the mesh's own UVs in metres |
| `_FR_PRINT` (5) | Level 0 / Shift / Exit wallpaper | the C+2 hung-paper sandwich: paper modulation, emboss in normal and tone, seam (lap, mismatch, dirt), per-strip gloss, the 6 x 3 m substrate normal (RNM blend) in normal and tone, wear that dulls the sheen |
| `_FR_TILE` (26) | `Pool/Prop_PoolTile_*` (landed 11-04 12:52) | data maps (tone, glaze thickness, grout), per-tile shade jitter, odd lots, a per-tile set of +-0.18 deg, joint relief in the tone, crazing and chips by `_TileWear` |
| `_FR_DAMAGE` (12), `_FR_SHEEN` (3), `_FR_OWN_LIGHTS` (11), `_FR_LAMP_FIELD` (1), `_EMISSION` (22) | door break stages, creature cloth, stairwell, exit signs, lenses | their own feature; the surface terms as the plain path |

On top of every path: the **macro layer** (`MacroWear_M`, 1024², sampled at 8 m and 12.8 m in world space): discolouration and dirt, water-stain rings, damp patches, grime along the floor, streaks under the ceiling. **All of it multiplies the albedo only**, except damp (gloss up) and, on the paper, a dulling of the sheen. The wet band (`_WaterY`, pool only) is the one other term that touches smoothness and normal.

What the shader does not have (checked in the source, lines 194-1291):
- no detail normal or detail mask (one normal map per material; the paper's substrate is the only second normal, `_FR_PRINT` only);
- no roughness variation beyond the mask's own R (no macro roughness, no wear-driven roughness outside the paper);
- no specular anti-aliasing and no normal-map filtering (no Toksvig or variance term, no geometric specular AA);
- no height map, parallax or self-shadowing of normal-mapped relief: `cavity` reaches only the indirect light (URP multiplies the probe's diffuse and specular by it, `GlobalIllumination.hlsl` line 566) and the SSAO minimum, never a lamp ("Lamps never see a groove", wall_material §5);
- no view-dependent specular occlusion and no bent normals (the cavity's flat multiply above is all);
- no decal receiving (no DBuffer), no stochastic or hex tiling, no per-instance or per-tile variation outside `_FR_TILE` and the paper's strips;
- the DepthNormals pass writes the geometric normal only (URP's `DepthNormalsPass.hlsl` without `_NORMALMAP`), so SSAO (Source = Depth Normals, intensity 1.6, radius 0.45, direct-light strength 0.35) never sees normal-mapped relief.

**61 of the 256 materials carry no map at all** (flat colour, one smoothness): among them `Cove_Base` (every room's cove), `Prop_CeramicGlaze`, `Prop_Chrome`, `Prop_LaminateBeige`, `Plenum_Dark`, 11 `RunB_*` (cove, chairs, bumpers), 11 `Stair_*` (frames, steel, emt). 84 have no normal map, 86 no mask. Normal maps: 82 at 1024², 56 at 2048², the rest trims.

Reading the table:
- **Smoothness is constant almost everywhere.** Standard deviations of 0.001-0.02 on walls, carpets, block and laminate (the VCT and the ceiling grid are the only exceptions). Under a lamp or a probe the specular is the same over the whole wall: nothing in the sheen says "this was touched, wiped, wet, worn or laid by hand".
- **Normal relief dies with distance.** Office wall 3.9 deg at mip 0, 0.35 deg at mip 5; Run wall 2.4 -> 0.02; carpets lose most by mip 3-4. The relief is 1-6 mm scale detail, so past 2-3 m every family shades as a plane, and nothing replaces it (no gloss from normal variance, no macro relief, no tone).
- **The tile is two different problems.** Scene 4's LS set: flat faces, constant gloss, pure white, mechanically regular joints, no wear. The landed set: real per-tile set and lot jitter, orange peel and swell in the normal, but a glaze gloss of 0.935 +- 0.006 that never varies and turns mirror-like with distance, and joints that are still perfectly straight.
- **Architectural edges are knife-sharp.** The map builds walls, columns, arches, door reveals, steps and the pool's edges from boxes (`FrontRoomsMapWorld`, `FrontRoomsMapWorld.Pool.cs`, `Structures/*Kit*`): no bevel, chamfer or bullnose anywhere in that code. The props are better: `Tools/Blender/frontrooms_kit/kitlib.py` bevels every box 4 mm (2 segments), cylinders 3 mm.

## 3. What "ray tracing" is in this game, and what Red sees in scene 4

| Path | Where | What it traces or reflects | What a surface gets from it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GlassRT** (`FrontRoomsMetalGlassRTRendererFeature`, `GlassRT/*`, `NativePlugin/FrontRoomsGlassRT.metal` 563733bc) | macOS desktop only, Off / High / Ultra; TLAS cap 2,048 (High) / 4,096 (Ultra) instances | one mirror ray per pixel of a **receiver**: only `FrontRooms/Glass` panes with `_RTReceive` 1 | Walls, tile, floors are never receivers. They appear **inside** glass reflections, hit-shaded by a port of the Surface shader (base x albedo map, mask smoothness, the normal map, macro tone and dirt, wet; GGX). Its material table (`FrontRoomsGlassRTSystem.cs` 43b4a8c4, lines 947-997) knows `_FR_MESH_UV`, `_FR_PRINT`, `_EMISSION`, `_FR_LAMP_FIELD` but **not `_FR_TILE`**: a pool tile seen in RT glass would shade its data map as colour (green-yellow with bright joints). Also missing there: the paper's seams, strips, emboss and substrate (it shades the paper and print keyframe only), the wet band, the waterline, damage and sheen. Its texture fetches take their mip from the ray cone's footprint (`FRMakeFootprint`), so gloss written into the mips (`20` K1) reaches RT reflections at no extra cost. |
| **Water** (pool_water, landed 13:04) | desktop High / Ultra: planar reflection (half resolution on Ultra) + refraction; phones, WebGL, Medium / Low: probe + lamps | the room above the water | Tile and walls are mirrored by the planar pass with their full shading; their edges and joints therefore show twice, once mirrored |
| **Reflection probes** | every tier | one baked cube per zone (`FrontRoomsZoneReflection`); URP probe blending and box projection both off (`FrontRooms_URP.asset` 9c3c038b); the pool family's own cubes (70 §7.6); scene 4 re-renders the halls' realtime probes on every water change (`FrontRoomsStructuresWater` d0475f65) | the glossy reflection on every surface: one cube, no parallax, sharp at the glaze's 0.85-0.935 smoothness |
| **Lamps** | every tier | analytic GGX highlights of the punctual lights (troffers, wall boxes, niches) | the "light breaking on the tile" Red describes is these highlights and the probe's image, on a constant smoothness |
| **SSAO** | desktop (Medium samples since 13:23), phones per tier | depth + geometric normals | contact darkening at geometric corners only |

**In scene 4 at RT High**, J cycles GlassRT, and the scene says so itself (`FrontRoomsStructuresWater.cs`): "只有玻璃会变；水不接收光追反射，也不在光追场景里". Scene 4's pool route has no RT receivers on screen (the gallery's "observation windows" are glow panels; `handoff/visual.md` §3 item 3b(b): GlassRT traces 1.2-4.3 ms there with 0 glass pixels). So what Red reads as "光线追踪打在上面" on the tile is the **lamp highlights, the zone or realtime probe's image and the planar water mirror** on a perfectly flat, constant-gloss tile with knife-sharp edges: the reflections are clean because the surface is, not because of a tracer. That is good news for the fix: it is a surface problem first, and it holds on every tier, phones included.

### 5.1 The pool tile in scene 4 (what Red walks)

- **Too flat ("面过于平整").** On the gallery wall (P stop 8) a lamp's reflection is one soft round blob about 0.9 m across that runs over five or six tiles and their joints without a break: the wall shades as one glossy plane. Real tile (and POOLS) breaks that reflection into tile-sized patches (10 §11).
- **Too neat ("细节过于整齐").** Joints are dark ruled lines of one width, straight over 20 m; every tile face has the same tone; the aqua band is a flat stripe.
- **Edges too crisp ("边缘过于清晰").** Pillars, beams, wall corners and the step edges are knife-edged boxes: the tone jumps across a hairline at every corner, with no highlight line and no shade line (hall, P stop 9).
- **"Ray tracing".** GlassRT High and Ultra give the same frame as Off (0 glass pixels; `bathroom_rooms/sheets/water_rt.jpg`). The clean reflections Red reads as RT are the lamps' GGX highlights and the probe on a constant 0.85 gloss, and on desktop the planar water mirror.
- **The landed tile (pool look, S8 and S10 at 1:1)** is better (grout, a waterline course, lots, a wet band) but under P1-A's indirect light its field is still an even white grid with even joints: no lamp image to break up (level_space's finding: realtime probes are off on phones and P1-A puts no bright lamp in view), and the course reads as a stripe of identical tiles.
- **The booth: scene 4's tile (A) beside the landed tile (B), same room, same lamps** (`today_v7`, rows A and B of `sd_today_tile.jpg`; 1:1 crops looked at in the frames):
  - *Hero 0.35 m (H).* A: a lamp's image is one smooth soft disc on a perfectly flat face, and every joint has the same bevel highlight on one edge. B: the image is a crinkled, foil-like patch inside one tile: the orange peel and swell at 0.935 gloss give a high-frequency sparkle, not the slow waviness of real glaze; the per-tile shade steps hardly show.
  - *3 m square on (W3) and grazing (G).* A: the lamp images are soft blobs that the joints cut with a square edge (one glossy plane with a grid printed on it). B: pinpoint glints that stay small and sharp at distance (mirror-like, as 10 §5 predicts for a bumpy glaze whose relief mips away). In both the field is one tone and the joints are ruled; neither breaks a reflection tile by tile.
