## 0. Short answer

1. **Share texture space instead of adding unique textures.** Pros build tiling materials plus trim sheets with a fixed layout and baked bevel normals (Insomniac, Sunset Overdrive [C1]), and grid-snapped kits that "add up to far more than the sum of its parts" (Skyrim: 7 kits, 400+ unique cells, 2 kit artists [C2, C3]). FrontRooms already does this once: the troffer kit (one trim sheet, a lens atlas, a mesh-decal stain, phones 0 extra draws). The rest does not: 255 prop models average 3.29 material slots each, and every slot is its own draw in every pass.
2. **Readable wear is authored, not computed.** DOOM (2016) applies decals in the same clustered forward loop as its lights [C6]. DOOM Eternal adds "geometry decals" authored inside the mesh, built to cost almost no extra frame time [C7]. FrontRoomsSurface draws tide marks, floor grime and ceiling streaks from smoothstep bands of one macro map. Red's bar (procedural shader-drawn cracks read fake) points at authored decals for the readable marks. The macro map can stay for broad tone variation.
3. **The decal trap is real here.** URP DBuffer decals reach only shaders that declare `_DBUFFER_MRT1/2/3` and call `ApplyDecalToSurfaceData` [C12]. FrontRoomsSurface (219 of 230 materials) has neither, so DBuffer decals would not show on walls, floors or ceilings today. URP 17.6 "Automatic" picks DBuffer on desktop and Screen Space on mobile shader APIs and on WebGL [C12]. That matches the tier rules with one renderer feature. Screen Space needs no keywords in the receiving shader.
4. **On Apple GPUs, only opaque draws get hidden surface removal for free.** Alpha test, discard and depth writes from the shader weaken it, and so does blending; Apple's order is opaque, then alpha test/discard, then translucent [C23, C24]. The opaque world here is already clean (FrontRoomsSurface has no `clip`). The waste sits in the transparent layer: 60 additive beams cost 8-18 SetPass for a 0.02-0.26 % image change [C38].
5. **MaterialPropertyBlocks are a hidden draw cost.** An MPB makes a renderer incompatible with the SRP Batcher [C13]. The maze has about 935 in-frustum renderers with an MPB, the corridor 47-55 [C38]. Unity 6.3+ has the Renderer Shader User Value (RSUV): one 32-bit value per renderer, read in the shader, that keeps SRP batches [C14]. URP 17.6 in this project already declares `unity_RendererUserValue` in the per-draw buffer [C12], and the 6000.6.3f1 player carries `SetShaderUserValue` [C39]. Moving lamp levels from MPBs to RSUV is invisible to the player.
6. **Close-up quality comes cheaper from detail maps than from bigger base maps** [C15]. At the game's 76° vertical FOV the carpet and wallpaper maps already hold more texels than 1080p pixels at normal distances (ESTIMATE in §1); the gain is at under 0.4 m and at 1440p/4K, and on handheld a detail normal lets base maps shrink.
7. **The NPOT wallpaper problem is already fixed in the runtime set** (the 2048x3072 maps now sit in an Editor-only folder). Re-measure texture memory before quoting 335 MB again. The larger NPOT and duplication cost is now `Resources/WorldText` (997 localized PNG, 87 MB on disk, all shipped).
8. **Measure with the tools the project's Mac has.** Xcode's Shader Cost Graph, per-line shader cost and Overdraw heat map need an M3 or A17 Pro or later [C26]; the project's measurements run on an M3 Max [C38]. URP's Rendering Debugger overdraw view needs shaders that support SRP debug views [C20, C21].

## 1. Trim sheets and atlases

**What pros do.** Insomniac's environment team for Sunset Overdrive named its method "The Ultimate Trim": a fixed trim layout and normal-map structure that, in the talk's own words, addressed "production speed, memory usage and performance", plus a UV-mapping script and "a special shader for the unique texture style and added variation" [C1]. Secondary write-ups describe the layout as horizontal strips of rising height with 45-degree bevels baked along every strip edge, so one asset can swap materials without new UVs (pointer only; the Vault page gives no numbers and the slides were not readable here) [C1]. Bethesda's kits are the same idea at room scale (section 2) [C2]. Unity's own mobile guidance says "Placing multiple textures into a single texture can reduce draw calls and speed up rendering" [C22].

**Why it works.**
- Draws: one material per kit means one SRP Batcher state for every piece that uses it; Unity notes that features built as shader variants "require more SRP Batcher batches" [C15]. Pieces that share a material can also be merged into one mesh per room, which removes draws outright (inference).
- Memory: detail is paid once per sheet, not once per asset. A trim sheet at 2048x1024 covers every casing, base and grid profile of a kit (inference from [C1]).
- Look: the baked bevel normal makes a low-poly edge catch light like a real eased edge, so the silhouette can stay simple while the shading reads as machined or milled (inference from [C1]).
- Atlases for flat-colour parts: a small swatch atlas (flat colour, smoothness and metal per swatch) lets a prop with several flat materials draw once. The troffer kit already does this ("the frame sits in emission-free swatches of the lens atlas", `Scripts/Rendering/FrontRoomsTrofferKit.cs` header).

**Texel density.** No source gives an industry-wide number; what studios fix is consistency, decided early. One shipped example, Kerbal Space Program, set 512x512 per 8x8 m on terrain and 64 px/m on buildings [C36]. Unity's Rendering Debugger has a "Mip Ratio" view that colours "the pixel density of the highest-resolution mipmap levels Unity uploads" [C20]; it sits under Mipmap Streaming, which this project does not use, so whether it works here is UNCONFIRMED FOR 6000.6.

FrontRooms numbers (ESTIMATE; screen density = H / (2 d tan(FOV/2)) with the game camera's vertical FOV 76° from `Scripts/FrontRooms3DGame.cs:515`, tan 38° = 0.781):
- Screen needs 691/d px/m at 1080p, 921/d at 1440p, 1,382/d at 2160p (d in metres).
- Base maps today: L0 carpet 2048 px over 1.0 m = 2,048 px/m; office carpet tile 2048 over 1.2 m = 1,707 px/m; ceilings 2048 over 1.2 m = 1,707 px/m; L0 wallpaper 2048 over 0.75 x 1.125 m = 2,731 x 1,820 px/m; office drywall 1024 over 1.2 m = 853 px/m; door frame 1024x2048 over 0.5 x 2 m = 2,048 x 1,024 px/m.
- So at 1080p the carpet seen from eye height (d ≥ 1.6 m, 432 px/m needed) has about 4x more texels than pixels; the wallpaper runs out of texels only closer than about 0.38 m (691/1,820), and at 4K closer than about 0.76 m. The drywall runs out at 0.81 m on 1080p.
- Outlier: `Prop_KeyTagNo_A/S` are 4096² for a key tag (`Resources/Surfaces/Textures`): 42.7 MiB for the two maps block-compressed at 1 byte per pixel with mips, 170.7 MiB if uncompressed (ESTIMATE: 4096² x 1 B or 4 B x 4/3 x 2 maps); whether it is a print atlas for many tags was not checked.

**Shared material counts.** Bethesda reused a small number of kits very widely (the Cave kit "used 200 times", Nordic 150) [C3]. FrontRooms' prop library already shares 129 materials across 255 models; the cost is not the number of materials but the 3.29 slots per model (section 6), because each slot is a submesh and a draw in the forward, shadow and depth passes (inference from the URP pass list in `FrontRoomsSurface.shader`).

**Unity 6 / URP 17 way.** Trim and swatch atlases need no engine feature: one `FrontRooms/Surface` material with `_FR_MESH_UV` (mesh UVs in metres) per kit, UVs laid on the sheet, and `Mesh.CombineMeshes` per room for static pieces that share it. Limits per platform: none in the technique; block compression needs a multiple-of-4 size on BC/ETC (all sheets are POT anyway); mip bleeding between strips needs padding (UNSOURCED, common practice).

## 2. Modular kits for interiors (and an office of about 1990)

**What pros do.** Bethesda's Skyrim kits: footprints must be multiples of each other ("256X256x256 can match 512x512x512", "256x256x256 cannot match 384x384x384"), designers snap to half the footprint, kits tile on one or two axes, never all three; 128 units are about 6 feet or 2 m [C2, C3]. Scale of reuse: 7 kits, 8 level designers, 2 kit artists, 400+ unique cells, 30 months; the Cave sub-kit used about 200 times, Nordic 150, Ratway twice [C3]. That is about 57 unique cells per kit (ESTIMATE: 400 / 7). Variation without new geometry came from more granular clutter (players notice repeated props before repeated architecture), kit-bashing two kits (Dwarven halls with ice caves), a few unique assets per dungeon, free-placed pieces over grid-snapped shells, and lighting and ambient occlusion that hide awkward joins [C2]. Fallout 4 used the same modular-kit plus iterative-layout process so that a relatively small content team could build a very large world [C4] (Vault description only; no numbers read).

**Why it works.** One change to a kit piece updates every placement in the game [C2]; memory stays flat because the same meshes and textures are instanced everywhere; designers build fast on a fixed grid [C2].

**How a streamed corridor stays varied without new geometry** (inference, built from [C2, C3, C7] and the project's own stream):
- Dressing sets: a seeded choice of prop arrangements per recycled room (Skyrim's "clutter first" lesson [C2]).
- Material variants: the macro wear map is already sampled in world space at 8 m and 12.8 m (`FrontRoomsSurface.shader` header), so tone shifts along the corridor at no cost; a per-room offset or a second macro map adds more.
- Decal sets: a per-room stain and scuff layout (section 3) is the cheapest readable variation; one atlas, instanced draws [C10].
- Light states: dead, flickering and dimmed tubes are already a project feature; they change the read of a room more than any texture (Red's lighting bar).

**An office of about 1990, as kit metrics** (real-world references; inference for the kit):
- Ceiling: 2 x 4 ft lay-in panels (24 x 48 in, 0.61 x 1.22 m) on a 15/16 in exposed tee grid [C34]. The room stream uses a 0.6 x 1.2 m grid and a 2.9 m ceiling (`FrontRoomsRoomStream.cs:17`). Kit base module: 0.6 m; sub-kit footprints 1.2 / 2.4 / 4.8 m; snap 0.3 m (Burgess' half-footprint rule [C2]). The stream room is 11.5 m wide, not a multiple of 0.6 m, so its perimeter tiles are cut, which is how real offices look; new room families can still keep their kit pieces on the 0.6 m grid.
- Walls: commercial Type II vinyl wallcovering comes 54 in (1.37 m) wide [C35], so strip seams every 1.37 m are the period-correct rhythm (the project's hung-paper seam logic lives in `_SheetParams`).
- Carpet: carpet tiles are commonly 24 in or 50 cm square (UNSOURCED); the office carpet tile material repeats at 1.2 m.
- Base and casings: offices of the period commonly used a 4 in (about 10 cm) vinyl cove base (UNSOURCED, check a manufacturer sheet); the stream's baseboard box is 0.16 m tall and 0.08 m deep (`FrontRoomsRoomStream.cs:1167`), a wood-base proportion. Door casings, cove base, T-bar, chair rail, window stops and thresholds are all extruded profiles: one trim sheet covers them (section 7, CA3).

**Unity 6 / URP 17 way.** No engine feature needed; the kit is meshes plus one trim material. If a kit is placed at runtime (the stream builds rooms in code), merge per-room static pieces that share a material (`Mesh.CombineMeshes`) after placement; Unity's baked occlusion does not help runtime geometry (brief fact).

## 3. Decals and detail maps instead of geometry

**What pros do.**
- DOOM (2016), idTech 6: the SIGGRAPH 2016 talk "The devil is in the details" by Tiago Sousa and Jean Geffroy [C5]. The slide text shows decals resolved per cluster cell in the forward lighting function ("for each decal in cell", before the loop over lights), sampling a single decal atlas with explicit gradients (`tex2Dgrad(decalsAtlas, uv * scaleBias.xy + scaleBias.zw, uvDDX, uvDDY)`) [C6]. Atlas size, format and decal counts were not readable in the slide text (the PDF is over the fetch limit).
- DOOM Eternal: "geometry decals" are small decals that artists define as part of the authored mesh; the constraints were almost no additional frame budget and a forward renderer that cannot blend on a G-buffer; each decal stores a world-to-texture projection, decal indices go into an R8 buffer after the depth pass; limits include 254 projections per submesh and 1M decals per level [C7] (search snippet of the slides).
- Naughty Dog, Uncharted 4: the technical-art talk lists shader-side features such as moss and wetness shading, BRDF micro-shadowing and mip fog [C8] (abstract only). How Uncharted 4 used detail maps and vertex-painted blend layers was not found in a readable primary source (the slide PDF is over the fetch limit): UNSOURCED here.

**Why it works.** A decal adds a specific event (a leak, a scuff, a sticker, a bolt) on top of a shared tiling material, so the base texture stays shared and the unique detail costs a small atlas instead of a unique texture per wall (inference from [C6, C7]). A mesh decal (geometry decal) is drawn only where its few triangles are, so it costs vertices and a little fill; a projected decal costs a volume draw plus a per-pixel test, or a buffer pass (DBuffer) (inference; Unity costs below).

**Mesh decals vs projected decals.**
- Mesh decal: a quad or strip placed on the surface with a decal or multiply material. Unity documents it: "Create a Quad GameObject. Assign a Decal Material ... adjust the mesh bias value to prevent z-fighting" [C11]. The troffer "Soil" stain is already a mesh decal (URP Unlit multiply, transparent, culled past 12 m).
- Projected decal (URP Decal Projector): needs a material with `Shader Graphs/Decal` [C10]; "does not work on transparent surfaces" [C10]; decals "do not support the SRP Batcher by design because they use Material property blocks", so many decals of one material need "Enable GPU Instancing", and an atlas with UV offsets lets "any instance of the four decals" render "in a single instanced draw call" [C10].

**URP 17.6 Decal Renderer Feature (Unity 6.6 manual [C9], package source [C12]).**
- Technique **Automatic**: in URP 17.6 code, Automatic means DBuffer unless the target is WebGL or a mobile shader API (`IsAutomaticDBuffer()` in `DecalRendererFeature.cs`: "As WebGL uses gles here we should not use DBuffer") [C12]. So Mac and Windows get DBuffer, iPhone/iPad and WebGL get Screen Space.
- **DBuffer**: decals render into the DBuffer, which is overlaid "on top of the opaque objects during the opaque rendering"; Surface Data Albedo / Albedo Normal / Albedo Normal MAOS; "does not support the OpenGL and OpenGL ES API"; "requires the DepthNormal prepass, which makes the technique less efficient on GPUs that implement tile-based rendering"; "does not work on particles and terrain details" [C9]. In URP 17.6 the three DBuffer targets are RGBA8 (the first sRGB in Linear), no MSAA [C12]. ESTIMATE memory with MAOS: 3 x 1920 x 1080 x 4 B = 24.9 MB at 1080p, 44.2 MB at 1440p, 99.5 MB at 2160p, plus a non-MSAA depth target when MSAA is on (`dBufferDepth`, size UNCONFIRMED).
- **Screen Space**: decals render after opaques "as meshes on top of the opaque meshes", normals reconstructed from depth (Normal Blend Low/Medium/High = 1/3/5 depth samples); "recommended for mobile platforms that use tile-based rendering, because URP doesn't create a DepthNormal prepass unless you enable Use Rendering Layers" [C9]. It does not need receiver keywords (inference from the pass placement in [C9, C12]).
- **Max Draw Distance** (default 1000 m in code [C12]); Adaptive Performance can lower it [C9]. The project has Adaptive Performance on (brief fact).
- **WebGL**: the manual names only OpenGL and OpenGL ES; WebGL2 is a GLES3-class API and URP's own code excludes it from Automatic DBuffer [C12].
- **Forward+**: the manual says nothing specific; in URP 17.6 code the technique choice branches only on deferred vs not deferred, so Forward+ (this project) behaves as forward: DBuffer or Screen Space (inference from [C12]). DBuffer also needs 4 or more render targets (`SystemInfo.supportedRenderTargetCount >= 4` check) [C12].
- **Per platform for FrontRooms** (inference from [C9, C12]): Metal on Mac, DBuffer (the DepthNormals prepass is already there for SSAO; MSAA 4x adds a resolved depth for the DBuffer pass). Metal on iPhone/iPad, Screen Space (SHADER_API_MOBILE), the documented choice for tile-based GPUs. D3D12/Vulkan on Windows, DBuffer. WebGL2, Screen Space only.

**The trap: a custom shader without DBuffer keywords does not receive DBuffer decals.** In URP 17.6, `DBuffer.hlsl` defines `_DBUFFER` only `if (defined(_DBUFFER_MRT1) || defined(_DBUFFER_MRT2) || defined(_DBUFFER_MRT3)) && !defined(_SURFACE_TYPE_TRANSPARENT)`, and URP Lit applies decals with `#if defined(_DBUFFER) ApplyDecalToSurfaceData(input.positionCS, surfaceData, inputData);` after building its input data [C12]. URP Lit's forward pass declares `#pragma multi_compile_fragment _ _DBUFFER_MRT1 _DBUFFER_MRT2 _DBUFFER_MRT3` [C12]. FrontRoomsSurface declares none of this (grep, section 6). What a custom shader needs (from [C12]): the pragma line in ForwardLit, `#include ".../ShaderLibrary/DBuffer.hlsl"`, and one `ApplyDecalToSurfaceData(input.positionCS, s, inputData)` call after `SurfaceData s` and `InputData inputData` are filled and before `UniversalFragmentPBR`. The project's prefilter flags (`m_PrefilterDBufferMRT1/2/3: 1`) mean those variants are stripped while no Decal feature exists, so the shader change costs nothing until the feature is added (inference from the asset fields). The earlier quality audit already flagged the missing keywords (`Documentation/research/interaction_audit/03_rendering_quality.md`).

**DepthNormals prepass: already there on desktop?** The SSAO feature uses Source = Depth Normals (`FrontRooms_URP_Renderer.asset`, `Source: 1`), which is a DepthNormals prepass; the Mac-only Glass RT feature also asks for a depth prepass (sibling draft `14_post_upscale_tiers.md` §1.3). MEASURED.md found SSAO off removes only 4 draws, consistent with a prepass that stays for another reason [C38]. Inference: on desktop the DBuffer technique adds the DBuffer pass and the decal draws, not a new prepass. Must be confirmed in the Frame Debugger before landing (section 7, CA1).

**Detail maps.** URP Lit has Detail Inputs: a mask (alpha channel), a second "more detailed" base map and normal map with their own tiling; they need shader model 2.5+ [C15]. The Unity way for FrontRoomsSurface is a hand-written equivalent: one extra normal sample at a high world-space frequency, blended with `BlendNormalRNM` (already used for the wallpaper substrate), faded out with distance so far pixels do not pay for or alias on it (inference). Where it pays (ESTIMATE from §1): wallpaper closer than about 0.4 m at 1080p and 0.8 m at 4K; drywall closer than 0.8 m at 1080p; carpet only when crouching or on 4K.

## 4. Shader complexity and overdraw

**Tile-based Apple GPUs vs desktop immediate-mode GPUs.**
- Apple GPUs use tile-based deferred rendering: "the GPU ... discard[s] any occluded (or concealed) primitives" per tile and shades "only the visible primitives", while an immediate-mode GPU "fully processes primitives ... regardless of whether or not they're visible" [C23]. A11 and later "improve fragment discard performance" [C23].
- WWDC20 "Harness Apple GPUs with Metal": "HSR is both pixel perfect and submission order independent"; overdraw is "the number of fragment shader invocations per pixel"; with non-opaque geometry "overdraw will depend on the HSR efficiency", so draw "First, opaque. Second, alpha test, discard or depth feedback. And third, and finally, translucent", and "if we discard or update the depth, we will need to go back to HSR" [C24].
- PowerVR (same TBDR family): "alpha-tested primitives cannot write data to the depth buffer until the fragment shader has executed", which stalls later primitives; order opaque, alpha-tested, blended [C30].
- Arm Mali: "Early ZS testing is much less expensive than late ZS testing"; many quads killed by Forward Pixel Kill means back-to-front order; "High levels of overdraw can reduce performance, even if the cost per fragment is low"; primitives "culled by sample test" (too small to hit a sample) "should be kept as close to 0% as possible"; dense geometry costs both vertex work and fragment efficiency [C27]. Hidden surface removal needs opaque occluders, so discard or alpha-to-coverage moves work to late ZS [C28].
- Qualcomm Adreno: early Z rejects at up to 4x fill rate; writing depth or using discard disables it; discard runs the shader anyway when only some pixels in a thread are killed; run discard shaders after opaque draws; "Use strict half-precision types as much as possible" [C29].
- Desktop discrete GPUs (AMD RDNA): "Avoid `discard` in long running shaders when there are other paths" and use depth-equal after a Z prepass instead of discard [C31]. On desktop the main costs are pixel shader length and fill; there is no HSR, so a depth prepass or front-to-back order does that job (inference from [C23, C31]).

**Per platform for FrontRooms** (inference from the sources above): Metal on Apple silicon Macs and on iPhone/iPad is the same TBDR family [C23], so the opaque world gets HSR and the transparent and alpha-tested layers are what cost. D3D12/Vulkan on Windows (immediate-mode GPUs) have no HSR: opaque overdraw is removed only by early-Z with good order or a depth prepass, and URP depth priming is not available with MSAA [C16, C31]. WebGL2 runs on whatever GPU the browser has, but its limit is the CPU cost per draw (about 40 us per draw, brief fact), so the draw-count rows (CA3-CA6, CA8) matter most there.

**What costs, in plain terms.**
- Alpha blend (glass, glow cards, beams, particles such as the door-break dust and haze): every layer is shaded and blended; no layer hides another. Additive "volumetric" meshes with Cull Off draw both faces (inference from the shader state). The project's `FrontRoomsDoorHaze.shader` already collapses near-lens wisps in the vertex stage so they do not cost "a screenful of discarded fragments" (its own header), which is the pro fix for particles near the camera.
- Alpha test / discard (cut-out stencils, grit, UI): breaks early-Z/HSR for that draw on tilers [C24, C30]; on Adreno it disables early Z [C29].
- Full-screen passes: each one reads and writes the whole target; on tilers each also breaks the tile pass unless merged (sibling draft 14 covers SSAO and pass merging).
- Many small triangles: GPUs shade 2x2 quads, so triangles a few pixels wide waste lanes; Arm calls these microtriangles and partial warps [C27]; Unreal's "Quad Overdraw" view colours this [C32]. LODs are the fix [C27].
- Shader length: texture reads take "a couple hundred cycles" on average and the hardware needs "at least two texture reads at a time" to hide latency; dependent reads serialize [C25].

**How pros measure.**
- Xcode on M3/A17 Pro and later: Shader Cost Graph (passes and pipelines sorted by cost, flame graph of functions), per-line cost pie charts in the source gutter, and Performance Heat Maps including Shader Execution Cost, Instruction Count and "Overdraw: pixels rendered by more than one thread" [C26].
- Unity URP Rendering Debugger: an Overdraw view "to check where Unity draws pixels over one other" (Unity 6.3) [C20]. Unity's issue tracker closed a wrong-count report "By Design" because the old Built-in Unlit shader "does not support SRP debug view" [C21], so FrontRooms' hand-written shaders must be checked for this before trusting the view (inference).
- Unreal: Shader Complexity and Quad Overdraw view modes [C32]; the GDC 2019 tech-art bootcamp talk "Gotta Go Fast: Shader Optimization for Tech Artists" surveys profiling for tech artists [C33] (description only).

**The fixes and their Unity 6 forms.**
- Replace cut-outs with geometry or decals: stencil lettering is paint, so it is a decal (albedo only), not an alpha-tested quad (inference). The two alpha-clipped URP Lit materials in the project are stencils (`Prop_StencilBlack` on `Kit_Crate.fbx`, `Run_StencilNotExit` on `Kit_StencilNotExit.fbx`).
- Depth prepass trade-off: URP Depth Priming "Skips drawing overlapping pixels", but "isn't supported if you use a deferred rendering path or Multisample Anti-aliasing, or at runtime on mobile devices that use tile-based deferred rendering (TBDR)", and Auto is not supported on iOS [C16]. FrontRooms desktop uses MSAA 4x, so depth priming is off the table on desktop at Ultra (inference); on Apple GPUs HSR already does the job [C24].
- Depth texture copy: "After Transparents" avoids a store and reload of the colour (and MSAA) buffer between the opaque and transparent passes on mobile [C16]; the project already uses `m_CopyDepthMode: 1`.
- LOD to avoid microtriangles: [C27]; the project's LODGroups and missing `LOD_FADE_CROSSFADE` are covered in `research/webgl/04_techniques.md` and sibling draft 11.
- Shader variants and keyword stripping: `shader_feature` builds only the keyword combinations materials use, `multi_compile` builds every combination ("over 6,000 shader variants" in the manual's example), `dynamic_branch` keeps one program and branches at runtime and must be used with `if`, not `#if` [C18]. URP strips feature variants only if "Strip Unused Variants" is on and the feature is off in every URP asset in the build; Unity warns stripping can remove variants a build needs, so enable strict variant matching to check [C17]. Keep variants low; Unity says they "can have a dramatic effect on runtime memory usage" [C22].
- Half precision on mobile: on Apple GPUs 32-bit types use "twice the register space, twice the bandwidth, potentially twice the power"; use half for texture reads and interpolants; conversions are typically free; a float literal promotes the operation to float [C25]. In Unity, `half` has no effect on macOS by default (treated as float) and is real 16-bit on mobile; the Shader Precision Model setting changes this [C19]. FrontRoomsSurface already uses `half` for colour and masks and `float` for world positions (section 6), which is the right split (inference).

## 5. "Staged assets, not shader tricks": believable wear at low cost

**What shipped games do.**
- id Software placed decals as a first-class authoring layer, applied in the forward lighting loop with the lights, from one atlas [C6], and in DOOM Eternal moved small details into geometry decals ("small decals ... part of the authored mesh") with almost no frame cost [C7].
- Bethesda varied hundreds of cells with clutter, kit-bashing and lighting, not with new architecture [C2].
- Insomniac built a whole open world from trims and tiling materials plus a shader that added variation [C1]: the shader varies tone, the authored trim carries the readable shape.
- Naughty Dog's shader-side features (moss, wetness) [C8] show that shader wear is used for broad, physically driven layers, not for single readable events (inference from the abstract).

**What this means for FrontRooms' shader-drawn wear (inference).** FrontRoomsSurface computes its wear from one macro map: tone and dirt (two scales, rotated), water-stain rings and pools (`smoothstep(0.70, 0.73, m2.r)` bands), damp carpet, a floor grime band (`_FloorGrimeHeight`) and ceiling streaks (`_CeilingHeight - 1.6` up to the ceiling). Tone, dirt and damp are broad layers in the Naughty Dog sense and can stay. The ring, the floor band and the streak band are readable events: a real tide mark sits under one leaking tile, a mop line follows the cove base at a fixed height with gaps behind furniture, a streak runs from one diffuser corner. Those read as staged only if they are placed. Authored decals (stain, tide mark, scuff, tape residue, cart scuff on the base, yellowing above a desk) from one 2048² atlas, placed per room as a decal set, give that at the cost of one instanced decal draw per decal material plus the DBuffer pass on desktop [C9, C10].

**Cost comparison (ESTIMATE, to measure).** Shader bands: zero extra draws, a few ALU per pixel on every wall and ceiling pixel, no authoring. Decals: a handful of instanced draws per room, the DBuffer pass (3 RGBA8 targets) and one 3-texel fetch per opaque pixel on desktop, an atlas (2048² BC7 with mips, ESTIMATE 2048² x 1 B x 4/3 = 5.3 MiB per map), and art time. The trade is art time for believability, which is Red's stated bar.
