## 0. Short answer

- **The period tube was big.** Enclosed tubes of 1983–88 were 4 to 4.5 ft (1.2–1.4 m) across [LS71][LS72][LS73]. Today's 32 in (0.8 m) body tube is later [LS76].
- **The open flume was a U trough about 36 in (0.9 m) wide** [LS76], with an outward lip flange along both top edges, walls leaning in 3° and a compound round bottom (patent filed 1979) [LS70].
- **Sections were joined in two ways.**
  - Bolted flange pairs sealed with flexible sealant, up to 60 stainless bolts a flange [LS77].
  - A "seamless" joint of resin putty and a screwed bracket [LS70].
  - A joint may step only downstream [LS77][LS78]. Joints are where slides leaked, cracked, worked loose and showed daylight [LS73][LS77].
- **A closed section began as a lid.** A canopy was bolted to the lip flanges of an open trough [LS70]. That is why mixed slides had a hard edge at every change from open to closed, the 1986 fault [LS51].
- **Operation was a system:** a gate, a red/green start light tripped by sensors, a guard at the top and one at the bottom, CB radio, a monitor of the exit pool, 8–10 s between riders [LS72][LS75]. Rules were on boards at the foot and the top of the stair [LS73][LS75].
- **Slides were added to existing pools from outside.**
  - The tower stood behind or outside the building; the flumes pierced its skin and came back in [LS73][LS74].
  - Swindon's flume "snaked outside and back into the centre" [LS74].
  - Our module (§3, a flume through a room you never reach) is that fact moved indoors.
- **Translucent tubes were a novelty sold to spectators, not the norm** (malls and resorts, 1984–85) [LS53][LS79]. Most enclosed tubes were opaque and dark inside [LS79].
- **Multi-lane racers came only in 1993–94**, at the era lock [LS79]. Several flumes side by side from one tower are period-true [LS79].
- **Greybox:** 16 stills on `W/level_space/sheets/LS11_slides.jpg`. One 18 × 18 × 9 m hall, one 55.8 m flume (6.3 m drop, 11.3 % average) and the same cameras for all three styles.
- **Recommendation: SL-A, the translucent tube.** In the stills it is the only style that reads as a slide from the floor, at a distance and through a window. From below, the open flume reads as a pipe. The module depends on recognising one tube in every room it crosses. SL-C's tunnels stay available as a deeper, "wrong" variant (§5).

### 1.2 Joints, sections and supports

- **Flanged joints** (current manuals) [LS77]:
  - the faces are cleaned, primed, caulked with flexible polyurethane sealant and bolted with 3/8 in stainless bolts, "up to 60 bolts" a flange, pre-drilled;
  - inside, the sealant is wiped flush and shows as a thin line;
  - daily checks: drips from flanges, worn or peeled sealant, loose or missing fasteners;
  - a separating joint shows a gap, torn sealant and "in extreme cases, daylight";
  - joints must never be glassed over: they crack into jagged edges;
  - alignment pins make sure "the downhill piece is not higher than the uphill piece". A current standard allows a step only in the sliding direction and openings of at most 8 mm [LS78].
- **The 1981 "seamless" joint** [LS70]:
  - the outer faces are machined for 1 in either side of the joint;
  - resin putty goes on a 2 in strip;
  - an L-bracket 2 in wide is screwed across with recessed, capped screws: "for all purposes a seamless junction";
  - the exit ramp, by contrast, is in flanged sections bolted together.
- **Closed sections:** "a canopy or cap 58 can be attached to the flanges". It adds stiffness "and an element of excitement" [LS70].
- **Faults in service:** loose bolts closed the flumes for a day (1988); minor cracks and leaks were found in testing [LS73].
- **Supports:**
  - small slides stand on 6 and 8 in steel pipe columns with yoke plates and lateral arms [LS76];
  - large ones stand on round tube columns 16–20 in across, with radial arms reaching 11–15 ft. Yoke plates are "field positioned for slope and rotation" and then welded; steel is galvanised after fabrication; footings are 3 ft below grade (structural drawings, project no. 9507, c. 1995) [LS77];
  - columns stand every 4–5 m along the path [LS77].
  
  Both the paired portal of `50` §2.2 and the single column with an arm are period-plausible.

### 1.3 Towers, the start and dispatch

- **Stairs:**
  - a spiral staircase to the flume entries (a leisure pool, 1990s) [LS75];
  - red and teal stairs climbing a little over 40 ft at a mall (1982–83) [LS72];
  - a tower 40 ft high "at the rear of the pool", its stair reached behind the diving pool (1988) [LS73];
  - small catalogue towers: steep stairs (8 in rise, 8 in tread, 24 in wide) or easy ones (7 in, 11 in, 36 in); platforms 4 × 8 ft to 6 × 8 ft; options of "a locking gate to flume entrance" and a roof of steel, wood or fabric [LS76].
- **The start (1981 patent)** [LS70]:
  - the rider stands in a starting pool and sits on a seat 19 in high, "transversely bowed and longitudinally tipped" so that water runs over it into the trough;
  - pool, seat and handrail flange are one fibreglass piece, with steps leading into it.
  
  Today a crossbar sits 800–1,100 mm above the slide surface between the start and the slide, "advisable also for tubes" [LS78].
- **Dispatch** [LS75]:
  - one rider at a time;
  - a red/green light above each entry turns red when an entry sensor sees a rider; it turns green again after a lower sensor (two thirds of the way down) plus 3 s;
  - a guard watches the exit pool at all times, and a second works the entries at busy times;
  - the guards talk on CB radio; the top guard can watch the exit pools on a monitor.
  
  In 1983 riders went 8–10 s apart. At closing the water pressure dropped and a rider had to "scoot to the bottom" [LS72].

### 1.4 Speed, several lanes, translucency

- **Speed slides:**
  - a 1979 speed slide; free-fall speed slides in 1984–85, one from a 72 ft tower [LS79];
  - a 1988 municipal speed tube with "a freefall gradient": 4 s, about 40 mph [LS73].
- **Several flumes from one tower:**
  - four slides, two enclosed and two open (1985); four enclosed slides (1986); a five-lane mat slide (1986) [LS79];
  - four slides at a 1988 municipal pool [LS73];
  - racing slides with many lanes only from 1993–94 [LS79]. That is the era lock, so they are not for FrontRooms.
- **Translucency:**
  - see-through tubes in a mall in 1984, lit at night (`50` [LS53]);
  - "two slides made of semi-transparent tubes" at a resort (proposed 1983, opened 1985) [LS79];
  - against these, a maker in 2010: "Before translucent technology, enclosed waterslides were dark, almost industrial looking" [LS79].
  
  Reading: opaque tubes were the norm, and translucent ones were sold for the spectator ("so everyone can watch", [LS53]). Inference, to verify: period see-through fibreglass was unpigmented polyester laminate like rooflight sheet, milky and slightly green-blue, showing light and silhouettes rather than detail.
- **Darkness as a feature:** a 1988 ride went "from light into darkness and through a waterfall" [LS73].

### 1.6 Signs and rules

- "Over the age of eight, four and a half feet tall or more and could swim"; 20p a ride in books of five or ten tickets (1988) [LS73].
- Boards at the foot and the top of the stair [LS75]:
  - health, age (over 8) and swimming ability;
  - feet first, on the back;
  - no kneeling, no sitting, no riding in pairs;
  - wait for the green light.
- After the 1983 injuries the operators removed a curve, made riders wear helmets for their first two rides, rounded the outlet lip and added "extra warning and instruction signs" [LS71].
- Wristbands with ten ride tags (1983) [LS72].

### 1.7 How slides were added to existing pools

- **Edinburgh** (a 1970 pool; flumes from 1988) [LS73]:
  - design and build by a private partner who owned the flumes;
  - the 40 ft tower rose "at the rear of the pool"; four slides and a separate splash pool;
  - the flumes' plant room had its own staff, and chlorine exposure there led to a ventilation upgrade in 1989;
  - one flume was out of action by 1996, and all were removed in 2001;
  - the tower stood "disconnected from the main building" until 2008.
- **Swindon** (a 1976 dome; slides 1987) [LS74]:
  - three enclosed slides "accessed from a tower outside the dome";
  - they emerged "spaghetti-like from the exterior of the dome";
  - the slides, the tower and the splash pool are excluded from the listing.
- **Small indoor slides (1981):** they started and finished inside while the flumes ran outside the walls (`50` [LS52]). Swindon's flume "snaked outside and back into the centre" [LS74].

For FrontRooms: a slide added to the club in about 1987 would have its tower in a neighbouring space. Its flume would cross rooms that were never planned for it. The module in §3 is that story.

### 1.8 Noise and air

- A 1970s leisure pool: "the noise in the pool … is deafening, like some electronic aviary" [LS74].
- Neighbours objected to the noise of a planned slide (1983) [LS79].
- The closed tube from inside: "like being hurled at breakneck speed down a narrow sewage pipe" (the Evening News on the 54 in spiral) [LS73].
- Chlorine fumes in the flume plant room; staff claimed "flume flu" (1992) [LS73].

### 2.2 SL-A · translucent tube

- **Cross-section:** a round tube 1.2 m inside (the period's 4 ft); laminate 8 mm. An external flange pair at every joint: 2 × 50 mm wide, 65 mm proud.
- **Sections and joints:** 2.4 m, bolted flange pairs, a thin sealant ring inside. Flanges and saddles are denser laminate, so in light they read as darker rings. From outside the tube is banded every 2.4 m; from inside, every joint passes as a ring of shadow.
- **Colour and translucency:**
  - milky aqua (greybox #6BD1D6 at 55 % opacity), the family of F1's turquoise water;
  - flanges denser (85 %);
  - through the wall you see light and shapes, not detail;
  - not banked (it is round).
- **Night:** lit from inside, so the tube becomes a line of light through the building (still v6). The 1984 tubes "light up" at night (`50` [LS53]); the greybox uses a lamp per section and the hall lamps at a fifth.
- **Supports:** §2.1; the saddle follows the lower 130° of the tube.
- **Tower:** B4-S, a spiral round a post: Ø 2.4 m, 30 risers of 0.20 m, 2.5 turns, arriving at the platform's west edge.
- **Start:** the tube's mouth (1.2 m tall) stands above the tub; the crossbar runs just over the mouth, 1.62 m above the platform.
- **Splash pool and exit:** §2.1; the round mouth sits 50 mm above the water.

### 2.3 SL-B · open flume, white with one band

- **Cross-section:** a U 0.9 m inside: a round bottom of 0.45 m radius, walls to 0.65 m, a 70 mm lip flange along both top edges. In the spec but not modelled: walls leaning in 3° and a compound bottom as in [LS70]; a higher outer wall in bends.
- **Sections and joints:** 2.4 m; bolted U-shaped flange ribs, 60 mm proud and 100 mm wide, meeting the lip flange; a sealant line inside.
- **Colour:**
  - ivory gelcoat inside and out (greybox #F2EDDB), slightly warmer than the tile so the flume reads as its own material;
  - one teal band (greybox #008085), 0.27 m high, on the outside under the lip, picking up the water;
  - a bright water film in the bottom.
- **Banking:** up to 20° in bends, outer wall raised.
- **Supports:** §2.1; the saddle follows the round bottom.
- **Tower:** a square switchback: four flights of 10 risers (0.15 / 0.30 m), 1.0 m wide; galvanised grating landings of 2.2 × 1.2 m; guards on every open edge.
- **Start:** the open trough leaves the tub at the seat; the crossbar is 1.3 m above the platform.
- **Splash pool and exit:** §2.1.

### 2.4 SL-C · open runs into opaque tunnels

- **Cross-section:** SL-B's trough. On closed sections a bolt-on cap (the canopy of [LS70]) sits on the lip flanges: a half-round lid of 0.52 m radius, 1.17 m total height.
- **Where it is closed:** the 180° bend in mid-hall (the blind spot of [LS51]) and every section within 1 m of a wall it passes through. 13 of 24 sections in the greybox.
- **Transitions:** only at joints. The cap's leading edge carries its own flange, which is the cut of [LS51]. The step in silhouette, from 0.65 to 1.17 m, can be seen from the floor (still x4).
- **Colour:** ivory runs as in SL-B, without the band; caps dark grey-teal (greybox #263D42), dark inside. SL-C is the only one of the three with a dark element, and the dark marks the blind spots.
- **Supports, tower, start, splash pool:** as SL-B.

## 3. The module: a flume through rooms you never reach

**What the player sees, in order** (stills 1, 5 and 3 of each style):
1. **In the slide hall:** the flume runs overhead and enters a square hole in the west wall. Round the tube the hole shows a dark gap and a dim block wall behind.
2. **Through a window in the same wall** (E3: sill 0.9 m, head 2.5 m, two panes in a painted steel frame): a tiled room with ankle water and no door on this side. The flume crosses it 2–3 m up on two paired supports, turns 90° and leaves through the room's far wall.
3. **Somewhere else, the flume comes back** through a different wall of the hall (the north wall), low, 1.0 m above the floor. It turns once and drops into the splash pool.
4. **The turn in between** happens in a service void the player never enters: block walls, one dim lamp. It is glimpsed only through the gaps round the flume in the wall holes.

**Modules (proposals for 关卡设计):**

| Module | Footprint | Content | Ports |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_PL_FlumeCrossing | 2 × 6 cells (6 × 18 m), ceiling 4.8 m | the flume (a straight and one 90° bend at 2.0 m), two paired supports, ankle water (C2) or dry tile, two lamps | no walkable port on the player's side; one E3 window from a neighbour; two flume holes on different walls |
| T_PL_ServiceVoid | about 4 × 1.5 cells, height 4.5 m | the flume's unseen turns; block walls, one dim lamp | flume holes only; a staff door on the far side (never reached) |
| T_PL_SlideHall | 6 × 6 cells (18 × 18 m), Tall 9.0 m (D5) | F4: tower, platform, start, flume, C1 splash pool, exit lane | the hall's doors; two flume holes on different walls |

**Rules:**
- **M1** · A flume enters and leaves a crossing room through different walls, never the same wall twice.
- **M2** · The flume is seen, never touched: no walkable route leads into a crossing room. Sight crosses, bodies do not (R7).
- **M3** · Every crossing room is lit with two lamps, even though nobody reaches it (darkness is never cover; R8).
- **M4** · Every wall hole leaves a gap round the flume, so the other side is glimpsed.
- **M5** · At most one window per crossing room, and the window never shows both holes. The player joins the pieces in the mind.
- **M6** · Over any walkable cell the flume clears 2.1 m. In the greybox the run over the hall floor is 3.0–4.4 m up.
- **M7** · Length budget: for a +6 m start at an average slope of at least 10 %, the flume is at most about 60 m. Each crossing costs 10–15 m, so a slide has at most two crossing rooms.

## 4. Splicing into the terrain grammar

- **F4 restated:** B4-S (spiral, SL-A greybox) or B3 (switchback, SL-B/C greybox) → A5 platform at +6.0 → the flume → C1 splash pool (0.9 m) → the exit lane: B1 steps out of the pool, then a deck route back to the tower foot and to the hall's doors.
- **A new connector, B10 · Flume:** one-way, down only. (Renamed from B9 by the parent chat at 17:16: `40` §4 had given B9 to the creeping slope.)
  - Ports: the start at a platform; the end at C1 water level.
  - Per-cell data (`40` §6.1): `connector = Flume (one-way, from, to)`. Cells under the flume keep their own floor, with at least 2.1 m clear (M6).
  - Not in the Relay's navigation graph.
- **Height:** a +6.0 m platform needs about 3.0 m above it (a standing rider, the crossbar, clearance), so the hall needs at least 9.0 m clear.
  - The F4 module in `40` §6.2 ("B4 to a +5.0 platform" in Tall 5.4) does not fit. Choose one:
    1. a double-height slide hall (D5, 8.1–9.0 m), as in the greybox;
    2. the tower rises through a ceiling opening to the floor above (the period's rooftop and outside towers [LS72][LS73][LS74]). The start is on the upper floor and the slide becomes a one-way storey connector;
    3. a low version in Tall 5.4: platform +2.4 m (16 risers of 0.15 m), flume at most 22 m, one 180° bend, no crossing room.
- **Grid (R5):** the hall is 6 × 6 cells, the tower 2 × 2. The 180° bend (3.0 m radius) is inscribed in 2 × 3 cells and the 90° bends (2.0 m) in about 1.5 × 1.5. The splash pool spans 2.4 × 1.7 cells.
- **R1:** a built object at every height change: the tower stair, the step down into the start tub, the splash pool's steps.
- **R2:** wall holes belong to the wall module of the room they pierce.
- **R4:** the flume is never symmetric. One 180° bend, and its in and out holes are on different walls (M1).
- **R6:** the platform's only way up is the stair. The slide is the fast way down; the walking way back (exit lane, hall doors) always exists, and the Relay uses it.
- **R7:** the window into the crossing room.
- **R8:** lit landings and platform; the splash pool lit from below and above.

## 5. Gameplay (proposals for Red)

- **P1 · The player may ride.** The slide is a one-way fast route from the platform to the splash pool, about 11 s for 56 m. Like a spiral stair, it is the player's shortcut. The only way in is through the gate into the start tub; the only way out is the splash pool.
- **P2 · The Relay never rides** and never waits in the tube. It takes the stair and the deck. R6's second route (the exit lane and the hall doors) is always present.
- **P3 · No stopping.** Once started, the ride cannot be stopped or reversed: riders stopping in tunnels was the period's fault [LS51]. The camera follows the riding line, and the player can look around.
- **P4 · The landing is the slow point.** 0.9 m of water means wading speed, and the steps out are at the side.
  - The splash pool is always lit and always visible from the deck (the 1993 fault [LS73]).
  - Proposal: no chase starts at the splash pool for N seconds after a landing (fairness; 系统设计 sets N).
- **P5 · What a ride shows.**
  - SL-A: the rooms it crosses, as blurred light through the wall, and a ring of shadow at every joint.
  - SL-C: light, dark, light; the tunnels are where you cannot see out.
  - SL-B: the ceiling and the walls going past.
- **P6 · The start light is a state, never a radar.** Red and green follow the tube's own sensors (the period system [LS75]). It never reports the Relay.
- **P7 · Water on or off** is a level state. Running water means sound, a water film and the spill at the tub. A dry flume is slower and sounds hollow. Red picks per level, or uses it as an event.
- **P8 · A silhouette in the tube** (the `50` idea) only if Red wants someone else in the building. Otherwise the tube is seen empty, lit and running.
- **Variants:**
  - SL-A as the main slide (recommended, §0);
  - SL-C deeper in the level as the "wrong" slide, its tunnels at every wall;
  - SL-B as a small club slide near the pool entrance: no crossing room, platform +2.4 m.

## 6. Sound hooks (for the sound line; list only)

- running water down the flume: a rush that follows the flume through the walls of every room it crosses;
- water spilling over the seat of the start tub (a small weir);
- pumps and filters behind the walls (the plant room, the service void);
- a body on gelcoat: a squeak of skin, and a thump at every joint (2.4 m apart, about every 0.5 s at 5 m/s), louder at SL-C's cap edges;
- the hollow drum of a fibreglass section when struck, boomier in closed sections;
- the splash at the lip, then the wave slapping the splash pool's walls;
- the start light's relay clicking from red to green; the gate's latch;
- drips from a leaking joint onto the floor below;
- the pool hall's long reverberation ("deafening, like some electronic aviary" [LS74]).

## 10. Limits

- **Colours** come from text: one mall's red and teal stairs, "shiny" slides, red leisure-centre structure. No period colour photograph was opened.
- **Section length (2.4 m)** is inferred. Catalogues give only small slides' segments (1.0–2.0 m straights).
- **Modern standards (EN 1069-1:2017, a Maryland code of unchecked date)** fill gaps only: the crossbar, the flume end, the splash-pool length. A period sign must not quote them.
- **The dispatch system [LS75]** was published in 1998. The 1980s malls show the spacing (8–10 s) but not the lights. To verify before the start light is called era-true.
- **Translucent period fibreglass** as milky unpigmented laminate is an inference.
- **The ride time (11 s)** is estimated from period speeds (5.3 m/s), not measured.
