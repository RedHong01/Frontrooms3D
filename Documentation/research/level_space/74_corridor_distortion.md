## 1. The short answer

**Yes, games do both, but rarely while the player sprints toward a goal they can see.** The four sweeps' 137 rows, plus 13 catalogue rows that `comfort.md` §2 mined, merge in §3 into 106 rows on 109 titles, demos and tools. The space family appears in 79 rows and the camera family in 37 (18 rows have both). These counts measure what the sweeps looked for (each had its own angle: AAA, indie, build, comfort), not how common a technique is across all games.

**Family A · the camera distorts (镜头畸变).** It is common, small and switchable. Rows by technique: a full-screen effect 24, roll, tilt or shake 14, a whole-frame lens 8, an FOV or zoom change 7 (only 2 of them dolly zooms), tunnel vision 4, a change of camera mode 4, speed cues 2.
1. **The most common form is a full-screen effect that follows one fear number** (wave, glitch, blur, ink, tendrils, tint; the number is sanity, distance to the monster, gaze or damage). Indie horror uses it most: Amnesia's open code drives a screen wave with 1 − sanity / threshold and a throbbing zoom with how fast sanity is draining (M1); SOMA, Mouthwashing, Bendy, Amnesia: Rebirth and The Bunker do the same by look. AAA uses the same thing as a short **status layer**: Halo 3's Cortana moments (tint, focus pulls, edge imagery, the player slowed), Call of Duty 4's "Aftermath" crawl (blur, tilt, slowed, all level-script calls), Max Payne 3's colour separation.
2. **Real lens changes are small pulses with a ceiling**: SCP – Containment Breach caps its zoom pulse at 1.1×, Amnesia squashes FOV and aspect once when a sanity hit lands, Quake III wobbles its underwater FOV by ±1°.
3. **A lens that is distorted all the time is a whole-game look**, not a corridor event: Alien: Isolation's late-1970s film and VHS, Hellblade II's anamorphic lens, the bodycam fisheye of Unrecord and Paranormal Tales, POOLS' VHS (shipped on iPhone and iPad).
4. **Edge darkening (tunnel vision)** is the one camera effect the comfort research recommends rather than limits: in VR a soft circular mask that narrows during motion lowered sickness without lowering presence (Fernandes and Feiner 2016).
5. **The true dolly zoom is rare.** Only The Last of Us Part II documents one, and it ships behind its own On/Off switch; Amnesia's "early hallway" dolly zoom rests on a fan wiki.
Every camera distortion found with documented comfort handling sits behind a switch or slider, or was tuned below what felt best (Amnesia, 2010).

**Family B · the space deforms (空间变形).** It is common in AAA, but almost never as a mesh that bends in front of a running player. Rows by technique: the place swapped 23 (14 of them AAA), the light taken away 19, a loop 18 (12 indie), real architecture moving 17 (12 AAA), a stretch or narrowing 15 (10 indie), portals 7, bends 7.
1. **AAA swaps the place**: two complete copies and a teleport (Titanfall 2, Dishonored 2's Timepiece), a world streamed in during a short rift (Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart, "that .7 seconds"), dedicated nightmare maps (Arkham Asylum), a spot swapped for its dark version when its light is taken (Alan Wake 2). Indie does the same out of view (Layers of Fear: a trigger spot or a gaze check, the eye lured by a light or a sound).
2. **The loop** (Super Mario 64, P.T., The Exit 8, SCP-970, every jam corridor) is the cheapest and most shipped; it is ruled out for Run!.
3. **Real architecture moving in view** is the AAA answer to "the space itself changes": Dishonored 2's Clockwork Mansion (one prime mover), Control's Ashtray Maze (modular animated pieces timed to music), Portal 2's panels, Uncharted's baked collapses; Observer's walls splitting into hanging shards is the indie twin. Quantum Break is the only case found where geometry is distorted in real time (a wave system displacing tessellated geometry).
4. **The light taken away** reshapes the readable space: Dead Space's light failures on tension curves, Resident Evil Village's House Beneviento, F.E.A.R.'s hallway going dark section by section, DOORS' Rush breaking every light room by room, NaissanceE's leaving light.
5. **An in-view stretch or narrowing** (the classic "tunnel getting longer" and its kin) is rare and mostly indie, and it is almost always covered by darkness, blur or a slow scripted walk: P.T.'s red loop, Mouthwashing, Spooky's closing walls, Layers of Fear's elongated hallway; Max Payne is the AAA case. **No developer source explains how any of them stretched the corridor.**
6. **Bends and portals** belong to runners (the Trash Dash sample's vertex curve) and puzzle games (Hyperbolica, Portal, Manifold Garden); portals are the most expensive family found.

**How they did it, in one line each.** Camera effects are one or two floats a frame inside a post pass or a camera script. Space effects are copies plus a teleport, rigid pieces animated from one mover, or lights on a schedule. The only in-view mesh deformations with a build source are SCP-CB's per-frame mesh scale, Observer's GPU vertex animation and Quantum Break's wave displacement.

**For #3 THE LIGHT LEAVES.** REC 离港 is already built from the three space-side moves with the most support: light taken away (S-DARK), real architecture moving inside the door's light (c2 LET GO, S-MOVE: the Observer and Dishonored 2 pattern) and a corridor changed in the dark (the hidden cells revealed unlit: the Layers of Fear and Alan Wake 2 pattern). What it lacks is anything in the camera. §6 proposes four layers: **L1 the dark closes in** (tunnel vision that follows the leaving light: URP Vignette, free on every platform, comfort-positive), **L2 the lens flinches once** at the trip (a one-way Lens Distortion and Chromatic Aberration kick centred on the door, free, scaled by Camera Motion %), **L3 the whole-lens warp** Red asked about, shown on a key for comparison only (it is the classic stretch made by the lens, and it must be paid back in view), and **L4 c2 LET GO kept as the space answer** (the vertex stretch of the dark corridor is rejected: it is the classic). **Prototype L1 and L2 first, together, as one lever in SP7**: both live in URP's existing uber pass, so they cost no new pass on desktop, iPhone, iPad or WebGL.

## 2. The taxonomy: what each technique feels like, who did it, how, what it costs

Row counts are over §3's 106 merged rows (a row counts once for each technique it names); "AAA" includes AA and platform-holder work, "indie" includes demos, jams and fan games. Costs are for FrontRooms' renderer as read from its files (`build.md` §2: Forward+, MSAA 4x, HDR, depth texture on, opaque texture off, two renderer features; `FrontRoomsPost.asset` already runs Lens Distortion −0.04 at scale 1.01 and Chromatic Aberration 0.06 in URP's single uber pass). On iPhone and iPad "store and load" means the frame leaves tile memory for main memory and comes back, which Unity names as the cost of standard post-processing on tile-based GPUs (`build.md` N11). Comfort risks are the comfort sweep's readings (§5 has the numbers).

### 2.1 Family A · the camera distorts (镜头畸变)

**A1 · C-FX · a full-screen effect that follows one fear number (24 rows: 13 indie, 11 AAA).**
- *Feels:* the picture itself goes wrong as the danger grows (waves, glitch, blur, ink, tendrils, tint, static), and eases when it passes.
- *Strongest:* **Amnesia: The Dark Descent** (indie, 2010; M1): sanity drives a screen wave and a zoom; open code: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrictionalGames/AmnesiaTheDarkDescent/master/amnesia/src/game/LuxPlayerHelpers.cpp . **SOMA** (indie, 2015; M4): glitches by distance, by gaze and by damage, https://www.pcgamer.com/soma-review/ . **Mouthwashing** (indie, 2024; M80): "the screen will start shaking and blurring" near the duct creature, https://vgtimes.com/guides/115177-mouthwashing-walkthrough-guide.html . **Halo 3** (AAA, 2007; M5): Cortana moments tint the view, pull focus and slow the player, https://www.halopedia.org/Cortana_moments_and_Gravemind_moments . **Call of Duty 4, "Aftermath"** (AAA, 2007; M6): blur pulses, a tilting ground reference and a slowed crawl, all level-script calls, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dotNNJ/CoD4MW/master/raw/maps/aftermath.gsc . **P.T.**, **Arkham Asylum** and **Max Payne 3** use a fake crash or colour separation as the extreme form (M51, M38, M7).
- *How built:* one post pass whose weights follow a game value, eased toward a goal each frame (Amnesia: wave alpha = 1 − sanity / threshold at 1 per second; zoom alpha = drain volume × a 0.6-1.0 sine; a menu toggle), or a stack of engine hooks fired by level script (CoD4).
- *Cost:* effects inside URP's uber pass (Lens Distortion, Chromatic Aberration, Vignette, colour, grain) cost almost nothing more on any platform once post-processing is on. A custom wave or glitch is one Full Screen Pass Renderer Feature: small on desktop; one more full-frame store and load on iPhone and iPad (a warp reads other pixels, so it cannot use framebuffer fetch: `build.md`'s inference); one more pass on WebGL, where "Web performance is close to native apps on the GPU", with `highp` UVs because WebGL2 shaders default to `mediump`.
- *Comfort:* moderate. XAG 117 asks for an option to turn such camera behaviours off, and its Halo Infinite example puts full-screen effects on a slider; Frictional's own line went from "pull back below best" (2010) to an off switch (Rebirth 2020, Bunker 2024; M2, M3); Minecraft swaps its nausea warp for a green overlay at low settings (M14).

**A2 · C-FOV · the lens itself changes: FOV, dolly zoom, zoom pulse, aspect squash (7 rows: 5 AAA, 2 indie).**
- *Feels:* the view breathes, zooms, or (dolly zoom) the far end stretches away while the near frame holds.
- *Strongest:* **The Last of Us Part II** (AAA, 2020; M11): "moments where the camera moves backwards and zooms at the same time", shipped behind a "Dolly zoom effect" On/Off, https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/the-last-of-us-part-ii/accessibility/ . **Amnesia** (M1): a dolly zoom in "one of the early hallways" (TV Tropes, ONE SOURCE, https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/VertigoEffect ) and an FOV and aspect squash on each sanity hit (code). **SCP – Containment Breach** (indie; M12): a zoom pulse near threats capped at 1.1×, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Regalis11/scpcb/master/Main.bb . **Quake III Arena** (AAA, 1999; M13): underwater the horizontal and vertical FOV move ±1° in opposition every 2.5 s, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake-III-Arena/master/code/cgame/cg_view.c . **Minecraft** (M14) and **Spider-Man 2** (M15): the speed FOV on a slider.
- *How built:* one float a frame (`Camera.fieldOfView`, vertical in Unity); a dolly zoom holds the target's frustum height h = 2 · d · tan(FOV / 2) and recomputes the FOV each frame (Unity's old manual recipe, `unreachable_technique.md` B1).
- *Cost:* free on all four platforms.
- *Comfort:* HIGH. XAG 117 asks for FOV changes to be optional and for an FOV setting; Nesky (GDC 2014, via a translation) lists a quick FOV change as a cause of nausea; Draper 2001 found image scales of 0.5 and 2.0 worse than 1.0.
- *For Run!:* agreed rule 11 says "no FOV trick"; a widening FOV is the stock sprint cue, and a narrowing one makes the door bigger (§4.1).

**A3 · C-LENS · a whole-frame lens look (8 rows: 5 AAA, 3 indie).**
- *Feels:* the world seen through a camera, all the time: fisheye edges, colour fringes, grain, scan lines.
- *Strongest:* **Alien: Isolation** (AAA, 2014; M17): a late-1970s film image, its interface recorded to VHS on tube TVs, https://www.engadget.com/2014-10-07-alien-isolation-launch-interview.html . **Hellblade II** (AAA, 2024; M18): an anamorphic look from lens distortion, chromatic aberration, spherical aberration and bokeh, https://mp1st.com/news/hellblade-2-will-bring-anamorphic-cinema-like-effect-to-video-games . **Bodycam and found footage** (indie; M19: Unrecord, Paranormal Tales), https://80.lv/articles/tutorial-setting-up-an-unrecord-style-bodycam-effect-in-ue5 . **POOLS** (indie, 2024; M20): VHS, a death as a rewind, shipped on iPhone and iPad, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pools_(video_game) . **Observer** (indie, 2017; M70): "everything goes fish-eyed or wobbly".
- *How built:* a lens post pass; in URP the stock **Lens Distortion** override ("distorts the final rendered picture to simulate the shape of a real-world camera lens") in the uber pass, and **Panini Projection** (for "a very large field of view") as its own pass.
- *Cost:* Lens Distortion and Chromatic Aberration are already paid for in FrontRooms; Panini adds one full-screen pass everywhere (a store and load on iPhone and iPad).
- *Comfort:* moderate; bodycam players ask for a switch (snippets, UNSURE); Unity's own VR advice is to avoid Lens Distortion and Chromatic Aberration.

**A4 · C-ROLL · roll, tilt, shake (14 rows: 10 AAA, 4 indie).**
- *Strongest:* **Eternal Darkness** (AAA, 2002; M21): the camera tilts more as sanity drops, https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/481054/nroke-reality-the-unsettling-results-of-eternal-darkness-sanity-effects/ . **Uncharted 2**'s collapse shakes the screen (M66). **Spider-Man 2** puts its swing roll, pitch and FOV on one slider (M15), https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/marvels-spider-man-2-all-accessibility-options-2315984 . **DOORS** shakes slightly during the Seek chase (M98). CoD4 tilts the view by rotating a ground-reference entity (M6).
- *How built:* an additive camera layer (Naughty Dog: shake as "a separate layer of additive cameras", GDC 2012 abstract, https://gdcvault.com/play/1015514/The-Cameras-of-Uncharted ).
- *Cost:* free.
- *Comfort:* HIGH for roll (Bonato 2009: pitch plus roll worse than pitch alone; GAG: keep a constant horizon; Nesky #27: no roll about the camera's own axis); shake is on XAG 117's avoid-or-switch list.

**A5 · C-TUNNEL · edge darkening, tunnel vision, letterbox (4 rows: 2 AAA, 2 indie; plus the VR comfort tool).**
- *Feels:* the edges of sight darken or close; the world narrows to the middle.
- *Strongest:* **Amnesia: Rebirth** (indie, 2020; M2): "black tendrils at the edge of your screen", https://primagames.com/gaming/amnesia-rebirth-beginners-guide-fear-survival-tips . **Bendy** (indie; M10): on mobile ports the ink "will instead cover the player's screen border and grow thicker the closer the Ink Demon is", https://bendy.wiki.gg/wiki/Ink_Demon . **Halo 3**'s Gravemind moments: tentacle imagery at the screen's edges (M5). **The Evil Within** (AAA; M24): 2.35:1 letterbox for the whole game. In VR it is the comfort tool: soft-edged, circular field-of-view restrictors that narrowed during motion made people more comfortable "without decreasing the participants' sense of presence", and most did not notice the subtle ones (Fernandes and Feiner 2016, via Columbia's release, read for 74: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160614121922.htm , ONE SOURCE); Unity's XR Interaction Toolkit eases its tunnelling vignette in "to reduce the potential distraction from instantaneously changing the user's field of view" (`build.md` N9).
- *How built:* URP **Vignette**, in the uber pass (code read for 74: `dist = abs(uv - center) * intensity`, with intensity = 3 × the slider; `vfactor = pow(saturate(1.0 - dot(dist, dist)), smoothness)`, with smoothness = 5 × the slider; the colour is multiplied in by `lerp(color, 1, vfactor)`), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal/Shaders/PostProcessing/Common.hlsl .
- *Cost:* free; the one effect of this family that Unity 6.5+ can keep in tile memory on iPhone and iPad.
- *Comfort:* LOW: it lowers sickness in the studies read. Its risk is hiding what must be read.

**A6 · C-SPEED · radial blur, speed lines, motion blur (2 rows).**
- **Halo Infinite** (AAA; M16): radial blur and speed lines each 0-100%, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117 ; Amnesia's `FadeRadialBlurTo` (M1).
- *Cost:* URP Motion Blur is its own pass (4, 6 or 8 taps plus depth); a radial blur is a custom pass. *Comfort:* HIGH (XAG 117 names motion blur; Unity: avoid in VR).

**A7 · C-VIEW · the camera's mode or direction changes (4 rows).**
- **Crash Bandicoot**'s boulder levels (AAA, 1996; M25) turn the camera to face the runner; Andy Gavin: "we worried about the camera, dizziness, and the player's ability to judge depth", https://all-things-andy-gavin.com/2011/02/02/making-crash-bandicoot-part-1/ . **Returnal** drops into first person in the house (M26). **Outlast** gives a look-back control during a run (M28). **God of War** (2018) never cuts (M27).
- *Cost:* free. *Comfort:* moderate (a camera move the player did not make).

### 2.2 Family B · the space deforms (空间变形)

**B1 · S-SWAP · the place is replaced: another copy, another time, a dream, a change out of view (23 rows: 14 AAA, 9 indie).**
- *Feels:* the corridor becomes another one, or is different when you look back.
- *Strongest:* **Titanfall 2, "Effect and Cause"** (AAA, 2016; M34): two maps stacked, "I just teleport you the exact distance to the other level", even mid-jump, https://www.vice.com/en/article/a-behind-the-scenes-look-at-the-best-mission-in-titanfall-2 . **Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart** (AAA, 2021; M36): the next world loads only "in the middle space", in "that .7 seconds", https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/ratchet-and-clank-rift-apart-interview . **Alan Wake 2** (AAA, 2023; M37): taking a spot's light switches it to "the 'dark' version of this spot", https://gameinformer.com/preview/2023/09/27/exploring-a-killer-hotel-as-alan-wake . **Layers of Fear** (indie, 2016; M46): a trigger spot or a gaze check, the eye lured by "lighting or sounds", https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/terrifying-players-with-unstable-level-design-in-i-layers-of-fear-i- . **Arkham Asylum** (M38): the nightmares are their own maps from modular sets. **Anatomy** (M49): the house rebuilt between real program quits.
- *How built:* complete copies plus a teleport by the exact offset; a stream hidden in a short transit; trigger volumes and gaze checks for out-of-view changes.
- *Cost:* near zero per frame; the copies cost memory; streaming time matters on phones and WebGL.
- *Comfort:* none if seamless (Insomniac: a naive teleport breaks motion blur and physics); disorientation by design.
- *For Run!:* teleports are out (rule 11). The change-in-the-dark form is what #3 already does with its hidden cells (revealed unlit).

**B2 · S-LOOP · the corridor repeats (18 rows: 12 indie, 6 AAA).**
- *Strongest:* **Super Mario 64**'s Endless Stairs (warp, fog, a Shepard scale; M54); **P.T.** (a map return at the door; M51); **The Exit 8** (two identical modules joined at a bend, anomalies of scale and light; M52), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exit_8 ; **SCP-970** in SCP – Containment Breach (a shift of exactly 1024 units that carries door states and nearby items, with a pass counter; M55), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Regalis11/scpcb/master/UpdateEvents.bb ; the jam baseline, "a single hallway that teleports you back to the beginning past a certain point" (M56), https://itch.io/post/14018686/view-in-topic . Drawn instead of hidden: **Manifold Garden** instances the world in a grid (M58), https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=11525 ; **Infinite Hall** repeats a distance field in a shader (M59).
- *Cost:* near zero. *Comfort:* none if the seam is invisible.
- *For Run!:* out (Red's 11-03 agreement, 71 §4.2 M2, and rule 11).

**B3 · S-MOVE · real architecture moves in view: rearranges, folds, rotates, collapses, splits (17 rows: 12 AAA, 5 indie).**
- *Feels:* the building is a machine or a body; walls travel, rooms turn, the way between drifts apart.
- *Strongest:* **Dishonored 2, the Clockwork Mansion** (AAA, 2016; M63): kinetic sculpture with "one source of movement", AI working in both layouts and while rooms move, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-deep-dive-i-dishonored-2-s-i-clockwork-mansion . **Control, the Ashtray Maze** (AAA, 2019; M64): "fast prototyping of heavily animated environments" on "the most linear section of the game", https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/see-how-i-control-i-s-ashtray-maze-was-made-at-the-gdc-2020-summits- . **Portal 2** (AAA, 2011; M65): big moves only "where we could be sure of the player's location", https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_2_developer_commentary . **Uncharted 2** (AAA, 2009; M66): "the building collapsing is a pre-simulated event" under "real-time control of the player", https://mcvuk.com/?p=121982 . **Quantum Break** (AAA, 2016; M68): a wave system "actually distorting the geometry", https://www.fxguide.com/?p=185607 . **Observer** (indie, 2017; M70): walls "breaking into shards that hang in the air", built with "custom vertex factories" that "pushed procedural animations entirely into GPU", https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/probing-the-minds-behind-the-cyberpunk-horror-of-observer . **ICO**'s bridge splitting (M76) and Little Nightmares II's breaking path (M78) are the "way between drifts apart" form.
- *How built:* kinematic rigid pieces from one prime mover (Animator, Timeline or script); baked simulations played under live control; GPU vertex animation for many pieces.
- *Cost:* transform updates for rigid pieces on all four platforms; moved pieces lose baked lightmaps and need probes; skinned pieces use compute in Unity 6.6, which WebGL2 lacks (CPU skinning expected, UNSURE); the per-camera light cap (32 on mobile and WebGL) binds.
- *Comfort:* LOW if movers stay ahead of the player and outside the walk volume; the lessons are about fairness (Portal 2 cut crushers that hit players who were not looking).
- *For Run!:* the best fit; #3's c2 LET GO is this family.

**B4 · S-STRETCH · the corridor lengthens, narrows, closes or breathes in view: the classic and its kin (15 rows: 10 indie, 5 AAA).**
- *Feels:* the far end pulls away while you look, or the walls close; the corridor is alive.
- *Strongest:* **Max Payne** (AAA, 2001; M79): the hallway stretches into a blood trail over a void (on iOS and Android since 2012). **P.T.**'s red loop (M51): "the corridor stretches out into infinity", with blurred vision and a faster walk, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.T._(video_game) . **Mouthwashing** (M80): a corridor "getting longer and longer" into "complete darkness", another that "will narrow and distort". **Spooky's Jump Scare Mansion** (M81): "the walls will gradually close in on the player until they can no longer move", https://spookys-jump-scare-mansion.fandom.com/wiki/Specimen_9 . **SCP – Containment Breach** (M12): tunnels that breathe by per-frame mesh scale (room height ±20%). **F.E.A.R.** (M82): "a long hallway will begin to close" as its lights shut off.
- *How built:* almost never documented. SCP-CB's `ScaleEntity` with a sine is the only code read; almost every case comes with darkness, blur or a slow scripted walk (`indie.md` §2.3, §2.8; Kid A Mnesia's lit atrium, M84, is the exception found), so stretched textures and moving collision are rarely inspected at speed.
- *Cost:* a vertex stretch is a few ALU a vertex, but every pass that draws the mesh (shadows, depth, SSAO, motion vectors) must apply it, culling uses the unstretched bounds, lights do not stretch; a mesh scale stretches UVs and colliders.
- *Comfort:* moderate: peripheral flow the player did not cause; a corridor that breathes with a 2.5-5 s period sits on the worst band found (§5).
- *For Run!:* Red ruled out "那种经典的隧道变远的效果" (11-04).

**B5 · S-BEND · the world curves or breaks Euclid (7 rows).**
- **Trash Dash** (Unity's runner sample; M86): vertices dropped after projection by `_CurveStrength × dist²`, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/EndlessRunnerSampleGame/master/Assets/Shaders/CurvedCode.cginc ; **Subway Surfers** (M87, UNSURE); **Animal Crossing**'s rolling log (M88); **Hyperbolica** (M89): per-vertex hyperbolic transforms and Unity's culling defeated with a huge `cullingMatrix`, https://github.com/HackerPoet/HyperEngine ; **Psychonauts**' twisted roads (M90).
- *Cost:* ALU, every material, culling. *Comfort:* low for a horizon bend. *For Run!:* no case found of a bend used to deny a goal.

**B6 · S-PORTAL · seen-through joins, a tunnel longer inside than out, two worlds at once (7 rows).**
- **Portal** (AAA, 2007; M91): stencil recursion, at most nine levels, the tail copied from the previous frame; playtesters "panicked" at a hall that looked narrower, https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary . **Non-Euclidean Worlds Engine** (M92): a short tunnel long inside, by portals with oblique clipping and a render target per level, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HackerPoet/NonEuclidean/master/NonEuclidean/Portal.cpp . **The Medium** (M93): "we are rendering two worlds at the same time", and "frames will drop". **Prey (2006)** (M94): "portal tricks such as these should be used as tricks, not as an engine paradigm".
- *Cost:* the most expensive family: another render of part of the scene per portal per recursion level; extra cameras hurt URP; on WebGL doubled draw calls meet slower CPU dispatch; on iPhone and iPad another render target is another store and load. *For Run!:* out (crossing is a teleport; an infinite view ahead is the classic).

**B7 · S-DARK · the light is taken away: darkness reshapes what can be read, or the light keys the change (19 rows: 10 AAA, 9 indie).**
- *Feels:* the way ahead is no longer there; light becomes the only map.
- *Strongest:* **Dead Space** (AAA, 2023; M95): the Intensity Director's light failures on per-area tension curves, https://www.ea.com/technology/news/inside-dead-space-4-the-intensity-director . **F.E.A.R.** (M82): a hallway's lights shut off section by section, the threat a step closer each time. **Resident Evil Village** (M96): "that's when the lights go out", https://avclub.com/resident-evil-village-s-scariest-section-isn-t-afraid-t-1846875299 . **DOORS** (indie, 2022; M98): flicker warns, then Rush passes "breaking every light" room by room, and a blue light marks the one real exit, https://doors-game.fandom.com/wiki/Rush . **NaissanceE** (M99): "keep up with the light or you will be left in darkness". **Outlast 2** (M28): "The main tool is light". **MADiSON** (M100): the end of the hallway relit "darkness and red lighting". **Alan Wake 2** (M37): the light's removal is the swap.
- *How built:* light-intensity animation on schedules or tension curves.
- *Cost:* cheap everywhere; fewer lights are cheaper; Run! B's WebGL tier culls lights beyond 10 m, so the far end reads by emissive there.
- *Comfort:* low for motion; photosafety is the risk (a stutter makes flashes, §5).
- *For Run!:* #3 itself.

**B8 · CHASE tools seen with them (32 rows).** The pursuer kept just behind the camera (P.T.'s ghost "tethered directly behind the player character", two sources: https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/camera-hack-reveals-i-pt-i-s-ghost-is-legitimately-always-just-offscreen ); a pursuer paced door to door and slower than the boosted player (DOORS' Seek, fan wiki); the runner slowed (Halo 3, Mass Effect 3, Metal Gear Solid 3, CoD4); a goal that floats away (The Sorrow, "just out of reach"); a look-back control (Outlast); reversals (Mouthwashing teleports you back if you turn; DOORS' Halt flashes "TURN AROUND"); a wave or a collapse as the chaser (Uncharted 3, The Evil Within, Assassin's Creed Unity).

### 4.1 Camera tools in URP, as numbers (computed from Unity's code, read for 74)

The code: `UberPostProcessPass.cs` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal/Runtime/Passes/PostProcess/UberPostProcessPass.cs ) turns the Lens Distortion intensity I into θ = min(160°, 1.6 × max(|100 I|, 1)) and σ = 2 tan(θ / 2), and passes `center * 2 - 1`; `UberPost.shader` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal/Shaders/PostProcessing/UberPost.shader ) runs `DistortUV` (a tan branch for I > 0, an atan branch for I < 0) on the colour sample, the two extra chromatic aberration samples and the vignette's UV, all in one fragment pass. Read on the master branch, not the 6000.6.3f1 package (the split is long-standing; not checked against that tag). Nothing below was tried on a device.

**Lens Distortion.** The image scale at the distortion centre, and how far the sample point moves elsewhere (r in 0-1 screen units from the centre; 0.5 is the middle of an edge, 0.707 a corner):

| intensity | the door at the centre | mid-field (r 0.25) | edge middles (r 0.5) | corners (r 0.707) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| −0.04 (today) | −0.1 % | samples 0.1 % farther out | 0 | samples 0.1 % nearer |
| −0.20 | −2.6 % | samples 2.0 % farther out | 0 | samples 2.5 % nearer |
| −0.30 | −5.9 % | 4.6 % | 0 | 5.1 % |
| −0.50 | −16.8 % | 13.8 % | 0 | 11.8 % |
| +0.20 | +2.7 % | 2.0 % nearer | 0 | 2.8 % farther |

- **The sign** (`build.md` §4 had it as undocumented): from the code, a negative intensity shrinks the middle of the picture and stretches the corners (pincushion-like); a positive one magnifies the middle and squeezes the edges (barrel), which is why Scale exists, to hide the border it pulls in. Lines through the distortion centre stay straight, so in a corridor seen down its axis the long edges stay straight and only the cross lines (frames, cell joints, the end wall's courses) bow.
- **The centre**: the shader centres the distortion at UV 2 × Center − 0.5, so Center 0.5 is the screen centre and a door 0.03 below it needs Center 0.485 (from the code; check on a device).
- **What −0.50 means for Run!**: the sprinter's door already shrinks 94 → 75 px (−20 %) between its nearest and farthest moments (`light_leaves_deepen.md` §1.3); a −0.50 lens on top makes it −34 %. That is a lens recede: the classic look, made by the camera.

**Chromatic aberration.** The shader offsets the green sample by a third and the blue by two thirds of `coords × |coords|² × 0.05 × intensity` (coords −1 to 1 from the screen centre). At 1920 × 1080 the blue fringe is: intensity 0.06 (today) 3.8 px at the edge middles and 7.7 px at the corners; 0.15: 9.6 and 19.2 px; 0.25: 16 and 32 px. **It is always zero at the screen centre**, so the door stays sharp while the edges fringe.

**Vignette.** From `Common.hlsl` (read for 74): the factor at a pixel is (1 − (3 I Δu)² − (3 I Δv)²)^(5 S), Δ in 0-1 screen units from the centre, the colour multiplied in. Not rounded, so it follows the screen's shape. Heights below are measured from the bottom of the screen (the UV direction was not checked on a device).

| base profile (0.26 / 0.45) | 1.00 | 0.69 | 0.69 | 0.88 | 0.69 | 0.44 |

**Field of view.** The door's on-screen size against today's 76°: 71° ×1.095 (narrowing by 5° makes the door **9.5 % bigger**); 81° ×0.915; 86° ×0.838; 91° ×0.768. Holding the door's size as a runner closes from 18 m needs 83.7° at 15.7 m, 92.3° at 13.5 m, 99.1° at 12 m, 114.8° at 9 m; the rig's 15° cap (91°) holds it for about 4.2 m, 0.76 s at a sprint. A wider FOV also streams the near walls outward: the sprint cue (Minecraft, `unreachable_technique.md` B1).

**Every picture-only recede must be paid back before the door** (this page's arithmetic). A lens or FOV that makes the door look farther has to be returned by the time the player reaches it, and the return makes the door grow faster than the approach alone: ×1.06 for a −0.30 lens, ×1.20 for −0.50. Returned during #3's runway (0.8-1.2 s, the corridor lit red), that reads as the door jumping closer. Spread over the approach it hides in the door's natural growth (19-33 % a second at the start of the approach, by whether the runner still has bar), but then the pinch stays on the lit runway. The set version (the door really is 15 m farther) never has to be paid back.

### 4.2 Custom full-screen passes and local warps

- **Full Screen Pass Renderer Feature** (Unity 6.6 manual, `build.md` T3): injection Before Transparents, Before Post-processing or After Post-processing (the default); a Fullscreen Shader Graph or blit shader; "Fetch Color Buffer" binds the frame to `_BlitTexture`. One more pass on desktop; on iPhone and iPad a warp that reads other pixels needs the frame stored and loaded again; Tile-Only Mode switches renderer features off; on WebGL one more pass with `highp` UVs.
- **A local haze or refraction in the world** (a transparent quad sampling what is behind it) needs the Opaque Texture, which is off in both URP assets: turning it on copies the opaque frame each frame (2x or 4x downsampling available), and on mobiles without StoreAndResolve "Unity ignores the Anti Aliasing (MSAA) property at runtime" (URP asset page via `build.md` N8; whether that applies to Apple GPUs was not checked: UNSURE).

### 4.3 Vertex bends and stretches

- **How:** offset positions in the vertex stage: Trash Dash drops vertices by `_CurveStrength × dist²` after projection (M86); a stretch along the run axis would be z' = z_player + (z − z_player) × s(t) for vertices ahead (a formula, not a source).
- **What it really costs** (`build.md` T4): every pass that draws the mesh must apply the same offset (shadow caster, the depth texture, SSAO's depth and normals, motion vectors), or shadows and occlusion slide; FrontRooms has the depth texture and SSAO on. Culling uses the unbent bounds (Hyperbolica replaces the culling matrix). Lights are not bent. Only materials that carry the code bend. Positions should be `highp` on WebGL. Colliders stay straight, so the drawn door and the walkable door separate and must meet again before arrival.

### 4.4 Real mesh animation (the family that fits)

- **Rigid pieces from one prime mover**: transform updates only on all four platforms; SP7's head and #3's c2 LET GO already work this way (about 90 T2 blocks, about 5.4k triangles in flight, 1-3 draws on desktop and iPad; about 28 half-course strips on iPhone; WebGL rigid, c1; `light_leaves_deepen.md` §3.c). Moving pieces lose baked lightmaps and need probes; the CharacterController "is not affected by forces and will only move when you call the Move function", so a moving wall does not push the runner (keep movers outside the walk volume, rule 8).
- **Bones and blend shapes**: Unity 6.6 GPU skinning "use[s] compute shaders"; WebGL2 has compute "Not natively supported", so expect CPU skinning there (the fallback is not documented: UNSURE).
- **Vertex animation textures** (SideFX Labs VAT 3.1): no compute; limits "texture memory budget on lower-end hardwares, the lack of animated collisions"; a WebGL route for a wall that re-stacks (vertex texture reads on WebGL2 not checked: UNSURE).
- **Runtime mesh rebuild** (Viewfinder): CPU-heavy, worst on WebGL.

### 4.5 Loops, teleports and portals (excluded by rule 11, listed for completeness)

- **Teleport by one module**: move the controller by exactly the module length with `Physics.SyncTransforms` before the next query; Cinemachine's `OnTargetObjectWarped` so the camera "also warp[s] seamlessly"; `resetHistory` for TAA; camera-only motion blur reprojects with the previous view-projection, so a jump may smear one frame (UNSURE).
- **Portals**: a stencil window (URP Render Objects with Stencil and a camera Position Offset) or a second camera into a render texture with an oblique near plane; another render per portal per level: the most expensive row on iPhone, iPad and WebGL.

### 4.6 Where any camera layer plugs in

- **One runtime Volume** with a priority above the Run! profile, holding only Vignette, Lens Distortion and Chromatic Aberration overrides, its values driven from #3's one trigger time (SP7's `RunRigState.Recede`, "one timeline from one trigger time", `72` line 159). URP blends an overridden value between the lower profile and the override by the volume's weight (standard behaviour, not re-read here), so its Lens Distortion rest value must match the live profile (−0.04 under the base profile; 0 under Run_B, which has none).
- **Comfort scaling**: multiply every lens delta by Camera Motion % (as the rig does for FOV); L1's darkening stays at every level (§6).
- **Never write `cam.fieldOfView` directly**: other code reads it live (the door check, the key HUD's flight FOV); any FOV change goes through `FrontRoomsCameraRig.PushFov` and its clamps (`build.md` T1).

## 5. Comfort, accessibility and photosensitivity: the limits, and the settings a player needs

All from `comfort.md` §3-§5 unless marked; the sources and their URLs are there. Most lab numbers come from VR or large projected displays, so read them as what the lab found, not as flat-screen limits.

### 5.1 What makes players sick, in numbers

- **Console play itself**: 42-56 % of 40 undergraduates got sick within 50 minutes of off-the-shelf console games (Stoffregen et al. 2008); 67 % of adults and 56 % of 10-year-olds (Chang et al. 2012).
- **Oscillation**: fore-and-aft optic flow made people sickest between **0.2 and 0.4 Hz** (a period of 2.5-5 s) over 0.025-1.6 Hz tested (Diels and Howarth 2013). A corridor that "breathes" on that period is the worst case found.
- **Change, not motion**: an optic flow that alternately expanded and contracted made people sicker than a steady one (Bonato et al. 2008). A dolly zoom out and back is that.
- **A second rotation axis**: pitch plus roll at 60° a second was worse than pitch alone (Bonato et al. 2009). A camera roll on top of the player's own look is that.
- **Image scale**: scales of 0.5 and 2.0 were worse than 1.0 (Draper et al. 2001, head-coupled).
- **Surprise and lost control**: unexpected self-motion was reported by 88 % of those who got sick against 31 % of those who did not; a felt loss of control by 16 of 17 sick players (Teixeira et al. 2022, HMD, small sample).
- **What helps**: narrowing the view during motion with soft-edged circular masks lowered sickness and did not lower presence, and most players did not notice the subtle version (Fernandes and Feiner 2016; the mask detail read for 74 in Columbia's release, https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160614121922.htm ); a zoom kept to the centre of the view was more comfortable (Chang and Cohen 2017); a background fixed in space lowered sickness (Duh, Parker and Furness 2001, 2004); a cleaner, less detailed world lowered it in Mirror's Edge at speed (ONE SOURCE).
- **Note the word "narrowing"**: in the comfort studies it means a mask over the edges (a vignette), not a zoom. A narrower projection magnifies the door (+9.5 % for 5°, §4.1), which works against #3.
- **Guidelines**: XAG 117: avoid shake, bob, motion blur, or give a switch; give an FOV setting; let players turn off automatic camera movement; no degrees, durations or frequencies. GAG: keep the camera in the player's control, reduce peripheral vision during movement, give a frame of reference, keep a constant horizon, avoid objects quickly moving toward, away from or past the player, avoid acceleration, keep a constant frame rate; FOV defaults of 60° for TV and 90° for a monitor, none for phones. Meta: vignettes at the screen's edges during movement, opaque and simple peripheral geometry, brief and infrequent accelerations. Nesky (GDC 2014, via a translation): a quick FOV change can cause nausea (#42), give a shake setting (#43), no roll about the camera's own axis (#27), do not change pitch, distance and FOV independently (#16).
- **Not found anywhere**: a flat-screen study of a dolly zoom, an FOV kick or a whole-frame warp; a published safe FOV change in degrees.

### 5.2 Photosensitivity, in numbers

- **A flash** (XAG 118, WCAG 2.3.1): a pair of opposing changes in luminance of 10 % or more of the maximum, the darker below 0.8. **It fails** when more than 3 happen in any second over about 20 % of the screen (XAG) or over 25 % of any 10° field, about a 341 × 256 box at 1024 × 768 (WCAG). **Saturated red** counts from R / (R + G + B) ≥ 0.8, and "People are even more sensitive to red flashing". **Patterns**: high-contrast bands over about 20 % of the screen (XAG); more than 8 static or 5 moving high-contrast stripes, or static patterns over 40 % (GAG).
- **Duration**: GAG fails any flashing sequence over 5 s; EA's IRIS fails 2-3 flashes a second held over 5 s, and a pattern held 0.5 s or more.
- **Who**: about 1 in 4,000 people (XAG 118); triggers commonly at 3-30 Hz, most likely 16-25 Hz, mostly ages 7-19; small hand-held screens are low risk but "being very close to a screen" raises it (Epilepsy Society).
- **What studios did**: Ubisoft committed in 2008 to pre-test every in-house game (whether it still stands: UNSURE); CD Projekt Red smoothed Cyberpunk 2077's braindance flash after launch (Hotfix 1.04); EA open-sourced IRIS (2023; running it is a download, so Red's OK).

### 5.3 Four tests for any effect during a 5 s sprint (`comfort.md` §5.3, restated)

1. **The view moves only as the player's own run and look explain it.** A lens move (FOV, dolly zoom, roll, whole-frame warp) fails by definition; geometry moving ahead of a true camera passes.
2. **One way, once; no pulse at 0.2-0.4 Hz; no reversal.**
3. **One rotation axis at most, and not roll.**
4. **Keep the rest frame rigid** (near walls, floor, horizon); change the centre and the far end, where the runner already looks.

And for light: no pairs of opposing 10 % changes faster than 3 a second over more than about 20 % of the screen, none at 2-3 a second for 5 s or more, no saturated red flash at those rates. And for settings: **every distortion has an off switch or a substitute**.

### 5.4 #3 and its layers against the limits (this page's arithmetic; nothing measured)

- **#3 itself** (`comfort.md` §5.4): lamps that go out and stay out are one-way changes, not flash pairs; the risk is a stutter on a red strike. The runway strikes about 7 strips a second (20 m/s over a 3 m pitch), each a one-way rise; with a stutter it would be 7 or more flash pairs a second. Passing under lit strips at a sprint is 1.8 a second (5.5 m/s over 3 m), under 3. Look B's dark pulses every 2.5 s (0.4 Hz) are safe by rate; whether a pulse train running down the corridor reads as the 0.2-0.4 Hz optic flow of Diels and Howarth is untested.
- **L1 (§6)**: a one-way darkening of the edges over 1.5 s (the side walls from 0.77 to 0.58 of their light), then a one-way release over 0.8 s at the runway, about 1.5 s later. No opposing pair inside a second, so no flash; it passes tests 1-4 (nothing moves).
- **L2 (§6)**: geometry and colour, not luminance: one kick up in 0.12 s and back over 1.0 s, once per trip. It fails test 1 mildly (the picture moves without the player's input), bends test 2 (it goes out and comes back, once, within 1.1 s: a single out-and-back, not a pulse train), and passes 3 and 4 (no roll, the centre still). Like Amnesia's hit squash and SCP-CB's capped pulse, it belongs under Camera Motion %.
- **L3 (§6)**: a sustained whole-lens warp fails test 1 for its whole length; centring it on the door (Chang and Cohen 2017) and keeping it one way help; its pay-back (§4.1) is a second change in view.
- **c2's block field** (`light_leaves_deepen.md` §3.c): about 90 like units moving is a moving regular pattern, but small: about 100 px across at 20 m, about 0.5 % of a 1080p frame and about 6 % of WCAG's 341 × 256 box (scaled to 1024 × 768), far under 20-25 %. The flash check still decides.

## 6. What fits Run! #3 THE LIGHT LEAVES (Red's pick, being deepened)

### 6.0 Where #3 already stands, in the research's terms

- **REC 离港 is built from the three space-side moves with the most support, and none of the risky ones.** The light taken away (a2 BROWNOUT, d1 CARRY, e1 SWEEP: S-DARK, as DOORS' Rush, F.E.A.R.'s hallway, Dead Space's light failures and NaissanceE's leaving light); real architecture moving inside the door's light (c2 LET GO: S-MOVE, the Observer look on the Dishonored 2 and Control build); and a corridor changed in the dark (the hidden cells revealed unlit, the true length found only when the runway lights it: the Layers of Fear and Alan Wake 2 pattern). The door's light leaving at 8 m/s (b1 GLIDE) is a far-end recede in the set, never a lens (rule 11 kept).
- **The dark is what makes #3 workable.** The indie sweep found that an in-view change of the space "almost always comes with darkness, blur or a forced slow walk", and that a lit sprint toward a visible door while the space changes has no precedent (`indie.md` §2.8); no developer source shows a first-person goal door receding in view (`build.md` §4). #3 is in new territory, and its darkness is the research's own condition for doing it.
- **What it lacks is the camera.** The treatment as built has "no camera change" (`comfort.md` §5.4). Its own named weak point is the sprinter: the door shrinks only 94 → 75 px (−20 %) at R 15, and at R 9 is never farther than at the trip; the deepening chose running in place against things that stay still (the embers, the hanging grit), not a lens (`light_leaves_deepen.md` §1.3; `72` §4.3).
- **The brief's four candidates, sorted.** Tunnel vision as the light leaves: yes (L1). A narrowing FOV: no, it magnifies the door (+9.5 % for 5°, §4.1) and is an FOV trick. The door's light through a lens warp: yes as one short kick (L2); as a sustained warp it is the classic by lens (L3, comparison only). Real model motion in silhouette: yes, it is c2 LET GO (L4). A vertex stretch of the dark corridor ahead: no (L4's note, §7).

### 6.1 The four layers

**L1 · 暗收 · THE DARK CLOSES IN (tunnel vision that follows the leaving light).** Camera family, A5. **Recommended.**
- *As the player feels it:* as the lamps ahead die, the edges of sight darken and close a little toward the door's light. The crimson walls at the sides drop to a duller red, the corners go black, the door stays as bright as ever, and the world narrows to one warm light. When the runway strikes, the edges open again with the red sweep. The fear adds one thing: it is your own eyes losing the light, not only the corridor.
- *How built:* one runtime Volume (§4.6). Vignette from Run_B's 0.32 / 0.20 to **0.38 / 0.28** over the leave front's 1.5 s, smoothstepped on the share of the strips ahead that have died, so it tracks a2 exactly; Center on the door's viewport point each frame, clamped to 0.45-0.55 so it never swings; black, as Run_B; back to 0.32 / 0.20 over the runway's sweep (0.8 s at a sprint, 1.2 s at a walk). In numbers (§4.1): the side walls keep 0.58 of their light instead of 0.77, the corners 0.27 instead of 0.54, the floor in the lower fifth 0.87 instead of 0.92, the door 1.00.
- *Cost:* none on desktop, iPhone, iPad or WebGL: the Vignette is already in the uber pass, and on iPhone and iPad it is the one effect Unity can keep in tile memory. Whether the Web tier runs post-processing at all was not checked.
- *Risk:* the red pocket at the frame's edges is "the most striking image of the five treatments" (`light_leaves_deepen.md` §1.2), and L1 dims it by a quarter; past 0.42 / 0.30 the side walls lose half their light and the pocket goes. It can read as a screen effect rather than the world. On a 19.5:9 iPhone it darkens a wider angle (it follows the screen, not the view). No flash; it passes all four tests of §5.3.
- *Research:* the comfort tool itself (soft circular masks in Fernandes and Feiner 2016; GAG's "Dynamically reducing amount of peripheral vision during movement"; Meta's vignettes; Unity's eased tunnelling vignette); in horror, Amnesia: Rebirth's tendrils (M2), Bendy's mobile ink border (M10), Halo 3's Gravemind edges (M5).

**L2 · 一颤 · THE LENS FLINCHES (one lens kick at the trip).** Camera family, A2 and A3 in the shape §5.3 allows. **Recommended as optional; Red decides.**
- *As the player feels it:* at the first clack the picture flinches once: the middle of the frame draws in toward the door a little, the corners stretch, the edges fringe red and blue, and the door itself stays sharp and nearly still; over the next second the lens settles. Once per trip, never during the glide. The far end jerked away, and so did your eye.
- *How built:* in the same Volume. Lens Distortion from its rest value (−0.04 under the base profile, 0 under Run_B) to **−0.20 in 0.12 s** at the tell (t = 0.00, the first clack), back over **1.0 s** (smoothstep); Chromatic Aberration **0.06 → 0.15 → 0.06** on the same curve; Lens Distortion's Center on the door (0.485 for a door 0.03 below the centre, §4.1); Scale unchanged (a negative value does not pull the border in). In numbers: the door −2.6 % for a moment; the mid-field shifts about 10 px at 1920 wide; the blue fringe 9.6 px at the edge middles and 19 px at the corners, zero at the door. Both deltas × Camera Motion %; at 0 the substitute is a still cue: L1's edges deepen by 0.04 for 0.3 s and return (the rule of Minecraft's green overlay: replace the warp, keep the beat).
- *Cost:* none (uber pass; two or three floats a frame).
- *Risk:* it bends the spirit of rule 11 (a camera event at the moment of the change, though the recede itself stays in the set): Red's decision. It can feel like a game effect on top of the clacks, the goal lamp's twitch, the first red light and look B's band behind. If the rig already marks the trip with its own punch in the game (not checked), L2 replaces it rather than adding to it.
- *Research:* Amnesia's squash on a sanity hit (M1), SCP-CB's zoom capped at 1.1 (M12), Halo 3's focus pulls (M5); FrontRooms' own door-push FOV dip is the same vocabulary.

**L3 · 镜头畸变（对照）· THE WHOLE-LENS WARP, for comparison only.** Red's "整个镜头变得畸变", in its least risky shape. **Not recommended.**
- *As the player feels it:* while the door glides, the middle of the picture slowly pulls inward: the door, its cloud and the last embers shrink while the walls at your sides stay; the frames and joints you pass bow slightly; during the approach the lens lets go. It is the classic "tunnel getting longer", made by the lens.
- *How built:* Lens Distortion from rest to **−0.50** across the glide (0.25 → 2.625 s at R 15), centred on the door, returned over the approach after the seat; Chromatic Aberration 0.06 → 0.20; × Camera Motion %, nothing at 0. In numbers: the sprinter's door shrinks −34 % instead of −20 %; the mid-field samples 13.8 % farther out; the pay-back is 20 % of extra growth.
- *Cost:* none (uber pass).
- *Risk:* it is the classic Red ruled out (11-04) by another means; it breaks rule 11 in spirit; it fails comfort test 1 for 2.4 s; its pay-back shows on the lit runway; players at different Camera Motion settings would see different rooms (`72` §4.3). It is here only so Red can see what a whole-lens warp would do to #3, and drop it or keep it.
- *Research:* The Last of Us Part II ships its dolly zoom behind a switch (M11); Amnesia's early hallway (M1, one fan source); no first-person game was found doing this to a goal door during a run.

**L4 · 墙在光里散开 · c2 LET GO, kept as the space answer.** Space family, B3. **Already in REC.**
- *As the player feels it:* as REC: at the clacks the wall's blocks lift out of their beds in the door's light, loosen into a lit cloud that goes with the door, and fold back into courses at the seat. The blocks that drift in front of the goal lamp read as dark silhouettes against its light: the "real model motion in silhouette" of the brief.
- *How built, cost, risk:* as `light_leaves_deepen.md` §3.c (about 90 T2 blocks posed per frame in SP7; in the game T2's pieces on desktop and iPad, half-course strips on iPhone, rigid on WebGL; spectacle can tip into magic; the flash check decides).
- *Research:* Observer's walls "breaking into shards that hang in the air" (M70) is the nearest look; Dishonored 2's one prime mover (M63) and Control's animated pieces (M64) are the builds; Quantum Break's looping debris (M68) is the nearest motion. It is what AAA does when the space itself has to change.
- *Rejected beside it: a vertex stretch of the dark corridor ahead.* In the dark the only things to see stretch are a2's embers and the door's light: stretching would spread the embers apart (the classic, exactly), push the door's light farther (doubling the glide), and would have to be undone in view before the runway lights the corridor (§4.1's pay-back); it would also need every Run! material, the depth texture and SSAO to carry the same code (§4.3).

## 7. What not to do, and why

1. **The classic stretch, by any means** (a dolly zoom, a widening FOV held on the door, a centre-weighted lens, a vertex stretch, a mesh scale of the far section, a door scaled down to look farther). Red ruled it out on 11-04 ("那种经典的隧道变远的效果"). The reasons that stand without that word: in first person a widening FOV is the stock sprint cue and reads first as "I sped up" (`unreachable_technique.md` B1); rule 11 forbids FOV tricks; the rig's 15° cap holds the door's size for 0.76 s only; any picture-only recede must be paid back in view (§4.1); a geometry stretch slides the near walls past the runner and spreads the lamps off the 3 m pitch (`72` §4.3); a scaled-down door is "an obviously miniature door at arrival" (B9).
2. **Narrowing the FOV as the light leaves.** It magnifies the door (+9.5 % for 5°), against "the light leaves", and it is an FOV trick. The comfort studies' "narrowing" is a mask over the edges: that is L1.
3. **A corridor or lens that breathes or pulses.** Fore-and-aft oscillation is worst at 0.2-0.4 Hz (Diels and Howarth 2013) and changing flow is worse than steady (Bonato 2008); SCP-CB's breathing tunnels are a pocket dimension the player wanders in, not a sprint.
4. **Camera roll or tilt during the sprint.** A second rotation axis is worse (Bonato 2009); GAG asks for a constant horizon; Nesky's #27; Uncharted 2 shakes and Eternal Darkness tilts a third-person camera, not a sprinter's eye.
5. **Motion blur, radial blur and speed lines.** XAG 117 names motion blur among the effects to avoid or put on a switch (its Halo Infinite example adds radial blur and speed lines); Unity's VR advice is to avoid motion blur; each costs its own pass on every platform; the strips rushing overhead already carry the speed.
6. **A fake crash, a glitch screen or an error screen** (P.T., Arkham Asylum, Anatomy). This page's analysis, not a sourced finding: on WebGL and on a phone a convincing fake crash can be taken for a real one and closed, and it breaks the honest machine #3 is built on (the soffit track as the tell, 71 §4.8 rule 1).
7. **Loops, teleports, stacked copies and portals.** Red's 11-03 agreement and rule 11; portals are also the most expensive family on iPhone, iPad and WebGL.
8. **Walls that close or a corridor that narrows** (Spooky's Specimen 9, Mouthwashing). Rule 8: nothing moves beside or behind the runner, nothing can slow, trip, trap or crush.
9. **A stutter or flicker on a dying or striking lamp.** A stutter turns one-way changes into flash pairs; on the runway's 7 strips a second that would fail XAG 118 and WCAG 2.3.1, worst in saturated red (§5.2). Every red change in view still goes through a Harding FPA, PEAT or IRIS capture with the bright tube (a download: Red's OK).
10. **Any camera layer with no off switch or substitute.** Every shipped precedent with documented comfort handling has one (§5.5); a layer that FrontRooms cannot switch off is the one thing all of them avoided.
11. **A full-frame warp the player did not cause, held through the sprint.** It fails comfort test 1 by definition (XAG 117's scoping question; Teixeira 2022's "unexpected vection"); if Red wants the lens to speak, it speaks once (L2), centred on the door, and settles.

## 8. Limits

- **No video was watched** by any of the five stages, so no timestamp is given, and how any precedent looks in motion is unverified. Where The Last of Us Part II's dolly zoom plays, and how strong it is, is not known.
- **Nothing was built, rendered, walked or measured.** Every FrontRooms number is arithmetic on the room's figures (walk 3.2 m/s, sprint 5.5 m/s for a 5 s bar, the trip at 9 m, R 15) or on Unity's code; the lens, aberration and vignette tables are computed from URP code read on the master branch, not the 6000.6.3f1 package, and not checked on a device (the Lens Distortion sign, its Center mapping and the vignette's vertical direction included).
- **Which post profile is live in SP7** (FrontRoomsPost or FrontRoomsPost_Run_B), whether the Web tier runs post-processing, and whether the rig already marks the trip with a punch were not checked.
- **The central gap stands**: no developer source shows a first-person game in which a goal door recedes in view while the player runs, by lens, shader or set. No developer source explains how any in-view corridor stretch was built (P.T., Mouthwashing, Layers of Fear, Spooky's, Max Payne, Arkham Asylum).

## 9. 给 Red（中文）：跑！· 第 74 页：无限长的窄走廊里，游戏怎么让镜头畸变、空间变形，又是怎么做到的

### 你问的

> "然后我认为是否可以去继续research不同的游戏，看一下在这种无限的狭长通道只能向前跑，目前有什么常见的处理手法，比如说是否能让整个镜头变得畸变，空间变形这些。以及他们都是怎么做到的（3A/独立游戏等）"

### 一句话回答

- 能做，游戏也常做。但几乎没有游戏在"玩家冲向一扇看得见的门"时去扭镜头。
- 一共整理了 106 条实例（109 个游戏、演示和工具）。分两大类：**镜头畸变**（出现在 37 条里）和**空间变形**（79 条）。数字只说明我们查到了什么，不代表所有游戏的比例。

---

### 第一类：镜头畸变（常见做法）

1. **最常见：跟着"害怕程度"变的全屏效果。** 画面波动、花屏、模糊、墨迹、边缘的黑色触手。离怪越近、理智越低，越强。独立恐怖最多：Amnesia: The Dark Descent（代码公开：理智越低，画面波动越大），SOMA，Mouthwashing，Bendy。3A 把它当作短暂的"状态"：Halo 3 的 Cortana 片段（画面变蓝、对焦乱跳、人走得很慢），Call of Duty 4 的 "Aftermath"（核爆后只能爬，画面模糊、倾斜）。
2. **真正改镜头（视野、变焦）的，都是小幅、短暂、有上限。** SCP – Containment Breach 的变焦最多 1.1 倍；Amnesia 被吓到时视野猛地一缩；Quake III 水下视野只晃 ±1 度。
3. **一直畸变的镜头，是整个游戏的"摄影机风格"，不是走廊里的事件。** Alien: Isolation（70 年代胶片和录像带感），Hellblade II（宽银幕电影镜头），Unrecord 这类执法记录仪风格（鱼眼），POOLS（录像带，iPhone 和 iPad 上也有）。
4. **画面四周变暗（隧道视野）**，是唯一一种"能减轻晕"的镜头效果：VR 研究里，移动时用柔边的圆形遮罩收窄视野，晕的人更少，沉浸感没有降，多数人甚至没注意到。
5. **电影里那种"推拉变焦"（眩晕镜头）很少见。** 有据可查的只有 The Last of Us Part II，而且它单独给了一个开关可以关掉。
- 有记录的镜头畸变，全部都有开关或滑条，或者被故意调得"比感觉最好再弱一点"（Amnesia 的设计师说他调到自己恶心，再往回收）。

几个链接：Amnesia 的代码 https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FrictionalGames/AmnesiaTheDarkDescent/master/amnesia/src/game/LuxPlayerHelpers.cpp ；The Last of Us Part II 的开关 https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/the-last-of-us-part-ii/accessibility/ ；Halo 3 https://www.halopedia.org/Cortana_moments_and_Gravemind_moments

### 第二类：空间变形（常见做法）

1. **3A 最常用：直接换场景。** Titanfall 2 的 "Effect and Cause"：过去和现在两张完整地图上下叠着，按一下就把你瞬移过去，跳在半空也行。Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart：穿过裂缝那大约 0.7 秒里才加载下一个世界。Alan Wake 2：把一个地方的灯拿走，这里就变成它的"黑暗版"。独立游戏在你背过身时换：Layers of Fear（踩到某个点、或看向某处就触发，用光或声音引你去看）。
2. **循环走廊**（Super Mario 64 的无尽楼梯、P.T.、The Exit 8）：最便宜、最多，但 11 月 3 日我们已经排除了。
3. **让建筑真的动起来**，这是 3A 对"空间本身在变"的回答。Dishonored 2 的发条宅邸：一个动力源带动整栋房子，敌人在房间移动时也能正常走。Control 的烟灰缸迷宫：一块块做好动画的墙，跟着音乐开合翻转。Portal 2：面板只在"确定玩家站在哪里"的地方大动。独立游戏里 Observer 的墙会碎成悬在空中的碎片。
4. **把灯拿走。** Dead Space 重制版按紧张程度安排灯坏；F.E.A.R. 的走廊一段一段黑下去；DOORS 里 Rush 经过时一间一间把灯打碎；NaissanceE："跟上光，不然就留在黑暗里"。
5. **走廊当着你的面拉长、变窄（就是经典的"隧道变远"）**：少，大多是独立游戏，而且几乎都配着黑暗、模糊或强制慢走：P.T. 的红灯那一轮、Mouthwashing、Spooky's 里墙一点点合拢。**没有任何开发者公开过是怎么做的。**
6. **弯曲和传送门**：跑酷游戏（Unity 官方示例 Trash Dash 用着色器把路往下弯）、解谜游戏（Portal、Manifold Garden）。传送门最费性能。

### 他们是怎么做到的（白话）

- 镜头效果：每一帧改一两个数字，在画面最后的处理里完成，几乎不花性能。
- 空间效果：复制一份场景再瞬移；或者一整块一整块的模型由一个"动力源"带着动；或者按时间表关灯。
- 当面变形、又有做法记录的只有三个：SCP-CB 每帧缩放模型，Observer 让显卡做顶点动画，Quantum Break 用波浪系统推动几何体。

### 对我们第 3 种「光走了」意味着什么

- 「离港」已经用了研究里最站得住的三招：灯被拿走；门的光里墙在动（砖散开又砌回）；在黑暗里改了走廊（隐藏的那几格是黑着出现的）。它没有用任何镜头把戏，符合公平规则第 11 条（不剪切、不瞬移、不玩视野把戏）。
- 研究也说明：当面改变空间，几乎都要靠黑暗来遮。所以「光走了」的黑，正是它能成立的条件。
- 它缺的是**镜头**这一层。还有一个它自己的弱点：冲刺的人看门，只缩小了五分之一。

### 四层可以叠上去的东西

**L1 暗收（推荐，先做）**
- 感受：前面的灯一盏盏死去时，你视野的四周也轻轻暗下来，向门收拢；两边的红墙暗一点，四角变黑，门一样亮；世界缩成一盏暖光。红色跑道亮起时，四周再打开。多出来的怕：是你自己的眼睛在失去光。
- 数字：两侧墙的亮度从 77% 降到 58%，四角从 54% 到 27%，门始终 100%。
- 成本：零。电脑、iPhone、iPad、网页版都一样。
- 风险：会压暗两边那圈红光（影片里最好看的画面），所以只能轻轻地；再强一点，红光就没了。不会闪，不会晕；它本身就是减轻晕的方法。

**L2 一颤（可选，你来定）**
- 感受：跨过门框、第一声"咔"的那一刻，画面抖一下：中间往门那边收一点，四角拉开一点，四周有一点红蓝色边；门本身清楚、几乎不动；一秒内恢复。只有这一下，门滑走的过程中不再动。
- 跟着游戏里的"镜头运动 %"走；设为 0 时，换成四周短暂暗一下（不动画面，只变暗）。
- 成本：零。
- 风险：可能像"游戏特效"，叠在咔声、灯一抖、第一盏红灯上面会不会太多，要看了才知道；它碰到第 11 条规则的边（画面在那一刻动了，虽然门的后退还是在场景里）。

**L3 整个镜头畸变（只做对照，不推荐）**
- 感受：门滑走时，画面中间慢慢往里缩，门显得更远；冲刺时门的缩小从五分之一变成三分之一。
- 但：这就是你 11 月 4 日说不要的"经典隧道变远"，只是改用镜头来做；最后必须"还回去"，还的时候门会显得突然变近；画面有 2.4 秒不是玩家自己造成的运动，最容易晕；不同设置的玩家会看到不一样的房间。做出来只是让你亲眼看一下，再决定扔掉。

**L4 墙在光里散开（已经在「离港」里）**
- 这就是"空间变形"在我们这里最好的答案，也是 3A 的路子（Dishonored 2、Control；样子最像 Observer 的悬浮碎片）。挡在门灯前面的砖会变成黑色剪影，这就是"模型在剪影里动"。
- 不建议的：把黑着的那段走廊"拉长"。黑里能看到的只有余火和门的光，一拉，余火的间距变大，就是经典效果；而且红色跑道亮起前必须当着你的面变回来。

### 场景 4 先做什么

- **先做 L1 和 L2**，作为 N 键里的新一项「镜头」：g0 不加（现在的样子）／g1 暗收／g2 暗收 + 一颤／g3 整个镜头畸变（只对照）。
- 不加新的渲染步骤，电脑、iPhone、iPad、网页版的时间和效果都一样。
- 做好后给你看：冲刺和走路两段影片，g0、g1、g2 并排；g3 单独一段对照；再加 5 张关键时刻的截图。

### 风险

- **晕。** 研究里，普通主机游戏玩 50 分钟就有 42% 到 67% 的人会晕。最容易晕的是：不是自己造成的画面运动；每 2.5 到 5 秒来回一次的"呼吸"；画面翻滚（左右歪）；视野一会儿大一会儿小。所以规则是：只动一次、只往一个方向、不翻滚、两边的墙和地板不动、变化放在门那里。L1 完全符合；L2 只差一点（画面在那一刻自己动了一下）；L3 不符合。
- **闪。** 一秒超过 3 次、占屏幕五分之一以上的明暗变化有危险，红色更危险。灯灭了就不再亮，不算闪；会出问题的是灯"一闪一闪"地亮或灭。红色跑道每秒点亮约 7 盏，只要每一盏不抖就没事。上线前每个红色变化都要用检测工具测一次（工具要下载，需要你同意）。
- **设置。** 现有的"镜头运动 %"和"减少闪烁"已经够 L1、L2 用。还缺两个：一个"全屏效果"开关，一个"视野"滑条（交给系统设计决定）。

### 怎么回

- "先做 L1 + L2" → 在场景 4 的 N 键里加「镜头」这一项，做好给你看影片。
- "也做 L3 对照" → 顺便加 g3，你亲眼看整个镜头畸变是什么样子。
- "镜头都不要" → 「离港」保持原样，只做空间那一层（墙在光里散开）。
- 或者直接说你想要的感觉。
