## 0. Short answer

1. **Why the levels feel flat.** Our skeleton is Archizoom's *No-Stop City* (1969–71) with wallpaper: a potentially endless interior under low white ceiling modules and columns, lit and ventilated artificially, with no hierarchy [LS35]. Burke (1757) names the effect, "succession and uniformity of parts … constitute the artificial infinite", and also its limit: of length, height and depth, "the length strikes least … we are more struck at looking down from a precipice, than looking up at an object of equal height" [LS34]. The endless horizontal is the weakest kind of vastness.
2. **The fix: keep the uniform field as the ground, and cut rare, strong vertical events into it, above all depth.** Uniformity is what makes the event count. Burke again: every change "checks" the infinite, so the checks must be few and large.
3. **The cross-level skeleton: the well.** One void through the whole building at a fixed plan position, from Level 0 down to the pool. From Level 0's rail you look down six floors to turquoise light; every floor below has its own balcony on the same void; the pool hall is its floor (§3). One landmark for the whole game, one loop round it, and the descent made visible before you take it.
4. **Seven devices, each made of 1990 building parts** (§2): the cut (a floor opened to the floor below), the drop (pits, split levels), the stack (atria with floors above and below in view), the tall void (rooms three to five storeys high in building-standard finishes), the bridge (catwalks and galleries over voids), the ramp (the floor itself slopes), and the moving structure (deep floors only).
5. **Per level** (§4, stills in §6): Level 0 gets the cut, the drop, the tall grid and the well's rail; Office gets the stacked atrium and the sunken bullpen (prospect from the edge, refuge in the pit, with 10's panel heights); Run! gets the ramp down, the catwalk over the plant room and a section that tightens; Pool (main K chat) gets depth seen but not entered, terraced basins, and the flooded bottom of the well.
6. **Drama must stay legible** (§5): Arkane cut its Clockwork Mansion's "impossible maze" because mastery was impossible, and kept every backstage area in view of the big spaces so players hold a mental map [LS37]. Voids are always guarded, darkness is never cover, the Relay reads across a void, and the stairwell stays the honest safe space.
7. **Cheapest first** (§7): "view-only" verticality (voids you see but never enter: the well, the cut, the atrium, the tall grid) needs no new Relay navigation. "Walkable" verticality (pits, ramps, catwalks) extends the walkable-stair contract (`stairwell/60`).

## 1. The ground and its checks

### 1.1 The endless horizontal has a history

- **No-Stop City** (Archizoom Associati, 1969–71) [LS35]: "The factory and the supermarket become the specimen models of the future city: optimal urban structures, potentially limitless … made uniform by a system of micro-acclimatization and optimal circulation of information." Its interior models show "low hanging white ceiling modules held up by shiny metal columns", spaces that "seem to go on endlessly". Branzi: "the house becomes a well equipped parking lot."
- That is Level 0 today: a lay-in grid, columns on a 6 m grid, one lamp per cell, no hierarchy, potentially endless. The Backrooms canon inherited it; FrontRooms does not have to stop there.

### 1.2 Why it is weak, and how to exceed it

Burke, *A Philosophical Enquiry* (1757), Part II [LS34]:
- **Sect. IX, succession and uniformity:** repetition of uniform parts makes the mind run on "beyond their actual limits": the artificial infinite. Any change in the parts "checks" it.
- **Sect. VII, vastness:** "Extension is either in length, height, or depth. Of these the length strikes least; an hundred yards of even ground will never work such an effect as a tower an hundred yards high … height is less grand than depth; and … we are more struck at looking down from a precipice, than looking up at an object of equal height."

Read together: the uniform horizontal field is a good ground and a weak climax. The climax is vertical, and depth beats height. So each level keeps its uniform field and spends its drama on a few vertical checks that the field makes visible.

### 1.3 Precedents for the vertical check

| Precedent | What it does | For us |
|---|---|---|
| Piranesi, *Carceri d'invenzione* (1749–50; 2nd ed. 1761, 16 plates) [LS33] | Colossal interiors of stacked stairs, bridges and galleries; perspective drawn from several viewpoints at once, so the space cannot be assembled | the bridge and the stair as the drama; space that reads as continuous but cannot be mapped (deep floors only) |
| OMA, Très Grande Bibliothèque (1989) [LS36] | The building as a solid block of storage with public voids "carved out": "absence floating in memory" | voids carved out of the uniform field (the well, the cut) |
| OMA, Jussieu libraries (1992) [LS36] | Floors cut and bent so each connects to the next: one continuous trajectory, about 1.5 km, a double helix of ramps | the ramp: the descent as a continuous floor (Run!) |
| Arkane, *Dishonored 2*, the Clockwork Mansion (2016) [LS37] | Rooms transform slowly on levers; play happens during the transition; an early "impossible maze" version was cut because the player could not master it; backstage areas stay visually connected to the big spaces | the moving structure, with its legibility rule |
| Escher, *House of Stairs* (1951), *Relativity* (1953), *Ascending and Descending* (1960) [LS38] | Stairs that connect incompatible levels and loop | the impossible connection, used once per deep floor at most |
| Portman's atria (Hyatt Regency Atlanta, 1967) and the Bonaventure (1976; `10` [LS6]) | The atrium as spectacle and disorientation | the stack (Office) and the well |
| Frank Lloyd Wright, Larkin Administration Building (1906); the Bradbury Building (1893) in *Blade Runner* (1982) [LS39] | An office organised round a top-lit court with tiers of galleries | the office atrium as the period's own dramatic type |
| Terry Gilliam, *Brazil* (1985) [LS39] | Ducts invading every room and corridor | the services layer as the room (Run!, the plenum) |
| *Dark Souls* (2011), *Control* (2019), *Silent Hill 2* (2001) [LS40] | A world knotted vertically with shortcuts; sectors stacked in a building that shifts; a staircase that goes down too long | vertical connection and the long descent as story |

## 3. The well: one void through the building

- **What:** a 9 × 9 m void (3 × 3 cells) at a fixed world position under every floor, from Level 0 to the pool hall. Every floor has a balcony on it, guarded at 1.07 m (the period 42 in guard). Its floor is the pool hall: turquoise water about 30 m below Level 0 (D1 c puts the club and the pool level on B2 and beyond; the well may go deeper than the floors the player walks, which is the building lying).
- **Why:** Burke's depth [LS34]; the Très Grande Bibliothèque's carved void [LS36]; the atrium as the period's dramatic type [LS39]. One landmark seen from every floor (Lynch's landmark at game scale, 10 [LS8]); one loop round it on every floor, so the Relay can be met across it and escaped round it (Alien: Isolation's loops, 10 [LS30]); and the descent shown before it is taken: from Level 0 you already see where the pool is.
- **How it reads per floor:** Level 0 looks down into it (prospect, anticipation); the middle floors look across and down; the floors near the bottom look up at the lit rings above (memory); the pool level stands in its light.
- **Build:** the map reserves the void cells on every floor (a void cell: no floor, no ceiling, guarded edges; the Relay never enters it; sight and sound cross it). Only one floor is loaded at a time, so the view down is a stand-in shaft: repeated balcony rings, fogged, with the pool glow at the bottom. Owners: 关卡设计 (cells), 游戏视觉 (the shaft view), the pool line (the bottom).
- **Stills:** `c1_well_approach`, `c2_well_down`.

## 4. Per level

### 4.1 Level 0 (space plan)
- **V1 the cut** (`a1_open_to_below`, `a2_section_down`): a 2 × 2-cell opening in a large suite; from the rail you see the next floor's carpet 4 m down, through a cut that shows carpet, slab and plenum. It previews the floor you will reach by STAIR A.
- **V2 the drop** (`d1_sunken_suite`, `d2_from_the_pit`): a suite sunk a full storey (2.4 m, fourteen risers) with a dais at its far end; the ceiling stays at 2.9 m above the corridor, so the suite is 5.3 m tall and its steps read as tiers. The corridor edge becomes a stage. (A first try at 1.2 m read as a conversation pit, too mild.)
- **V4 the tall grid** (`b1_corridor_to_tall`, `b2_tall_looking_up`): a 2.9 m corridor opens through a 2.2 m arch into a room 14.5 m high, the same paper all the way up, the grid and troffers five storeys overhead. From inside, the doorway you came through is a slot at the foot of the wall. The banal kit at the wrong scale is the drama, not a new material.
- **The plenum** (`a3_plenum_soffit`, 10 L0-3) stays as the small-scale version of the cut.

### 4.2 Office (stuff)
- **V3 the stack** (`e1_office_atrium`, `e2_office_atrium_up`): an atrium through five office floors with glass guards and panels at every edge; you see copies of your floor above and below. From the atrium floor, four floors of balconies rise to a laylight.
- **V2 the sunken bullpen** (`f1_bullpen_from_edge_165`, `f2`–`f5`): the cubicle field sinks 1.2 m below a perimeter walkway. From the walkway you see over every panel (prospect); in the pit the panel height decides everything (10 OF-1: 1.35 / 1.52 / 1.65 / 2.16 m, with the Relay stand-in at the far aisle crossing).

### 4.3 Run! (services)
- **V6 the ramp** (`h1_ramp_down_armed`, `h2_ramp_down_tripped`): the spine as a 1:8 ramp, 24 m long and 3 m down, ending at the goal door with a tall bright room behind it. After the trip only battery heads remain, and the goal door is the brightest thing (Run! rule 5).
- **V5 the bridge** (`g1_catwalk`, `g2_catwalk_from_below`): the chase crosses a three-storey plant room on a grating catwalk, with chillers, risers and mains around it; the Relay stands on the floor 6 m below.
- **The section tightens** (`i1`–`i3`, 10 RN-2): on the real Run! B rig, services hang to 2.45 m clear and fire-door walls cut the corridor every 9 m.

### 4.4 Pool (structure and site): for the main K chat
The pool belongs to the main K chat (18's M1–M7 and F1 are Red's picks). Vertical devices that fit 18 and D6 (wading only):
- **Depth seen, not entered:** a diving well 4–5 m deep under a diving tower, guarded, its water dark below the wading shelf (D6 keeps the player wading).
- **Terraced basins:** the pool level steps down in weirs, water falling from basin to basin; the descent is made of water (18 M7).
- **The flooded bottom of the well:** the pool hall is the well's floor (§3), so the player arrives in the light they saw from Level 0.
- **The slide tower:** a stair tower and a slide tube that pierce walls and floors (18 M7).

## 5. Rules that keep drama legible

1. **One vertical event per zone, not everywhere.** The uniform field is the ground; the event is the check [LS34].
2. **Mastery, not antagonism.** Every void, tall room and backstage space stays in view of a big space, so the mental map survives (Clockwork Mansion [LS37]). Impossible connections (Escher) only on deep floors, at most one per floor.
3. **Voids are guarded.** A 1.07 m guard at every edge; no falling, no climbing over.
4. **The Relay reads across voids.** Sight crosses the well, the cut and the atrium; darkness at the bottom never hides it (COMMON rule 12). It never enters a void cell.
5. **The stairwell stays honest.** Its drama is its arrival (10 J1): the far door can open onto a balcony over the new floor.
6. **No literal craziness.** No glitches, no warping textures, no impossible materials: the structure carries it, built from the period kit.
