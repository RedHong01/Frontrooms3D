**Yes: cyan water should tint the room, strongly wherever a surface is lit only from below.** Under downlights a white ceiling over 1.2 m of water on white tile should read about 0.55 : 0.87 : 1 (R : G : B) in a club hall and 0.39 : 0.83 : 1 over a flooded office, while ankle water moves it only to about 0.9 : 0.99 : 1 [EST]. FrontRooms cannot show this in the map today, and the same cause explains "漫反射和反射的颜色倾向并不对": each room's colour comes from three sources that never exchange information, namely one constant Trilight per zone (a single warm set shared by Level 0, the Office, the map's Run! rooms and the title corridor, blind to lamps and surfaces), one frozen reflection cube per zone (the Level 0 one captured on 2026-10-03 in a single cell, capped at 0.5, never box-projected), and a warm-on-warm grade, while the term that would carry the pool's colour onto the ceiling is built but switched off in the map's pool zones [F06, F10, F13, F20]. Shipped games and architectural practice agree on the remedy. In a troffer room every surface except the floor is lit mostly by bounce, so a bounce carrying the room's own, more saturated colour *is* the room's colour; real lenses sit 35-90 times above the carpet they light [EST, from measured photometry], against about 1.25 times measured in FrontRooms' pool harness; reflections stay honest through small local probes normalised to the diffuse light; shafts appear only where a compact bright source meets dark air; and interior windows act as borrowed light and night mirrors. The plan below ranks nine steps. The first four are to switch on the built water term in the map (measured at +0.9 ms at 1440p on the M3 Max), give each room its own bounce colour driven by live lamp power (about 0 ms on every tier [EST]), lock each lens to its light and fix exposure per zone (Red's decision, since Red chose lens 1.8), and make reflections local, lamp-true and normalised. All four are shading or data changes that run on iPhone, iPad and WebGL2; froxel fog, ray-traced water and dynamic GI stay desktop extras for later.

## Cyan water should tint the ceiling, and FrontRooms' three colour sources never talk to each other

Under overhead lamps a pool behaves like a large floor that is dimmer and cyan. Flat water reflects only **2.04 % of the light that meets it straight on** (2.14 % at 30°, 13.4 % at 70°, 34.8 % at 80°, for n = 1.333) ([S01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_equations)) [EST, computed from the Fresnel equations], so the diffuse light that a white basin sends back up comes from the tile under the water, filtered on the way down and again on the way up. Light rising from the tile is turned back down 47.5 % of the time, total internal reflection included, which is exactly the game's own `Trapping` constant of 0.475; the game's `PathFactor` of 2.4 equals the down-and-up path through the 48.6° escape cone (2 × 1.20) [EST; F34]. With the game's absorption the pool returns, relative to a dry white floor of albedo 0.8, **75 / 84 / 86 % (R / G / B) at 0.12 m and 29 / 70 / 86 % at 1.2 m** [EST]. A ceiling under downlights receives almost no direct light, so its colour is that return weighted by the share of its view the water fills: a view factor of about 0.65-0.67 for an 8 × 16 m club pool 4 m below, 0.66 for a 6 × 6 m Poolrooms basin in a 2.4 m room, and 0.96 for a flooded office floor [EST]. The same integrating-sphere arithmetic, bounce per channel proportional to first-reflected flux divided by (1 − mean reflectance) ([S02](https://iadpython.readthedocs.io/en/stable/sphere-basics.html)), gives the colour of every bounce-lit surface in FrontRooms' two dry zones.

The Level 0 and Office rows are this report's arithmetic: the relation above, the 72.5 % floor / 27.5 % wall first-hit split computed for a 12 × 12 × 2.9 m room on a 3 m troffer grid from the measured candela table of a period lensed troffer ([S03](https://webtools.cooperlighting.com/Public/files/ies/instabase/METALUX/RECESSED%20STATIC/G/2%20X%204/2G-240A125.pdf)), the texture means this line's audit measured by downsampling the base maps (wallpaper from a 2026-10-08 raster measurement) [F00, F38], and the zone lamp colours [F09]. The GI note's own model, with assumed albedos, gave a Level 0 ceiling of red/blue 6.1 and a lamp-facing white card of 1.65 [F00]; the measured means land within about 20 % of its assumed albedos, so the conclusion does not hang on them.

Two physical facts shape how the tint should look. The eye white-balances a uniform cast away, so the cyan reads through gradients: the ceiling over the water against the ceiling over the dry deck, the lower wall courses against the upper ones, which is where the game's water term already puts it [F34]. And a troffer blurs any ripple pattern over about 0.9 m at a height of 2.4 m, so under troffers the water adds only a broad 3-6 % sheen to the ceiling; moving light on a ceiling (波光粼粼) needs a compact source over the water or lamps under it [EST]. The yellow-room row shows the other half of Red's point: the bounce is more saturated than the wallpaper itself (blue/red 0.20 against the paper's 0.28) [EST], so in a real Level 0 room the strongest yellow sits on the ceiling, in the corners and on shadowed sides, while a white card facing a lamp keeps close to the lamp's colour.

FrontRooms' current stack produces none of this in the map. **Diffuse:** FrontRooms/Surface, on 83 of 86 surface materials, takes its indirect light from URP's `SampleSH` alone, per pixel, with no lightmap, probe-volume or screen-space term [F05, F23]. The SH is a Trilight whose Level 0 set, sky (.20, .19, .15), equator (.26, .24, .17) and ground (.40, .36, .24) in gamma, or linear luminance 0.030 / 0.047 / 0.108, also serves the Office (which has a grade volume but no ambient of its own), the map's Run! rooms and the title corridor; it stays constant whatever the lamps do, because the map never calls `FrontRoomsZoneLook.SetLampShare` [F06, F08, F09]. Only the four pool zones have their own cool sets [F08]. A cool grey-green fill, colour (0.769, 0.816, 0.800) at intensity 0.16, lights every surface without a shadow [F07]. **Reflection:** seven baked 256 px HDR cubes, one per zone; the Level 0 cube was captured on 2026-10-03 in one cell with static wallpaper, before the L03 troffer (2026-10-31) and the surface upgrade of 2026-11-05, is capped at 0.5 linear, and is sampled with neither box projection nor blending, because both URP assets ship those flags at 0 and FrontRooms/Surface does not declare the keywords [F01, F05, F10, F11]. **Grade:** ACES with a fixed post-exposure of +0.15, white balance +9 / −7, a warm bloom tint (1, .93, .78), a warm vignette, lifted blacks (lift w .035), contrast −6 and saturation −8 [F13]. **Water:** the term that makes a room take its water's light has existed since 2026-11-04 but is off by default; only the title stream's pool rooms and key I in the structures scene switch it on, so in the map's pool zones "the water's colour stops at the coping" [F19, F20, F21].

Set against the physics, Red's complaint has five concrete mechanisms (inference from the facts above). First, the ambient is identical in every room of a zone, so a yellow room, a blue-grey Office, a red Run! strip and a room whose lamps died all share one indirect colour; the Office inherits a yellow ambient where its own surfaces and cool lamps would return an almost neutral bounce. Second, the ambient is **about half as saturated as the physics**, with red/blue of about 2.8 on the ground term, 2.2 on the equator and 1.7 on the sky in linear light against about 5 for a Level 0 bounce [EST, from the values in F06], and steeper across directions (ground 3.6 times sky) than a downlit room, whose bounce arrives within about 1.5 : 1 from every direction (§2) [EST]. Third, the warm cast is applied by the grade to lit and shadowed pixels alike, the reverse of physics, where direct light keeps the lamp's colour and the saturated colour lives in the bounce. Fourth, the reflections are a dim, warm, generic smear: a cube from another cell and an older look (captured before the L03 kit), at half strength, without parallax, and lit by lenses far dimmer than real ones (the only measured ratio, in the pool harness, is about 1.25 times the floor [F21]; Level 0's has never been measured), so it holds no bright white source, whereas a dielectric reflection takes the colour of what it reflects (F0 of 2-5 %, achromatic by the standard metal/roughness convention, ([S04](https://www.adobe.com/learn/substance-3d-designer/web/the-pbr-guide-part-2))) and should show near-white lens streaks over a saturated room. Fifth, the cool fill adds a cast that nothing in the frame explains. The water is the same disconnection at its sharpest: the pool's colour never reaches the ambient, the cubes or the ceiling.

## Real troffer rooms put the lens 35-90 times above the carpet and light everything else by bounce

Architectural lighting judges a room by the luminance of its surfaces, not by desk lux. Richard Kelly's three layers, ambient luminescence, focal glow and the play of brilliants, remain the design vocabulary ([S05](https://www.architectmagazine.com/projects/richard-kellys-three-tenets-of-lighting-design_o), search summary); Flynn's office tests with 96 observers found that pleasant installations scored toward peripheral and non-uniform light ([S06](https://www.ecmag.com/magazine/articles/article-detail/lighting-psychology-light), search summary); Cuttle's mean room surface exitance calls a room dim near 30 lm/m², acceptably bright from about 100 and bright near 300 ([S07](https://leducation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/LEDucation2020-Luminance-and-Exitance.pdf)). Office guidance turns this into minima for walls and ceilings: CIBSE LG7 asks walls for at least 50 % and ceilings for at least 30 % of the task illuminance (20 % where ceilings are low), reports minimum-only downlight schemes as gloomy, and wants ceilings at reflectance 0.6 or more, walls at 0.3-0.7 and the floor cavity at 0.2 or less ([S08](https://www.building.co.uk/office-lighting/3044112.article)); IES RP-1 allows 3 : 1 between a task and its surround and 10 : 1 elsewhere in view, with 30-35 fc as the usual ambient goal ([S09](https://www.ecmweb.com/content/making-office-lighting-work)). The period's 2 × 4 lensed troffer explains the Backrooms look in these terms. A measured Metalux 2G-240A125, two 34 W F40 cool-white lamps behind a prismatic acrylic lens, emits 4,281 lm with 1,588 cd straight down, and its lens averages **2,502 cd/m² at nadir and 1,035-1,114 cd/m² at 75°**, the angle at which a player sees the next lamps ([S03](https://webtools.cooperlighting.com/Public/files/ies/instabase/METALUX/RECESSED%20STATIC/G/2%20X%204/2G-240A125.pdf)).

Summed point by point over a 4 × 4 grid on a 3 m pitch in a 12 × 12 m room with a 2.9 m ceiling (FrontRooms' Level 0 and Office cell), that fixture lights the floor almost perfectly evenly (429-440 lx, max/min 1.03), falls to 57 % 0.3 m from a wall and 34 % in a corner, puts 72.5 % of its flux on the floor, 27.5 % on the walls and none on the ceiling, draws a 2 : 1 scallop on the upper walls peaking about 1.4 m below the ceiling with a dark band right under it, and leaves the ceiling lit by bounce alone at about 20 % of the floor's illuminance, LG7's figure for low ceilings [EST]. The lens then sits **about 90 times above a carpet of reflectance 0.2 seen from straight below and 37-54 times at 75-85°**, and 13-40 times over light vinyl; the closed form, 17.4 / ρ_floor for this fixture and grid, does not depend on lamp count, ageing or dirt [EST]. A dead lamp leaves its floor at 57-64 % of the healthy level: it reads through its dark lens and its missing wall scallop, not as a black patch [EST]. The only lens-to-floor ratio measured in FrontRooms, in the pool harness, is about 1.25 [F21]. The checks that follow from this are concrete enough to run in a capture:

URP 17.6 supplies none of the physical tools that make these rules automatic. It has no physical light units, no realtime area lights (Rectangle and Disc lights are forced to Baked) and no automatic exposure, and its IES importer generates cookies but leaves intensity and assignment to the user [F36, F37]. Its punctual falloff is 1/d² times a smoothing window (1 − (d/r)⁴)², and its direct diffuse carries no 1/π, so one URP intensity unit stands for I_cd/π, which is where the π in the lens rule comes from [F36] [EST]. A plain point light radiates upward and over-lights the upper walls 4-20 times at 65-85°, which erases the scallop and the dark band and lifts the ceiling; FrontRooms' 162° map spots are closer to a troffer [EST; F09]. In the map the lens and its Light share only the lamp's level (flicker, dim, dead): the lens colour comes from the zone's lens material and the Light's colour from the zone theme, and the lens drive of 1.8 "as passed (gamma)" is about 3.6-4.1 in linear light, against roughly 25 that the lens rule gives for Level 0's intensity of 5 on a 2 × 4 opening, or 12-15 with the view-angle factor [F09, F12] [EST]. Shadow softness follows fixture size: under a 2 × 4 lens at 2.9 m a desk casts a 0.19-0.40 m penumbra while objects 0.1 m tall stay contact-sharp at 2-4 cm, so broad soft shadows plus SSAO suit URP's fixed filter kernel [EST]. Game flicker descends from Quake's light styles, ten characters per second where 'a' is off and 'm' normal, the fluorescent preset being mmamammmmammamamaaamammma ([S11](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2021/06/15/valve-flickering-lights/)); whatever the pattern, one value per fixture should drive its light, lens and ceiling glow so they never disagree [EST].

Shipped interior games add discipline rather than physics. Naughty Dog's Gabe Betancourt starts from the key light, makes everything support it, and deletes lights to rebuild from the key when the direction muddies ([S12](https://80.lv/articles/learning-lighting-for-video-games/)); Vivian Ding's GDC 2014 talk names dominant directionality, ambient shadows and flashlight bounce as the three techniques developed for The Last of Us ([S13](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020475/In-Game-and-Cinematic-Lighting)); Remedy left Control's walls bare instead of adding stains, cables and pipes, and trusted the lighting to give them life, in world design director Stuart Macdonald's account ([S14](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-remedy-harnessed-the-ethereal-beauty-of-brutalism-to-shape-i-control-i-)); Motive's Dead Space remake treated the light fixture as a developed system, with lighting behaviour driven by the Intensity Director and graded through ACES ([S15](https://gdcvault.com/play/1029020/-Dead-Space-Harnessing-the)); Cloud Imperium's Maria Yue sets exposure and tone mapping early, locks them once materials and the key are in, and pushes bounce saturation where needed ([S16](https://80.lv/articles/lighting-environments-tips-and-tricks)). Severance's windowless Lumon office was lit by about 90 Arri S60 SkyPanels ([S17](https://awardsdaily.com/?p=176012)), and the Backrooms photograph itself, taken in 2002 in a former furniture store in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, shows yellow wallpaper, carpet and fluorescent light ([S18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms)). Read together (inference): a troffer grid has no key light, so direction comes from the wall scallop and its dark band, from one focal source per space (an exit sign, a lit end wall, a doorway glow) and from the broken symmetry of dead lamps; refinement comes from Kelly's other two layers, from white lens streaks in glossy surfaces and from contact detail, not from more lamps or floor pools.

Materials set the stage for all of it. Acoustic ceiling tile measures 0.79-0.88 light reflectance (Armstrong data, [S19](https://sweets.construction.com/manufacturer/armstrong-world-industries--inc-nst3824/products/techzone-with-optima--1410---acoustical-ceiling-tile-nst584346-p), search summary); porcelain is 0.745 and concrete 0.51 in linear albedo ([S20](https://api.physicallybased.info/materials)); dielectric base colours belong between about 30-50 and 240 sRGB ([S04](https://www.adobe.com/learn/substance-3d-designer/web/the-pbr-guide-part-2)). The texture means this line's audit measured are plausible for Level 0 and the Office (loop carpet 0.196, Office carpet 0.101, ceiling tiles 0.551-0.591, drywall 0.427 in linear luminance) and near-white for the pool field tile (≈0.88, ≈0.99 with its tone) and the Run! hospital wall (0.738) [F00]. Near-white surfaces under downward lamps give bright floors under dim ceilings, which is the pool harness's "ankle water lit about ten times as bright as the ceiling" [F21]. URP multiplies the environment reflection by the occlusion term and nothing else, so crevices need AO in the mask map or the probe's bright lens shines where no lens is visible [F36].

## Colour bleeding in a recycled maze has to be data that follows the lamps

Only three families of realtime GI follow both moving geometry and changing lamps, and all three need compute or ray tracing. Ray-traced probe grids (DDGI) cost 1-2 ms on an RTX 2080 Ti at 1080p and lag indirect light by about 100 ms at the recommended 97 % hysteresis; their per-probe distance data is what stops light leaking through walls ([S21](https://morgan3d.github.io/articles/2019-04-01-ddgi/overview.html)). Surface-cache tracing (Lumen) costs about 8 ms for GI plus reflections at 1080p on current consoles at Epic's 30 fps indoor scalability, asks for walls at least 10 cm thick, does not work with forward shading, and lets global lighting changes take several seconds to propagate ([S22](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine); [S23](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-global-illumination-and-reflections-in-unreal-engine)). Unity's own Surface Cache GI is compiled out of URP 17.6 behind `#if SURFACE_CACHE` and planned for Unity 6.7, with a compute fallback on Metal (Unity has no hardware ray tracing there), procedural content as a core case, slower reaction to light changes and diffuse only ([F36]; [S24](https://discussions.unity.com/t/surface-cache-gi-preview/1720494)). Voxel and propagation volumes ran on older consoles: Kingdom Come: Deliverance's SVOGI with about a 6 % hit, a one-to-two-frame bounce delay and leaks ([S25](https://wccftech.com/kingdom-come-deliverance-gets-svogi-onoff-comparison-performance-hit-is-minimal/), search summary), Crytek's light propagation volumes inside about 3.3 ms on Xbox 360 ([S26](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2009/Light_Propagation_Volumes.pdf), search summary). Everything cheaper precomputes against static geometry. Enlighten precomputes patch visibility, takes several frames to propagate and calls fast-changing lights impractical ([S27](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/lighting-overview/direct-and-indirect-lighting/lightmapping/realtime-gi-using-enlighten/enlighten)); Half-Life 2's ambient cube stored six colours of bounced light per sample and blended them by the world normal ([S28](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2006/SIGGRAPH06_Course_ShadingInValvesSourceEngine.pdf)). The shipped answer for lights that change over fixed geometry is relightable transfer: The Division's radiance-transfer probes gave realtime bounce from fully dynamic light sources on consoles ([S29](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023273/Global-Illumination-in-Tom-Clancy), search summary), and Far Cry 3 relit its probe volumes on the fly for local lights such as muzzle flashes and explosions ([S30](https://gdcvault.com/play/1015326/Deferred-Radiance-Transfer-Volumes-Global)). Screen-space GI is instant but blind to anything off screen, so a pool behind the player cannot tint the ceiling ahead; among Unity's pipelines only HDRP has it ([S31](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/render-pipelines/choose-a-render-pipeline/feature-comparison)).

Light transport is linear in lamp intensity, so a room's bounce is its ambient plus the sum, over its lamps, of each lamp's intensity times a stored transfer. Kept per room in room space, that transfer survives translation, recycling and the 192 m rebase, and follows flicker, dimming and death exactly: the Division and Far Cry idea scaled down to one room (inference). FrontRooms' Trilight is a three-colour version of Valve's ambient cube, so the smallest real step up is a per-room ambient cube or L2 SH solved from the room's own albedos and live lamp powers [EST]. Unity 6000.6 offers three ways to carry it. Per-renderer SH through `LightProbeUsage.CustomProvided` works in URP, and the Run! rig already uses it, but every such renderer leaves the SRP Batcher and the GPU Resident Drawer [F35, F36]. Baked light probes can now be moved with `SetPositionsSelf`, rewritten through `bakedProbes` and re-tetrahedralized at runtime, which keeps batching but gives one SH per object ([S32](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/script-reference/unityengine/lightprobes); [S33](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/lighting-overview/direct-and-indirect-lighting/light-probes/moving)); this is new since the tech_art research, which found that probe groups cannot follow translation [F23]. The batch-friendly per-renderer channel is the renderer user value inside `UnityPerDraw`, which tech_art row L108 already proposes for lamp lenses [F23, F36]. One trap matters: the maze merges the walls of several 3 m cells into one 6 m block mesh, so a per-renderer room key cannot say which room a pixel belongs to [F23]; a per-pixel lookup of the cell from world position in a small grid texture can [EST]. Adaptive Probe Volumes still cannot follow moved modules, and their 1 m minimum spacing would leak through FrontRooms' 26 cm walls without per-room clipping [F23] [EST].

Reflections look fake when the specular term is lit by a different light field from the diffuse term. Cube-map lighting ignores occlusion, and a cube captured at one point and used everywhere is wrong ([S35](https://c0de517e.blogspot.com/2014/06/oh-envmap-lighting-how-do-we-get-you.html)). Treyarch's Black Ops II divided each environment map by its capture point's SH DC term and multiplied it by the pixel's irradiance, later taken in the reflection direction, and found the remaining errors "mostly intensity related and less often about color" ([S36](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2013-shading-course/lazarov/s2013_pbs_black_ops_2_slides_v2.pdf)); Infinite Warfare scaled each probe by the light grid's SH luminance ([S37](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/2017_DD_Rendering_of_COD_IW.pdf), search summary). Lagarde's survey adds Hoffman's divide-by-average, Battlefield 3's grey cube maps tinted by the dynamic ambient, and the seams and pops between local parallax-corrected cubes, whose correction is exact only for mirrors ([S38](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)). Unreal mixes reflection captures with the lightmap on rough surfaces and leaves very smooth ones unmixed so they still match screen-space and planar reflections ([S39](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/reflections-captures-in-unreal-engine)); HDRP normalises reflection probes from its probe volumes with an option that may only lower them, which Unity advises keeping on against leaks ([S40](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/probevolumes-options-override-reference.html)). Core 17.6 already ships that normalisation formula and the helpers for specular occlusion from AO, horizon occlusion and coloured multi-bounce AO, but URP calls none of them ([F37]; [S41](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.core/ShaderLibrary/CommonLighting.hlsl)); URP applies box projection only when a shader declares the keyword, which FrontRooms/Surface does not, and multiplies indirect diffuse and specular by one shared occlusion value [F05, F36]. FrontRooms' mismatch is therefore double (inference). In intensity, the cubes were captured with every lamp on, so reflections stay bright in a room whose lamps died: the 0.5 cap hides it only in bright rooms, and the switch to the DeadLamp cube follows only the lamp of the player's own cell [F06, F10]. In hue, a pool room whose diffuse takes the water's cyan keeps an untinted cube. Far Cry 4 met the same problem from the other side, relighting its baked cube maps because noon lighting looked too bright at 6 pm ([S92](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022234/Rendering-the-World-of-Far), second-hand via a search summary).

## Water reads real through contrast, lamps that stay live in the mirror, and shadows that remove light

No shipped title documents its water lighting the room with the water's own colour. Where a room takes a pool's light, the shipped means are baked basin colour, art-placed lights or projected caustics (inference across the cases below), which makes FrontRooms' per-pixel water term more physical than any documented practice. POOLS (Tensori, made in Unity with URP and HDRP) rests on baked lighting, light probes and probe volumes, reflection probes and SSGI on PC, with no ray tracing; its frames show a dark green body, a sharp HDR mirror, a medium ripple that never stops and a found-footage camera [F22]. Its PC VR version rendered the pool reflections in one eye only, and the reviewer's fix was to switch them off ([S42](https://www.uploadvr.com/pools-vr-review/)), which shows the look survives without its live pass. The Last of Us Part II projects static cube maps onto water with distortion, uses screen-space reflections only for characters and planar reflections only for mirrors, and its reviewers credit small bounded spaces with frequently changing cube maps for convincing reflections ([S43](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/The-Last-of-Us-Part-2-Remastered-Spiel-74482/Specials/PC-Release-Review-Steam-Test-Benchmarks-1469049/2/)). Alan Wake 2's path tracing makes lighting, reflections and shadows one solution and lets its many flickering lights respond correctly ([S44](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/alan-wake-2-dlss-3-5-full-ray-tracing-out-this-week/), vendor), while with ray tracing off its screen-space reflections drop small puddles ([S45](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/alan-wake-2-pc-performance/)). Real natatoriums are usually lit indirectly, side fixtures aimed up so the ceiling becomes the secondary reflector, partly because relamping metal halide over water meant draining the pool and scaffolding ([S46](https://recmanagement.com/articles/152591/natatorium-lighting-design)).

This line's study of 2026-11-04 ranked what a standing player reads first: two images that slide against each other (the floor through the water and the room in the mirror), a sharp mirror at grazing angles, glints far brighter than their surround, a body darker than the room, motion that never stops, the floor's lines wobbling, contact at every waterline, colour that grows with depth, light thrown by the water, and a camera that records it [F22]. Two of its consequences are decided by the room rather than the water. The water shows what is above it, so a uniformly white, evenly lit room over white tile gives a uniform pale mirror in which the water vanishes; render run s10b confirmed this for ankle water in district P's flooded offices, where both physical looks read as a clean wet floor [F22]. And the glints need lenses 10-40 times brighter than their floor, which today's drive of 1.8 does not give [F21, F22].

Shadows act on clear water by removal. Clear water takes no shadow on its surface, so a shadow shows as missing glints, missing caustics and a darker floor seen through the water, and at night underwater lamps throw the shadows of ladders and bodies up the walls and ceiling (inference from the optics). In order of payoff: lamp glints masked by the lamp's own shadow at the surface, since URP transparents can sample additional-light shadows, so nothing shines under a coping lip, a mezzanine or the Relay; caustics and the niches' thrown light multiplied by the same shadow, so a swimmer leaves a hole in the light; the bounce reduced where the water lies in shadow; and, costliest, shadowed niche lamps at night (inference). Lamps in the mirror must follow the lamps. A probe captured once shows every lamp as it was, so in FrontRooms a dead troffer keeps shining in the water on phones and WebGL, which use the probe tier only, while the desktop planar shows it dark beside a probe edge that shows it lit (inference from [F16, F17]). Every shipped answer found recomputes something: Far Cry 3 relights probes, path tracers trace. The cheap answer here is to capture probes with the lenses dark, draw each lamp's reflection from the lamp list with its live state (the water's probe tier already adds every visible lamp as a small source [F16]), normalise the probe to the live room light on the probe fallback only, never on the planar or a ray-traced mirror, as Unreal leaves smooth surfaces unmixed ([S39](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/reflections-captures-in-unreal-engine)), and apply horizon occlusion on the rippled normal ([S41](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.core/ShaderLibrary/CommonLighting.hlsl)).

Put together, when a FrontRooms lamp flickers or dies, seven things should change in the same frame: its emission and light; its glints on the water; its image in the planar mirror; its image in the probe, which stays correct if the probe is lamp-less; the probe's overall level, through normalisation; the bounce off the water onto the ceiling; and its caustics and thrown light, cut by its shadows. Each is a cheap shading term except recapturing probes, which this chain avoids (inference). That chain is the technical form of "光影水反应该是在一起相互影响的".

Wet surfaces follow Lagarde's calibrated model: porous diffuse falls toward 0.2 at full porosity and wetness, gloss rises toward water's at half strength, puddles go flat with water's F0 of 0.02, and drying is a sigmoid in which the specular goes first, convex parts dry before hollows and puddles last, about a minute being enough in a game ([S47](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)); glazed tile is not porous and does not darken. FrontRooms' `FRWetBand` already covers the waterline with soaked grout, a wandering wet band, the meniscus and the scale line [F21]; the deck, with splash patches that dry, puddles at drains and wet prints, is what is missing (inference). Every consistency tool in this section is shading only and runs on WebGL2 and phones. Only the live mirror differs by tier: desktop High and Ultra render a planar mirror at a third to a half of the viewer's pixels [F17]; a compute screen-space planar reflection could serve iPhone and iPad (under 1 ms at a 128 px target height and 1-2 ms at 256 px on a 2019 Android phone, untested on iOS, [S49](https://github.com/ColinLeung-NiloCat/UnityURP-MobileScreenSpacePlanarReflection), search summary); WebGL2 has no compute. Measured on the M3 Max at 1440p under load, the recommended water-light state costs +0.87-0.95 ms, the bounce alone about +0.3 ms, and all of a hall's water about 4.2 ms at Ultra [F21, F34]. GlassRT does not trace the water: the water is not a receiver and not in the traced scene, so a traced window shows the pool dry, and making it a receiver (P4d) is estimated at +3-5 ms at 1440p [F22]. One safety limit binds every step that brightens glints: turning at walking pace across lamp-lit beach water already gives 4.0 flashes in the worst second, above the limit of 3 [F32].

## Shafts belong where a compact source meets dark air, and lit troffers are not such a source

Since 2014 the AAA standard for volumetric light (体积光) is the froxel grid, a low-resolution 3D texture aligned with the camera frustum. Assassin's Creed IV used 160 × 90 × 64 or 160 × 90 × 128 cells with exponential depth slices over a 50-128 m range, lit by the sun, a constant ambient and only the point lights that artists marked as affecting the atmosphere, for about 1.1 ms in total; a one-sample temporal jitter with reprojection inside the volume removed almost all edge artifacts ([S50](https://bartwronski.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/bwronski_volumetric_fog_siggraph2014.pdf)). Frostbite's physically based version used 8 × 8-pixel tiles and 64 slices, 2.95 ms in total on PS4 at 900p or 0.83 ms with 16 × 16 tiles, blending 5 % of each new frame into its history, with trails behind moving lights as a known issue ([S51](https://www.slideshare.net/DICEStudio/physically-based-and-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite)). Unreal's volumetric fog costs 1 ms on PS4 at High and 3 ms on a GTX 970 at Epic, shadowed lights about three times unshadowed ones; flashlights and other fast-changing lights leave lighting trails, and the feature is unavailable on every mobile rendering path ([S52](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-fog-in-unreal-engine); [S53](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/rendering-features-for-mobile-games-in-unreal-engine)). In HDRP, reprojection doubles the fog's memory and is not compatible with dynamic lights, a slice distribution of 0.5-0.9 fixes most light flicker, and the fog's ambient has its own dimmer ([S54](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/fog-volume-override-reference.html); [S55](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.5/manual/Volumetric-Lighting.html); [S93](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.4/manual/troubleshoot-fog.html)). The cheaper per-light alternative marches each light's shadow map at reduced resolution: Lords of the Fallen spent 0.712 ms on a GTX 700-series GPU and 0.857 ms on PS4, with 8 × 8 interleaved sampling, a 7-tap bilateral blur and only vertex and pixel shaders ([S56](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/volumetric-lighting-for-many-lights-in-lords-of-the-fallen/34507223)); Killzone Shadow Fall hid its dither with random ray offsets and a bilateral blur at half resolution ([S57](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2014_Valient_Killzone_Graphics.pdf)). Screen-space radial god rays are a post-process that needs its source projected on screen ([S58](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-13-volumetric-light-scattering-post-process)).

Interior titles use fog for depth and air, and their recurring worry is compositing it with transparents and water. The Last of Us Part II moved to froxels so fog would blend with glass and particles, and went past 5 million cells where it needed sharp shafts ([S59](https://blog.siggraph.org/?p=11909)); Alan Wake 2 blends fog, transparent geometry, effects and water through one moment-based transparency system at three resolutions, with fog-affected reflections ([S60](https://www.remedygames.com/article/how-northlight-makes-alan-wake-2-shine)); the Dead Space remake used fog to convey room depth and air thickness ([S61](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/dead-space-remake-will-cut-some-elements-that-dont-work/1100-6494338/)); Half-Life: Alyx clamps its fog to authored volumes and gives volumetric lights a finite range for VR performance ([S62](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Env_volumetric_fog_controller); [S96](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Env_volumetric_fog_volume); [S63](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Light_environment_(Source_2))); frame breakdowns of RE Engine show separate volumetric-fog, volumetric-light and light-shaft stages ([S64](https://mamoniem.com/?p=2601)). The shared lesson (inference): one lit medium sampled by surfaces, particles, water and reflections, which is the volumetric half of 光影水反.

Real office air is optically almost empty. One hundred randomly chosen US office buildings averaged 7.2 µg/m³ of PM2.5 as a geometric mean ([S65](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-08/documents/base_a2_395.pdf)); with the IMPROVE extinction efficiencies ([S66](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-05/documents/appc.pdf)) that is about 3.5 × 10⁻⁵ m⁻¹, a visual range near 110 km by Koschmieder's 3.912 / σ ([S67](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visibility)) [EST]. A troffer's cone then scatters about **0.002 cd/m² toward the eye against about 95 cd/m² for a lit wall**, invisible by four orders of magnitude [EST]. A sunbeam through a dusty room adds about 50 % over a dim wall, and a 300 lm flashlight gives 0.6-1.8 cd/m² against a dark background of 0.01-0.1 cd/m², plainly visible even in clean air [EST]; dust reads as motes sparkling in a strong beam, never as uniform haze at room scale [EST]. A working pool hall is typically kept at 50-60 % relative humidity ([S68](https://iibec.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Smegal-FINAL.pdf)); its air stays clear while surfaces below the dew point, such as cold glass, sweat [EST]; visible mist is steam fog, which needs air much colder than the water and stays shallow ([S69](https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/Steam_fog)). Aerosol anisotropy runs g = 0.55-0.72 and cloud droplets about 0.85 ([S70](https://gml.noaa.gov/aftp/aerosol/doc/articles/Andrews_2006_JGR_AIOP_asymmetry.pdf); [S71](https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/Asymmetry_factor)). FrontRooms' exp² fog at density 0.014 fogs 16 % at 30 m, about 100-150 times real office air: a legitimate stale-air stylisation, and one that should be treated as a choice [EST; F06].

FrontRooms has no volumetric light today. The corridor's 60 additive beams were removed on 2026-11-04 on Red's "全部按推荐"; their replacement, `FrontRoomsDustShaft`, a ray-marched box under each lens with Henyey-Greenstein forward scattering, exists but nothing calls `SetRoomDust`; and only an analytic height fog sits over pool water [F05, F14, F15]. URP 17.6 still has no volumetric fog: a community thread of 2026-08-31 confirms the gap with no Unity reply ([S72](https://discussions.unity.com/t/urp-6-3-still-has-no-native-volumetric-fog/1735239)), and the roadmap card the project cited, `unity.com/19-volumetric-fog`, returned HTTP 404 when fetched on 2026-11-05 [F00]. The MIT-licensed CristianQiu pass supports Unity up to 6000.6 with a half-resolution fragment ray march, a depth-aware blur and no history buffer, but it does not support WebGL, where its Forward+ path froze browsers, and Metal is not among its verified APIs ([S73](https://github.com/CristianQiu/Unity-URP-Volumetric-Light); [S74](https://github.com/CristianQiu/Unity-URP-Volumetric-Light/issues/7)); the commercial Volumetric Light Beam lists every platform including WebGL ([S75](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/vfx/shaders/volumetric-light-beam-99888)).

The rules that follow are inferences from the sources above. No shafts under lit troffers, where physics cannot show them. Shafts only where a bright compact source meets dark air: the flashlight in a dead zone, metal-halide heads over a dark pool hall, the Run! emergency reds against black, a door or window slit. Haze takes the colour of the light passing through it (albedo near 1), and the fog's ambient gets its own dimmer so dark areas stay dark. Anisotropy about 0.6 for office dust and 0.7-0.8 for humid pool air, or two lobes (0.8 and −0.2) where a shaft must read from the side [EST]. No temporal history for flickering lamps, or a history dropped wherever a lamp changed this frame. Haze radiance stays below the bloom threshold (1.05 in gamma, about 1.11 linear [F13]) so it never blooms into milk. The same fog is evaluated per pixel in the Surface and water shaders, which Unreal's per-vertex fog artefacts on water warn about ([S52](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-fog-in-unreal-engine)). Without TAA, noise is hidden by a fixed blue-noise or interleaved offset per pixel, one or two depth-aware separable blurs, a nearest-depth upsample so MSAA-resolved silhouettes do not halo against bright shafts, and dithering before 8-bit output; a froxel lookup inside the forward shaders avoids both the halo and a full-screen pass on Apple's tile-based GPUs [EST].

## Windows in a generated maze are borrowed light and night mirrors, not daylight

A side window lights a strip of room about 1.5-2 times its head height deep, and with 2.7-3 m ceilings Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's daylighting guide says to plan on adequate daylight within 6.1 m of the glass ([S76](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1167562)). The same guide sets out the placement rules: strip windows give even light while punched windows make pools with dark piers between them; wide windows glare less than tall ones of the same area; glazing below desk height adds discomfort and little light; mullions and the wall facing the window should be light-coloured; a slight wash at the back of the room balances window glare; and typical window-to-wall ratios run 0.25 for punched windows, 0.35 for strip windows and 0.50 for curtain walls, with an effective aperture (visible transmission × window-to-wall ratio) near 0.30 as the target ([S76](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1167562)). Outdoors, direct sun gives 32,000-100,000 lx and an overcast day about 1,000 lx, against 320-500 lx of office lighting ([S77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lux)). For a c. 1990 office that puts the daylit strip at 3.5-5 m, so deep open-plan floors lived under the troffer grid, and any window that appears in a windowless Level 0 becomes a focal event rather than a light source [EST].

Glass changes how much light passes, its colour and the mirror it makes. PPG's tables give clear 6 mm float 89 % visible transmission with 8-9 % reflection, against 6 mm Solarbronze 53 %, Solargray 44 % and Graylite 14 %; tinted glass reflects only about 6 % because its back-surface reflection is absorbed twice ([S78](https://syracuseglass.com/E-DOCS/Architectural%20Glass/EDOCS/PPG%20Monolithic%20Glass%20Comparisons.pdf)). PPG introduced its pyrolytic Solarcool reflective coatings in 1972, and a Solarcool Graylite insulating unit passes about 5 % ([S79](https://sweets.construction.com/swts_content_files/510/P142032.htm)). A clean pane, two surfaces, reflects 8.2 % face-on, 15.7 % at 60° and 54 % at 80°, its back-surface image nearly as strong as the front one, so URP's single-surface F0 of 0.04 understates a window and the glass research recommended about 0.078 [F30]. Whether a pane reads as a window or a mirror depends on reflected against transmitted luminance: a dark room behind the glass turns it into a mirror of the player's room [F30]. Against a lit office wall of about 95 cd/m², a window on a bright day sits **+4.9 EV with clear glass, +4.2 with bronze, about +2.8 with a reflective coating and +0.8 with a Solarcool Graylite unit** [EST], so under a fixed exposure clear glass clips to white while the period's tinted and reflective glass keeps a readable view. In 1980-1993 US offices that glass was often sealed, tinted or reflective (no market-share data was found), after the mirror-glass façades that spread from Bell Labs (1957-62) through the 1970s ([S80](https://archive.pinupmagazine.org/articles/panorama-a-short-history-of-the-mirrored-glass-facade-buildings-ouida-biddle)), with blinds inside and troffers on all day under the post-1973 federal target of 50 fc at workstations ([S81](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/179853), search summary).

URP 17.6 renders none of this automatically. Area lights are baked only, cookies work as RGB textures on spot, point and directional lights, and spot cones wider than about 160° spread the shadow map until its quality drops ([S82](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/lighting-overview/lighting/light-component)); URP has no screen-space or planar reflections and only fixed exposure, and transparent objects receive shadows but do not cast them ([S31](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.6/manual/render-pipelines/choose-a-render-pipeline/feature-comparison)), so a realtime light passes a URP pane untouched and the tint and the angle loss have to be written into the light (inference). The realtime substitutes, cheapest first, are an emissive backplate (light seen, none cast); a cookie spot per window carrying mullions, blind slats and tint at an intensity of outdoor luminance × transmission; a shadowed sun patch per sunlit window; a custom rectangle light by linearly transformed cosines (Heitz et al., 2016), which cannot be shadowed accurately ([S83](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2016/s2016_ltc_rnd.pdf), search summary); and a dim unshadowed fill that washes the back wall [EST]. The view out can be interior mapping, three plane intersections per pixel, which SimCity used and Unreal ships as a material function ([S84](https://joostdevblog.blogspot.com/2018/09/interior-mapping-real-rooms-without.html)); true eye adaptation would need a custom luminance meter, for which Unreal's defaults brighten at 3 EV/s and darken at 1 EV/s ([S94](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/auto-exposure-in-unreal-engine), search summary). Shipped games split the problem. Hitman 3 keeps planar reflections for its huge vertical panes and gives shop windows ray-traced reflections, with cube-map fallbacks that can show two reflections at once on puddles ([S85](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Hitman-3-Spiel-72804/Specials/Hitman-3-Raytracing-Nvidia-Geforce-AMD-Intel-Benchmarks-Review-1396024/)); Control traces transparent reflections over the view without hiding it [F30]; Mirror's Edge Catalyst lit its city of glass with a dynamic time of day and updating radiosity GI ([S86](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/gdc-2016-see-how-the-striking-look-of-i-mirror-s-edge-catalyst-i-was-achieved)). Backrooms canon blacks office windows out or shows an outside that cannot be reached ([S87](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-4); [S88](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-684), search summary).

FrontRooms' windows are already the right kind. The map's windows are interior windows on `EdgeKind.Window` edges between maze cells, framed in walnut as "back-office light" in Level 0 and in dark-bronze pressed steel as "borrowed light" in the Office; they are still built in main, and 366 walnut windows among 411 were counted over 10 seeds on 2026-10-07 [F27, F28]. Each carries a copy of `Glass_Window` with `_RTReceive = 1`, so on Macs it is a GlassRT receiver, traced by default at quality level 3 and up on GPUs with 16 or more cores; on Windows, iPhone, iPad and WebGL it reflects the frozen zone cube [F29]. Measured, GlassRT changes 0.00-0.28 % of pixels in the maze views, intact and broken glass differed by only 2.9 luma at 1 m, and a window in view cost the main thread p50 2.76 ms and p99 8.03 ms on 2026-10-07 [F24, F27]. Light through these windows is not yet something a player can see (inference): about two thirds of maze lamps never cast shadows and light straight through walls [F23], so a window's borrowed light cannot be told from a wall's leak. It becomes visible once walls stop light, either through shadows or through tech_art's proposed light masking by cell reach (row L112), in which a lamp lights only the cells it reaches through open edges and a window edge can pass light at its transmission, 0.89 for clear float ([S78](https://syracuseglass.com/E-DOCS/Architectural%20Glass/EDOCS/PPG%20Monolithic%20Glass%20Comparisons.pdf)) or about 0.77-0.80 for wired glass ([S95](https://www.generalglass.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/GGI-Wire-Glass_Data-Sheet.pdf), search summary) [F23] [EST]. A dark room behind a window is the night-mirror case, where GlassRT and probes both look their best [F30].

**LW1, water lights its room.** The term is finished code. Its mirror part lights each pool-room surface with every overhead lamp seen in the water (Fresnel, the long ripple's net, no shadow on the mirrored path); its bounce part replaces the ambient's light from the water's directions with the light that went into the water and came back in its colour, by the exact form factor of a Lambertian rectangle [F18]. Its statics default to off, and "Recommended" sets bounce 1, mirror 1 (desktop only) and niche spread 0.5 [F19]. By the term's own arithmetic at gain 1, the bounce adds 113 % to the light of the hall ceiling over the basin and moves its red/green ratio from 0.81 to 0.49, and adds 331 % at the centre of the pool-level ceiling [F21]. Bounce 1 is the physical value, since the model's constants match the optics (§1), and 0.5 would be a softer stylisation [EST]. The step is to switch it on for the map's pool zones, recapture the pool cubes with it on (lamp-less, LW4), and retune PoolLevel's ambient, which was set by eye without it [F21]. Its flaws: the map's pool lamps are still stand-ins for Red's P1-A and P2-A picks, which fake the ceiling's bounce with a second downward spot whose colour knows nothing of the ceiling or the water (inference from [F39]; picks in [F40]); handheld SSAO runs after the opaques from depth, so the bounce's SSAO-aware occlusion exists only on desktop (inference, to confirm in a frame capture) [F02, F00]; under troffers the ceiling gets a sheen, not a caustic net (§1); and any brighter water light needs a flash count against the limit of three per second [F32].

**LW2, every room gets its own bounce colour.** Stage a costs nothing at runtime. Set each zone's three Trilight colours from that zone's own surfaces and lamps: the ground term from the floor's bounce, the equator from the walls', the sky at about 0.7-0.8 of them, all within about 1.5 : 1, and as saturated as §1's table says (Level 0 about 1 : 0.67 : 0.20, the Office near neutral) [EST]. Give the Office an ambient of its own; A/B the cool fill against a zone-coloured fill and against none; and call `FrontRoomsZoneLook.SetLampShare` from the map, so that a zone's ambient, haze and fill follow its lamps as they already do in the structures scene [F08]. As the bounce takes over the colour, the grade's warm push (white balance +9 / −7, the warm bloom tint, the lifted blacks) can move back toward a balance that keeps about 10-15 % of the lamp's cast [EST; F13]. Stage b replaces the zone constant with a per-room ambient cube or L2 SH, solved on the CPU from the room's albedos and its live lamp powers whenever a lamp changes, uploaded as one array and read by FrontRooms/Surface through a per-pixel cell key (world position into a small grid texture), which keeps the SRP Batcher and survives recycling and the rebase [EST]; pool rooms add LW1's water term on top. Its flaws: one colour set per room, so no gradient inside a room until LW9; colour steps at doorways that need a short blend; and a solver that needs each room's albedos and lamp list from the map, which 关卡设计 has to expose (inference).

**LW3, lamps become the brightest thing in the room.** Let one number drive each fixture: the lens emission follows its Light by E = π × I / A, about 5 × I for a 2 × 4 opening, and takes the Light's colour, so lens and floor change by the same factor through every flicker [EST]. For Level 0's intensity of 5 that is about 25 in linear light, or 12-15 with the view-angle factor, against about 3.6-4.1 today: lenses 3-7 times brighter [EST; F12]. The same ratio can be reached the other way, by lowering every Light and raising exposure. Either way, fix exposure per zone so floors sit in the mid-tones and lenses clip; put the bloom threshold between the brightest wall and the lens, at about 2-5 times the floor; and stop the pool grades from raising exposure by +0.25-0.55 in post where the light itself should rise [F13] [EST]. Then recapture the zone cubes so they hold white lenses (LW4). Its flaws: it overrides Red's own pick, "选择L03但是我希望灯罩亮度可以是1.8" [F33]; brighter lenses make brighter glints on rippled water, which already exceed the flash limit [F32]; a low threshold or a strong scatter lifts the whole frame into milk; and the Relay's lens must stay below the troffers' 99th percentile [F12].

**LW4, reflections become local, lamp-true and occluded.** Four parts give one result. Turn on probe blending and box projection in both URP assets and declare the two keywords in FrontRooms/Surface (tech_art row L105) [F01, F05, F23]. Recapture one cube per room family with today's surfaces and the lenses at zero emission, so that URP's direct specular from each live lamp carries that lamp's reflection. Normalise each probe sample by the room's live ambient from LW2, taken in the reflection direction and clamped so it can only lower the reflection, on probe fallbacks only and never on the planar water or GlassRT, so a dead district's reflections dim with it ([S36](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2013-shading-course/lazarov/s2013_pbs_black_ops_2_slides_v2.pdf); [S40](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/probevolumes-options-override-reference.html)) [EST]. Replace URP's single occlusion on everything with Core's specular occlusion from AO and horizon occlusion, plus coloured multi-bounce AO so that the corners of a yellow room go yellow-dark [F37] [EST]. Then raise the 0.5 cap where dead lamps are handled [F10]. Its flaws: the Run! box probes were dropped on 2026-10-31 because they "never showed a visible gain", possibly because box projection could not take effect on Surface materials at all (inference from [F23]), so a capture has to prove this one; probes seam at doors ([S38](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2012/09/29/image-based-lighting-approaches-and-parallax-corrected-cubemap/)); a capture freezes the wallpaper print [F10]; whether a probe keeps a correct box after the 192 m rebase is unverified [F23]; and until a rectangle light lands (LW7), a lamp-less cube turns lens reflections on smooth floors into round dots. The alternative is to capture the lenses lit and accept that a single dead lamp dims in the reflection rather than going out.

**LW5, shadows reach the floor, the water and the caustics.** Measured: corridor lamp shadows are invisible at eye level, maze shadows change up to 31.8 % of pixels, and SSAO is the main contact darkening at 3-10 % of pixels [F24]. So keep shadows only where an occluder matters, and make those few land: about 120° and 7 m for the corridor casters that stay, which cuts their bias from about 9.3 to 1.8 cm and the floor texel from 3.5 to 0.96 cm (row L103) [F23]; the nearest N lamps rather than a one-in-three hash in the map [F26]; the lamp's own shadow masking its glints, caustics, thrown light and bounce on the water [EST]; and, as a research spike, light masking by cell reach so unshadowed lamps stop lighting through walls (row L112) [F23]. Screen-space contact shadows for one or two key lamps are a desktop option in the medium cost class [F26]. Its flaws: a narrower cone takes the centre lamps' light off the lower walls 5.75 m away, which the side troffers have to make up [F23]; shadows already account for 33-62 % of draws [F24]; and light masking needs per-light data in URP's visible-light order, which is unverified [F23].

**LW6, light in the air only where physics shows it.** Keep the troffers clear of beams (Red's 2026-11-04 removal stands, and the physics agrees), and do not enable the existing per-lens dust shafts under lit troffers [F14, F15]. Build beams v2 with motes for the few fixtures that should show air, such as metal-halide heads over a dark hall, the Run! reds and door or window slits, at a few tenths of a millisecond on every tier; add a shadowed per-light cone march for one or two hero lights (the flashlight in a dead zone, a pool head) on desktop, iPad and iPhone, with no history so flicker stays clean [EST]. Keep the exp² density as a deliberate stylisation or thin it, which is Red's call; dim the haze with the lamps through `SetLampShare` (LW2); and keep the haze below the bloom threshold [EST]. A froxel volume applied inside the forward shaders is the long-term desktop and iPad path if fog inside water, glass and reflections becomes the goal. Its flaws: beam meshes cast no shadow into the haze; the march's cost grows with every light it serves; the CristianQiu pass is unverified on Metal and unsupported on WebGL ([S73](https://github.com/CristianQiu/Unity-URP-Volumetric-Light)); and WebGL gets the beams only.

**LW7, windows carry borrowed light and become night mirrors.** Set the window glass's F0 near 0.078 so panes reflect like two surfaces [F30]. Once LW5's reach masks exist, treat window edges as open at their transmission and tint, so light from a lit neighbour falls through the window and stops at the wall beside it [EST]. On desktop a rectangle (LTC) light would turn the round lamp highlights on glass into lens shapes, the open "A2" problem of the window landing [F27] [EST]. Keep dark rooms behind some windows, because that is where the night mirror and GlassRT pay off. If exterior windows ever appear, use period glass (bronze 0.53, gray 0.44, reflective about 0.05-0.25, [S78](https://syracuseglass.com/E-DOCS/Architectural%20Glass/EDOCS/PPG%20Monolithic%20Glass%20Comparisons.pdf)) behind the mini-blind kit that is in main but not placed [F27], one cookie lamp per window at outdoor luminance × transmission, and a backplate or interior mapping for the view [EST]. Its flaws: light masking is still a research spike; cookie lamps spend the 32-light budget on handhelds and WebGL [F23]; and LTC lights cannot be shadowed accurately ([S83](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2016/s2016_ltc_rnd.pdf), search summary).

**LW8, the pool deck gets wet.** Use Lagarde's 3b numbers: diffuse toward × 0.2 on porous grout and the abrasive deck, gloss boosted at half strength, glazed faces untouched, puddles flat at F0 0.02, and drying as a sigmoid over about a minute with the gloss going first ([S47](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)). Add a splash-wet zone about 0.5-1.5 m from the edge where the player or the Relay disturbs the water, and puddles at drains and wall feet whose reflection is the probe plus live lamp glints plus LW4's normalisation, since a deck puddle sits at a different height from the water's planar mirror [EST]. Its flaw: no shipped interior-pool wet-deck system is documented to copy.

**LW9, gradients of bounce inside a room.** If per-room colour is not enough, the next step is per-room relightable transfer: a few probes per room storing each lamp's unit bounce as L1 SH in a room-clipped 3D texture, about 0.8 KB per room, relit every frame by the live lamp powers, which gives gradients (cyan strongest over the water, yellow deepest in corners) and follows flicker exactly [EST]. Surface Cache GI deserves a desktop A/B only after a Unity 6.7 upgrade, given its compute fallback on Metal, its slower reaction than flicker, its diffuse-only output and no WebGL path ([S24](https://discussions.unity.com/t/surface-cache-gi-preview/1720494)). Commercial SSGI and a custom DDGI on Metal ray tracing cost milliseconds and either miss off-screen sources or need large engineering [EST]. Its flaw: every option here is desktop-first or adds authoring per room family.

## Conclusion

FrontRooms' colour problem is structural, not a matter of tuning. Each room's colour is decided today by a per-zone constant, a frozen cube from another room and a grade that tints lit and shadowed pixels alike, while physics and shipped practice decide it from what the room contains: its albedos, its live lamps, its water. Once one room-level light state (live lamp power times room albedos, plus the water term) drives the diffuse ambient, the reflection normalisation, the haze and the water bounce together, a lamp that dies dims its floor, its bounce, its reflections, its glints and its fog in the same frame, and cyan water tints the ceiling above it. That is what "光影水反应该是在一起相互影响的" asks for, stated as engineering.

Two further conclusions follow. Because FrontRooms cannot bake, the methods that fit it are cheap data and shading terms that stay exact under flicker, and these are also the ones that run on iPhone, iPad and WebGL2; ray tracing, froxels and dynamic GI are desktop garnish rather than the foundation. And physics argues for much brighter lenses than the chosen 1.8, and against any return of shafts under lit troffers, including the per-lens dust shafts built to replace the removed beams. What remains unknown can be measured: no Level 0 or Office luminance ratio has been captured, no device has timed any of these terms, and whether probes survive the rebase is untested. The first capture should settle the first of these before any step lands.

## 给 Red 的优化方案

**背景。** 你在 11-04 说："光影水反应该是在一起相互影响的"。你还问："如果水是青色，这个颜色是否会影响房间？" 11-05 你让我调研 3A 游戏和建筑照明怎么做体积光、水、水面反射、室内灯光、灯具、窗户和玻璃。这份报告用了六份调研笔记，还只读不改地查了一遍 main。没有跑 Unity，没有下载任何东西，也没有在 iPhone、iPad 或网页上测过时间。所以手机、iPad 和 WebGL2 的耗时都是估算。

**一句话回答：会，而且应该很明显。** 灯朝下照，天花板只能靠下面反上来的光。下面是 1.2 米深的白瓷砖池水时，天花板中间应该是青色：俱乐部泳池大厅约 0.55 : 0.87 : 1（红 : 绿 : 蓝），淹水的办公室约 0.39 : 0.83 : 1。脚踝深的水只让它微微发青，约 0.9 : 0.99 : 1。黄色墙纸的 Level 0 也是一样：天花板、角落和背光面应该比墙纸本身还黄，正对灯的白色表面几乎保持灯的颜色。

**为什么现在颜色不对。** 现在每个房间的颜色来自三个互不相通的地方。第一，环境光每个区一套。Level 0、Office、Run! 房间和标题走廊共用同一套暖色，灯死了也不变。第二，反射是每区一张冻结的立方体贴图。Level 0 那张是 10-03 在一个格子里拍的，亮度压到一半，也不跟房间对齐。第三，调色把暖色均匀地盖在亮处和暗处。物理正好相反：灯照到的地方保持灯色，饱和的颜色在反弹光里。让水给房间染色的那一项，代码 11-04 就写好了，但在地图的泳池区是关着的。还有一点：泳池测试里量到的灯罩只比地面亮约 1.25 倍（Level 0 还没量过），真实灯罩比地毯亮 35 到 90 倍。所以反射里没有白色的灯，只剩一片暗黄。

**方案（按"每毫秒换来多少画面"排序）。** 每一步上线前，都先在总控借的克隆里拍一组对比图给你看（不新建克隆）。

**LW1 让水照亮房间（地图里的泳池区）**
- 玩家看到：深水上方的天花板和墙的上半部变青，正上方最浓，往墙边渐淡。天花板上有很淡的灯光波动。脚踝深的水的房间几乎不变。
- 代价：桌面实测 +0.87 到 0.95 毫秒（1440p，M3 Max，有其他负载）。iPhone 只有反弹这一项，估算 1.5 到 2.5 毫秒。iPad 估算 0.7 到 1.1 毫秒。WebGL2 只有反弹这一项，没测过。
- 谁做：游戏视觉（这一项本身、泳池反射图、PoolLevel 的环境光）。关卡设计（在地图里打开）。灯具适配（P1-A、P2-A 泳池灯）。
- 建议：现在就做，用代码里的"Recommended"档：反弹 1，桌面再加镜像 1。上线前先数一遍闪光。
- 缺点：地图里的泳池灯还是替身，天花板的反光靠另一盏朝下的灯假装，它的颜色和天花板、水都无关（推测）。反弹光的 SSAO 遮蔽推测只有桌面有，要用截帧确认。水面闪光现在已经是每秒 4.0 次，超过 3 次的上限。

**LW2 每个房间有自己的反弹光颜色**
- 玩家看到：Level 0 的天花板、角落和背光面更黄，灯下的白色保持灯色。Office 变得几乎中性。Run! 红灯条旁边一两米的地面带红。灯死掉的区域变暗、变灰。
- 代价：第一步（按区重设三色环境光、给 Office 自己的环境光、地图调用 SetLampShare）四个平台都是 0。第二步（每个房间按自己的材质和亮着的灯算一套环境光）GPU 几乎是 0，每次灯变化 CPU 不到 0.05 毫秒（估算），四个平台一样。
- 谁做：游戏视觉（数值、计算、Surface 着色器）。关卡设计（在地图里调用，提供房间和格子数据）。
- 建议：第一步现在做，第二步接着做。
- 缺点：一个房间只有一套颜色，房间内部没有渐变（等 LW9）。两个房间颜色不同时，门口要做一点过渡。

**LW3 让灯成为房间里最亮的东西**
- 玩家看到：灯罩像真正的光源，带一点光晕。光滑地面、瓷砖、CRT 玻璃和水里出现白色的灯影。地面和墙的亮度不变。
- 代价：四个平台都是 0，只改材质和后期数值。编辑器里重拍反射图。
- 谁做：游戏视觉（灯具套件、后期）。灯具适配（各区的灯）。
- 建议：先做对比图，再由你决定（见下面第 1 个决定）。
- 缺点：会改掉你选的 1.8。灯更亮，水面闪光也更强，闪光次数会更高。bloom 阈值设低了，整个画面会发白。

**LW4 反射变成"这个房间的、跟着灯变的、有遮挡的"**
- 玩家看到：光滑地面和室内窗里映出这个房间自己的灯排，和天花板对齐。灯死了，反射也跟着变暗。桌子底下不再发光。泳池瓷砖的反射带上青色。
- 代价：每个像素多一次盒子计算、一两次贴图采样和一次归一化计算（估算很小）。每张 128 像素的 HDR 贴图约 1 MB。四个平台都能做。WebGL 受 16 个采样器、每个相机最多 32 个灯加探针的限制。
- 谁做：设置菜单与画质预设（URP 资产里的两个开关）。游戏视觉（着色器、拍摄工具、归一化、遮蔽）。关卡设计（迷宫里的探针）。夸张结构调研与设计（结构场景的探针）。
- 建议：在 LW3 之后做，因为重拍要先有亮的灯罩。
- 缺点：10-31 Run! 的盒子探针因为"看不出变化"被撤掉了，可能是因为当时 Surface 着色器根本用不上盒子投影（推测），所以这次必须先用对比图证明。门口会有接缝。墙纸图案在反射里是冻结的。192 米坐标重置以后探针盒子还对不对，没有验证过。在矩形光（LW7）做好之前，不带灯拍的反射里，灯影会变成圆点。

**LW5 影子落到地面、水面和焦散上**
- 玩家看到：桌子底下、门扇下面有贴地的接触阴影。泳池边沿下面、夹层下面、Relay 下面不再有闪光。人在水里会在焦散上留下一个洞。
- 代价：按实测绘制数推算，现在每盏有影子的走廊灯约 31-34 次绘制，迷宫灯约 213-264 次。把保留影子的灯收窄到 120°，它在地面的覆盖面积从约 1,296 平方米降到约 97 平方米（估算）。水面遮挡每个像素每盏灯多查一次影子（估算很小）。手机和 WebGL 只亮最近的 22 盏灯，影子灯本来就少。
- 谁做：游戏视觉（走廊灯、水面遮罩）。关卡设计（地图里哪些灯投影、灯能照到哪些格子）。灯具适配（各区灯的影子策略）。
- 建议：桌面先做。只在真正需要影子的地方收窄灯锥。
- 缺点：灯锥变窄后，中间的灯照不到 5.75 米外墙的下半部，要靠两侧的灯补。影子已经占 33-62% 的绘制。"灯不穿墙"那一项还在研究阶段。

**LW6 体积光只出现在物理上看得见的地方**
- 玩家看到：亮着的格栅灯下面没有光柱。黑区里的手电筒、暗泳池大厅上方的金卤灯、Run! 的红灯、门缝和窗缝会有光柱和飘浮的灰尘。只有剧情需要的泳池才有贴着水面的薄雾。
- 代价（估算）：新的光束网格四个平台都是 0.1-0.4 毫秒。给一两盏主角灯做逐灯光线步进：桌面 0.2-0.6 毫秒，iPad 0.3-0.8 毫秒，iPhone 0.3-0.8 毫秒。WebGL 只有光束。
- 谁做：游戏视觉（光束 v2、逐灯步进）。灯具适配（哪些灯有光柱）。关卡设计（摆放）。设置菜单与画质预设（按画质分档）。
- 建议：光束 v2 全平台做。逐灯步进只给一两盏主角灯。
- 缺点：光束网格不能把影子投进雾里。逐灯步进每多一盏灯就多一份耗时。开源方案在 Metal 上没验证过，WebGL 不支持。

**LW7 窗户传递邻屋的光，晚上变成镜子**
- 玩家看到：隔壁亮着的房间的光从室内窗透过来，照在窗边，墙挡住的地方没有光。窗后的房间是黑的时候，玻璃变成一面镜子。桌面版玻璃上的灯影变成长方形。
- 代价：玻璃的 F0 改到约 0.078，耗时是 0。每个窗户灯是一个 Forward+ 灯，在手机和 WebGL 上占 32 盏灯的名额。矩形光要改所有材质的着色器，耗时没测。GlassRT 照旧只在 Mac 上，追踪的地方 +3.2-5.0 毫秒。
- 谁做：关卡设计（把窗户边加进"灯能照到哪里"的计算）。游戏视觉（玻璃、矩形光、GlassRT）。
- 建议：玻璃 F0 现在就改。窗户透光等 LW5 的计算做好再做。
- 缺点：那个计算还在研究阶段。窗户灯会吃掉手机和网页的灯数。矩形光的影子做不准。

**LW8 泳池边的地面变湿**
- 玩家看到：水边被溅到的瓷砖缝变暗，大约一分钟后变干，先失去光泽。排水口旁边的小水洼映着灯。
- 代价：只是着色，很小（估算）。
- 谁做：游戏视觉（Surface）。夸张结构调研与设计（泳池区域）。
- 建议：在 LW1 之后做。
- 缺点：没有可以直接照抄的室内泳池游戏做法。

**LW9 房间内部的反弹光渐变（桌面加分项）**
- 玩家看到：同一个房间里，水的正上方最青，角落最黄。
- 代价（估算）：每个房间一个光传输体积，桌面 +0.1-0.2 毫秒，iPhone 和 iPad +0.2-0.4 毫秒，WebGL +0.3-0.6 毫秒。商业 SSGI 桌面约 1-3 毫秒。自己做 DDGI 约 2-4 毫秒，工作量很大。
- 谁做：游戏视觉。光影水反负责对比测试。
- 建议：先做每房间的传输体积。Unity 自带的 Surface Cache GI 等升级到 6.7 以后再试。
- 缺点：这些都是桌面优先，或者每一类房间都要多做数据。

**你的决定（2026-11-06）：「全部按推荐」。** 下面五件事都按推荐执行，方案按上面的顺序做；灯罩亮度（LW3）还是先给你看对比，再落地。

**需要你决定的事**

1. **灯罩亮度（LW3）。** 背景：你 10-31 选了 L03，灯罩 1.8（"选择L03但是我希望灯罩亮度可以是1.8"）。泳池测试里灯罩只比地面亮约 1.25 倍（Level 0 还没量过），真实的灯罩比地毯亮 35-90 倍。它决定光滑地面和水里有没有白色灯影，也决定灯看起来像不像光源。选项：(a) 保持 1.8，画面和现在一样，反射还是一片暗黄；(b) 只把灯罩调亮 3-7 倍，灯罩像真的光源，水面会闪，地面和墙不变；(c) 灯罩亮度跟灯绑在一起，再每个区固定曝光，最接近真实，灯闪、变暗、熄灭时灯罩和地面一起变。代价：GPU 都是 0；风险是闪光次数和画面发白。建议：(c)，先在克隆里做对比图给你看。
2. **水的染色强度（LW1）。** 背景：代码里"反弹 1"就是物理值。选项：(a) 1，深水上方的天花板明显变青；(b) 0.5，更柔和的风格化；(c) 继续关着，水的颜色停在池边。代价：桌面约 0.9 毫秒，手机估算 1.5-2.5 毫秒。建议：(a)。
3. **环境光的颜色方向（LW2）。** 背景：物理上，黄色房间的天花板和角落应该比墙纸还黄，灯下的白色应该接近灯色。现在是调色把整个画面均匀地染黄。选项：(a) 保持现在，整个画面同一种黄绿色；(b) 按物理来，亮处干净，暗处和天花板更饱和；(c) 折中，像眼睛适应后那样，保留 10-15% 的灯色。代价：第一步是 0。建议：(b) 加上 (c) 的白平衡，用"白卡放在阴影里和放在灯下"来检查。
4. **雾的浓度（LW6）。** 背景：现在的雾在 30 米处遮掉 16%，大约是真实办公室空气的 100-150 倍。这是"空气不新鲜"的风格。选项：(a) 保留；(b) 变淡，远处更清楚；(c) 灯死的区域雾也跟着变暗。建议：(a) 加 (c)。
5. **以后要不要有外窗（LW7，可以晚点定）。** 背景：现在地图里的窗都是房间之间的"借光"室内窗。Backrooms 粉丝设定里，办公室外窗有的被涂黑，有的看得见外面但出不去。选项：(a) 不要外窗；(b) 涂黑的外窗；(c) 能看到外面但出不去，用当年的茶色或反光玻璃加百叶窗。建议：先 (a)；要外窗的话选 (c) 加茶色玻璃，这样不用模拟日光，也不会过曝。
