## 1. The idea

**The building is on the air.** In 1990 every office has a TV, and the only channel is WXRM-TV 61: station IDs, the late movie, sign-off, bars and a clock until morning. When the alarm goes, the building takes over its own sets.

The campaign uses the same two voices the game does:
- **the station:** a dull, period-true local UHF broadcast day;
- **the building:** fire safety, EGRESS plans, building management.

The horror is the gap between them.

**How this differs from A24's *Backrooms* campaign** (Apr–May 2026, the same IP space). A24's issuers were a furniture retailer (a 1990 local furniture TV spot, fax-back flyers, a 1991 BBS) and the Async institute. Ours are a TV station and a building's fire-safety system. So the rules are:
- no fax, BBS or store-flyer endpoints;
- the furniture spots stay inside the game (Option A, §8);
- every paper or phone endpoint speaks for the building.

**For a new IP, the unbranded trick has limits.** Starcourt Mall (Stranger Things, 2018) and Fallout 76's "Please Stand By" stream worked because millions already knew the brand. An unbranded WXRM clip from a small account will read as a stand-alone retro parody. So every public piece is either:
- **in-world with a tie:** a caption or a 1-second end slate naming FRONTROOMS; or
- **out-of-world:** the Market register (Bayon + Source Serif) with the logo.

## 4. The chain (one handoff per piece)

1. **Trail:** B1 station breaks and B2 shorts, each with a caption tie, for 2–3 weeks before release.
2. **Showcase / venue:** P1 new-hire packets and P5 poster pairs at ArtCenter. The packet's panel card carries the facilities line (B6) and a code. P5's conditions (印刷与演示):
   - the pairs are framed or panel-mounted exhibition pieces inside the showcase room;
   - nothing goes on doors, corridors or stairwells, or next to real exit signs, fire equipment or evacuation routes;
   - no arrow points toward real egress;
   - each piece carries the FrontRooms title as its visible tell;
   - the showcase organiser gives permission, plus facilities if anything is wall-mounted.
3. **Phone (optional):** B6's voicemail gives a fire-safety message and confirms the code. In the game, the code works on a building fire-alarm panel. **This needs a game feature,** so it's for 系统设计 and Red (the Maniac Mansion model).
4. **Reveal:** B3 at the release time, then B4 and B5.
5. **After launch:** P3 plan fragments as collectables, perhaps one per playtest or showcase visit, assembling one floor plan.

**Design rules for the chain** (from the Portal 2010 ARG designer, `args_channels` NOTES):
- every step can be reached from scratch in about 30 minutes;
- answers are obviously right;
- a canon list is published;
- there is redundancy;
- every audio-only clue has a visual twin.

Crowds solve fast: P.T. was meant to take a week and fell in about 4 hours, and Portal's main puzzle in 7 h 16 min. So pace with scheduled air times, not physical hunts.

## 6. Hard rules (merged with 印刷与演示's §5)

1. **No emergency-alert audio or wording.**
   - Never 853/960 Hz, 1050 Hz, data bursts or the WEA cadence.
   - Never "test", "not a test", "we interrupt", agency names or seals.
   - The FCC fines simulated tones even in obvious fiction: the Olympus Has Fallen trailer, $1.93M across networks.
   - A plain building bell or horn is fine.
2. **The alarm slate appears only inside a TV in game footage,** under the title and fiction mark. Never full-screen, never the first frame, never a standalone loop.
3. **No real stations, brands or people.**
   - In-world numbers come only from 555-0100–0199.
   - No 800 or 900 numbers and no URLs on 1990 screens; the web went public in 1991–93.
   - WXRM's call sign is held by a real low-power FM station (§8.2).
4. **No fax, BBS or retail endpoints** (A24). Phone and paper speak for the building.
5. **IP distance:**
   - no Async, "Complex" or "Threshold";
   - no camcorder or date-stamp key art;
   - no "Level 0" or noclip;
   - nothing in Local 58's grammar (seals, the moon);
   - not Kane Pixels' episode 25 furniture store;
   - no wiki text.
6. **No mock exit or evacuation signs in real buildings** except in a venue or with the operator. No unattended or beeping objects.
7. **Paper identity: real grammar, fictional identity, visible tell** (印刷与演示 C §7.1).
   - No real agency name, seal, form number, CFR/FPMR line or GPO imprint on any paper piece.
   - Each piece has a fictional issuer, a fictional form number and one visible tell.
   - Missing-person or appeal flyers, if they ever come up, stay in the game or in a ticketed venue, marked "fiction".
8. **Platform rules:**
   - videos set "not made for kids";
   - no flashing over 3 per second;
   - pause during real emergencies and national alert tests;
   - proof every in-world line.
