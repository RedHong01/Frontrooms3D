## 1. The short answer: ten kinds of material

| Material | Strongest examples | What to copy |
|---|---|---|
| **Typed forms and memos from an in-world issuer** | Cloverfield's Tagruato TOP SECRET memo; Severance's quick-start guide and checklists; BioShock 2's TO/FROM memo; Infocom *Deadline* (1982) | One issuer, a real form genre, a dated header, and one wrong field |
| **Handwriting on typed paper** | Cloverfield's memo with a whistle-blower note on the back; the FNAF *Survival Logbook*, "already used" by a past employee; the F.E.A.R. 2 field guide annotated by an executive | Handwriting is the second voice, on the back or in the margin, and it varies from copy to copy |
| **New-hire packets: badge, handbook, card** | Severance's SDCC orientation (handbook, access card, meal token, bingo sheet, team photo); Yuppie Psycho's employee ID plus safety poster; *The Lurking Horror* (1987) freshman guide with an ID | Cast the visitor as staff |
| **Safety, warning and wayfinding signs** | The Exit 8's title set as a yellow exit sign, with anomaly-poster rallies run with six railway operators; Control's Bureau posters at 1:1; Maniac Mansion's "Nuke'm Alarms" owner's guide (1987) | The signage system is the brand, and only with the operator in a real venue |
| **Mail loops** | BioShock 2 (2009–10): P.O. box, letters, telegrams by courier, then bottles on ten beaches at sunrise; RE7: a VHS addressed to each outlet, plus a real VCR | Escalate through period document genres, one addressed object at a time |
| **VHS, tape and analog objects** | RE7 VHS; I Saw the TV Glow's numbered VHS runs; the Stanley Parable CopyConductor training cassette; Devotion's mock-VHS edition | Small numbered runs; the label carries one handoff |
| **Tickets, cards and keys** | Sleep No More's colour-coded card ticket; Manila's cinema ticket that doubles as a pass; FNAF *Ticket to Fun* (4 × 9.75 in, UV); the 近畿地方 talisman card with a 2D code | The card is the key: colour-code it and print one handoff on it |
| **Maps and floor plans** | Crow Country (1990!): a park map with a flier on the back; the A24 *Backrooms* zine with random map segments; Paranormasight's ward walking map | Fragments to collect; one folded sheet with two sides |
| **Glow and UV finishes** | The FNAF *Into the Pit* glow poster; BioShock 2 black-light posters; Out of Sight's glow sticker sheet; Elvira's red acetate lens (1990) | Ready-made finishes for the EGRESS phosphor ink |
| **Walls of paper, as exhibitions** | Japan's その怪文書を読みましたか, 行方不明展 and 近畿地方 shows: archive zones, printed disclaimers | Archive classification as the grid; "fiction, no need to search" |

## 2. The cases that fit FrontRooms best (merged ranking)

1. **A24 *Backrooms* (2026, B ADP-01; A §4.3).** The same IP has already gone to paper:
   - a Pluto TV furniture-store ad gave a fax number, and faxing it printed **flyers dated 17 and 18 May 1990**;
   - a voice line led to a BBS "dead since 1991";
   - there was also an Async ID-card generator, a missing poster, Level-0 wallpaper, a zine with map segments, and blue-tape hazmat walls.

   **This is the bar, and the thing not to copy** (see §4).
2. **Severance / Lumon (B OFF-01).** The best "office as issuer" model: the visitor is onboarded. Its faces are modern, so it fails FrontRooms' era lock.
3. **Yuppie Psycho Elite Edition (2022, A §6.8a).** A 1990s office-horror box: the employer's ID card plus a folded "Safty Information" poster. That is FrontRooms' badge plus EGRESS plan.
4. **The Exit 8, game and film (A §4.1–4.2, B ADP-02/03).**
   - Exit choice as print: the corridor's posters were sold as the bonus.
   - Spot-the-difference poster pairs ran in stations, with a QR code plus a keyword.
   - Exit-sign graphics appeared on everyday goods.
   - Fake exit signs hid a password, made with the operators.
5. **Maniac Mansion and The Lurking Horror (1987, A §7).** Inside the era lock.
   - Maniac Mansion: an alarm-system owner's guide whose codes are typed into the game. It is the template for a **Relay fire-alarm panel card**.
   - The Lurking Horror: an institutional handbook with fill-in ID and emergency pages.
6. **Cloverfield's Tagruato memo (B DOC-03).** The closest film match to Arthur's slip: typed memo, handwritten reverse, shipped inside merch orders packed in newsprint.
7. **BioShock 2, "There's Something in the Sea" (A §2.1).** The complete escalating mail loop through period document types.
8. **Crow Country (2024, A §4.4).** Set in 1990. The cheapest era-true insert: one folded 9 × 12 in sheet, a map on one side and a flier on the other.
9. **Stranger Things, Hawkins Power & Light (B OFF-02).** A dull in-world service company buys ordinary ad space, and its phone menu leaks the horror. That is the register of the Relay's fire-alarm contractor.
10. **Japanese document-horror exhibitions (B JP-01/02/05).** All paper, with archive classification, and disclaimers done properly.

## 3. Patterns that recur

- **One handoff per piece, then chain them.** Examples:
  - date → URL (Cloverfield);
  - glyph ad → site, billboard → hotline (Longlegs);
  - QR + keyword → the next station (Exit 8);
  - fax → printed flyer (A24).
- **Cast the visitor as staff.** New-hire packets, employee directories, night-shift briefings.
- **Typed form plus handwriting** is a proven device in both games and film. It is exactly the grammar of Arthur's slip and of FrontRooms' Print / Form system.
- **Merch made from the signage system:** exit-sign toilet paper, stickers cut from passage signs, wallpaper sold by the roll.
- **Era-true feelies already existed in 1987–90:** case folders, owner's guides, campus guides, code wheels, an acetate lens. FrontRooms can quote these honestly; it doesn't have to fake them with digital effects.

## 4. The same-IP benchmark and how FrontRooms differs

A24's campaign owns **retail and Async**: the furniture-store ad, fax-back flyers, the BBS, ID badges, blue tape. Advertising's rule, `AD_TYPE_SYSTEM.md` §5, now says any paper or phone endpoint speaks for **the building**, never for the store. On the print side, FrontRooms answers with:

- **Issuer:** building management, fire safety and the EGRESS drawing set. The Relay is the fire-alarm responder.
- **Signs:** placards and plans (the EGRESS placard), not hazmat tape.
- **Paper:** canary carbon copies, transmittal slips, owner's guides. Black ink only, the brand yellow as the paper (`PRINT_FORM_SYSTEM.md`).
- **Year:** everything dated 1990 or earlier, using real period form grammar (SF 63 rev. 8-81, ANSI Z35.1 headers, AIGA/DOT symbols). No 1991 orange WARNING, no ISO 7010.

## 5. Risk rules

These merge A §10, B's RISK section and C §7.

1. **No real issuer identity, ever.** No real agency name, seal, form number, CFR line or GPO imprint. **Real grammar, fictional identity, visible tell** (C §7.1).
2. **No mock exit, evacuation or guide-light signs in real buildings,** except inside a venue or with the operator.
   - US OSHA requires "Not an Exit" marking on passages that could be mistaken for exits.
   - Japanese fire law bans ads that can be confused with guide lights.
3. **No unattended electronics, locked or beeping objects, or anonymous parcels.** The Boston Mooninite signs cost $2M; Watch Dogs' safe brought a bomb squad.
4. **No EAS or WEA alert tones in anything broadcastable.** The FCC has fined up to $1.12M per company. A plain fire bell is explicitly allowed, so the Relay's sound is safe.
5. **Phone numbers inside the fiction come only from 555-0100 to 0199.**
   - Never 911.
   - Never a real number someone else owns.
   - A number the team controls is fine, provided it opens with a fiction disclosure (B RISK-18; Advertising's optional facilities line B6).
6. **Missing-person and appeal flyers stay in game or in a ticketed venue,** and say "fiction".
7. **Proof every line of in-world print.** One unchecked joke cost *Devotion* its publishers.
8. **Don't reuse wiki text.** The Backrooms wikis are CC BY-SA. Arthur's Manila Room label is copied from Fandom word for word.

## 6. Candidate print pieces for FrontRooms

These are ideas for Advertising's campaign plan; nothing is decided. Each uses the Print / Form system: canary paper, black ink, the typewriter grid.

1. **A new-hire packet from building management.** It holds:
   - an employee ID;
   - an EGRESS evacuation plan (the placard, folded);
   - a Relay fire-alarm panel quick-reference card, with codes that work in the game (the Maniac Mansion model);
   - a transmittal slip routing it all to "you".
2. **A transmittal-slip mailer chain.** Each slip carries one handoff, with a handwritten note on the reverse (the Cloverfield model).
3. **EGRESS plan fragments.** Collectable map segments that assemble into one floor plan. This differs from A24's random zine segments because ours are an architect's drawing set.
4. **A glow-ink EGRESS poster.** The phosphor narrative as a real print finish.
5. **Exit-choice poster pairs.** Spot the difference between two placards. **Only inside a venue or with an operator partner** (rule 2).
