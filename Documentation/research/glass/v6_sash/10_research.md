# V6 sash look: research (游戏视觉, 2026-10-31)

C12 (the quiet sash pry), Relay v2 step V6. Brief research before the build, real sources only.

1. **The latch is a cam sash lock on the meeting (check) rails.** The latch body is screwed to the top of the lower sash and the keeper to the upper sash; turning the lever swings an offset cam into the keeper and draws the rails together. This is the same two-part lock on period double-hung and single-hung sashes, whether wood, steel or aluminium (US 4,223,930, filed 1979, published 1980: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4223930 ; US 4,736,972 "Check rail lock", 1988: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4736972).
2. **What a pry does.** Force on the sash is resisted only where the lock is screwed to the rails. A blade or bar between the rails either slips the cam out of the keeper, or the sash is shoved until the keeper's short wood screws pull out of the rail. The glass stays whole. Period guards and pins exist because of this (US 4,235,465, 1980: https://patents.google.com/patent/US4235465 ; Seattle Police, window security: https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/Police/Prevention/Home_Security_Window_Security.pdf).
3. **Window types, 1985-1993.** Commercial operable windows were double-hung, single-hung (fixed upper sash) or horizontal sliders. A slider's latch sits on the vertical meeting stile, so the map's latch box (horizontal, centred at 1.175 m) means a hung sash. A double-hung opens at most half its height (each sash travels only the other's height). A **pocket window** is the type whose sashes run up into an overhead pocket in the wall and leave the opening completely (Lunken, US 1,326,677, 1919: https://patents.google.com/patent/US1326677A/en ; "Disappearing window", US 780,902, 1905: https://patents.google.com/patent/US780902A/en).
4. **The design call (built).** The map's windows become box-head pocket double-hungs, but only while sashPry is on. The game needs the whole 1.4 x 1.65 m opening clear: the player's climb carries the eye at 1.42 m through the wall line, and the 2.86 m Relay steps through in 0.6 s. Half an opening would put the camera through the upper sash. So the pry's result is: the keeper tears off the hall sash's rail and falls onto the sill, and both sashes run up into the head pocket, carrying the glass and its cracks (stage 1-2 kept, hidden up there). The latch is a die-cast cam lock: brass on the walnut W-L0 frame, satin brass on the steel W-OF frame.

No third-party media was downloaded. The URLs are the sources (patents: public domain; Seattle PD: public guidance).
